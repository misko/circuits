# 0. Posture and method

Every gate on this release passes, so re-running them proves nothing. This lens
went looking for what the gates cannot see, and deliberately re-derived the
high-consequence facts from PRIMARY sources rather than from the record (canon
M1 — checker and checked must not share a method):

* polarity and rotation were derived from **JLC's own cached models**
  (`projects/usb-hub-3s-v3/06_build/twin_v19/easyeda/<LCSC>/jlc.pretty/*.kicad_mod`)
  and the **KiCad system library**, read as raw geometry — not from
  `jlc_lcsc_rotations.csv`, which is what the exporter already used;
* pin maps were re-read from the datasheet **figures** in `02_parts/*/`;
* copper was measured with `pcbnew` on a copy of the sealed board;
* the E-OFF budget was re-enumerated from the shipped netlist, pad by pad.

`kicad-cli pcb drc` was NOT run — it writes a `.kicad_prl` into the directory
holding the board it reads, and nothing in this review needs it.

# P1-1 — The "OPEN, UNRECONCILED" 3× mesh disagreement is not a solver disagreement. The two solves were run on DIFFERENT COPPER, and this repo's own CHANGELOG says so.

**Where it lives:** `03_src/rules/power_tree.yaml` lines ~366-383 ("TWO MESH
SOLVES DISAGREE 3x ON THE IDENTICAL COPPER, and neither party could reconcile
it"), repeated in `01_docs/DETAIL_DESIGN.md` sec.4 "OPEN, UNRECONCILED (v1.9)".

**The premise is false.** RL-2 ran on the **v1.5** board; the v1.9 fix lens ran on
the **v1.9** board. Between them the delivery path was re-stitched. Measured with
`pcbnew` on the sealed release sources:

| net | v1.5 vias | v1.9 / v1.10 / v1.11 vias | RL-2 (v1.5) | fix lens (v1.9) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PMID** (Q6.S → F2.1) | **2** | **12** | 4.914 mΩ | 0.482 mΩ | **10.2×** |
| **VBUSC** (F2.2 → J5) | **5** | **15** | 2.209 mΩ | 0.815 mΩ | **2.7×** |
| **5VC** (L2.2 → Q6) | 4 | 4 | 2.198 mΩ | 1.712 mΩ | 1.28× |

**The one segment whose stitching did not change is the one segment where the two
solvers agree.** Zone areas are unchanged to within 3 % on all three nets
(PMID F.Cu 44.97 / B.Cu 52.48 mm² in both releases), so the *only* thing that
moved is the layer coupling.

**Why 10× on PMID specifically, and why RL-2's number was right for its board.**
The PMID F.Cu fill is **two disjoint filled outlines** — 18.07 mm² spanning
x[105.50,108.95] and 26.90 mm² spanning x[107.52,113.00]. Q6's source pads sit at
x = 106.67 (first island); F2 pad 1 sits at x = 110.61 (second island). **All PMID
current must therefore cross to B.Cu and back.** With 2 vias that is ≥ 2 × ~1.2 mΩ
of via resistance in series before any spreading — which is 4.914 mΩ. With 12 vias
(4-5 per island) it is ~0.5 mΩ — which is 0.482 mΩ.

**First-principles bound, independent of both solvers.** 1 oz outer copper is
0.494 mΩ/sq at 20 °C. The B.Cu PMID fill is a near-solid 7.5 × 7.0 mm slab; the
conduction length between the two pad edges is 3.9 mm across a 7.0 mm height =
0.56 squares = **0.28 mΩ on one layer alone**. There is no geometry here that
yields 4.9 mΩ *without* a via bottleneck. Even a hypothetical single 0.8 mm
(VBUS netclass minimum) trace over that gap is 1.45 mΩ.

**The answer was already in the repo.** `01_docs/CHANGELOG.md` line 604:

> "**USB-C delivery corner.** PMID crossed F.Cu<->B.Cu on 2 vias and F2 had ZERO
> vias on either pad at 0.775 W. Now 4 per F2 pad, 6 across the PMID pour, and 3
> per J5 VBUS contact pair, all sites derived from live pad geometry."

Two documents apart, and nobody joined them.

**Consequences.**
1. The **shipped** forward-path board copper is ≈ **3.0 mΩ**, not 9.32. The
   `ir_budget_mohm: 98` carried in `power_tree.yaml` is conservative by ~9 mΩ
   = 27 mV at 3 A; the true E-MARGIN slack is **+276.6 mV**, not +244.2 mV.
   Everything is in the safe direction — nothing blocks.
2. But the budget is labelled *"12 mΩ MEASURED"* and it measures a board nobody
   will receive. That label is now the stale one.
3. And **v1.5 shipped as an orderable release with the entire USB-C VBUS current
   crossing 2 × 0.3 mm vias** (2.5 A/via at the 5 A provisioning, against ~1.5 A/via
   IPC guidance). That defect was fixed in v1.6 as a *paste/thermal* item; it was
   never named as the via-ampacity defect it also was, and its resistance signature
   was then mis-read as solver noise.

**Recommended disposition:** CLOSE the open item in `power_tree.yaml` and
`DETAIL_DESIGN` sec.4 with the via-count table above rather than deferring it to
bench gates Q2/Q5. Keep 98 mΩ as the graded budget (it is conservative); re-label
the copper term as "≈3 mΩ measured on the v1.9+ stitching; 12 mΩ retained as a
deliberate pessimism." Anyone can reproduce the table in three lines of pcbnew.

# P1-2 — Bench gate Q6's 1.00 mA threshold has no bound on its largest unmodelled term. A correctly built board can land in the indeterminate band.

`power_tree.yaml` is unusually honest here: it names D2/R1 and C1/C2 as terms it
cannot bound, then sets **PASS ≤ 1.00 mA** = worst-case-good 744 µA **+ a 256 µA
"stated allowance"**. That allowance is asserted, not derived, and the term it has
to cover is the one with no data at all.

**The unbounded term, sized.** C1/C2 are 2 × 100 µF / 35 V polymer aluminium
(KNM2100UF35V149EC0055, C2982822) directly across VIN. The industry spec form for
this class is `I_L ≤ 0.1·C·V` (µA, µF, V) at **rated** voltage → 350 µA *each*,
700 µA for the pair at 35 V. At 12.6 V (36 % of rated) a typical polymer part sits
at 10-20 % of that — 70-140 µA for the pair, comfortably inside the allowance —
but a part at its spec limit is several hundred µA.
`02_parts/KNM2100UF35V149EC0055/` contains **`part.yaml` and nothing else**: no
leakage entry, no datasheet PDF. Nothing in the repo bounds it.

At the pessimistic end a **good** board reads 744 + ~350 + ~12 (D2/R1) ≈ **1.1 mA**
and lands in the "1.00-1.45 mA INDETERMINATE" band, or fails. That is one level
down from the defect the v1.9 correction was fixing ("300 µA would have condemned
a good board").

**Complete re-enumeration of every VIN/VBAT → GND path with SW1 OFF**, taken from
the shipped netlist (`fab`-sealed board, 26 pads on VIN), at VIN = 12.6 V:

| path | value | in the budget? |
|---|---|---|
| R6 + R7 = 56.88 kΩ (UVLO-A, permanently across VIN) | 221.5 µA | yes |
| R15 + R16 = 56.88 kΩ (UVLO-C) | 221.5 µA | yes |
| R8 100 kΩ → ENKILL → SW1 → GND | 126.0 µA | yes |
| R17 100 kΩ → ENKILL → SW1 → GND | 126.0 µA | yes |
| U2 VIN shutdown current (EN = 0) | ≤10 µA (SNVS499I: 1 typ / 10 max) | yes, at a *pessimistic* 9 typ |
| U11 VIN shutdown current | ≤10 µA | yes, same |
| R37 → D8 → Q8 drain (Q8 off) | ≤0.5 µA | yes |
| D1 SMBJ15A reverse leakage | ≤1 µA | yes |
| D2 (BZT52C12, K on VIN) → R1 100 kΩ → GND | 0-12 µA, no V-I data on file | **named, unbounded** |
| C1 + C2 polymer leakage | no data anywhere in the repo | **named, unbounded** |
| C9-C12, C24-C27 — eight 10 µF/50 V MLCC on VIN, IR floor 500 Ω·F ⇒ 50 MΩ each | ≈2 µA total | **NOT named** |
| Q2.5 / Q4.5 — the two AON6354 high-side **drains sit on VIN**; I_DSS → SW → L → 5VA/5VC → FB divider → GND | ≤2 µA @25 °C, ~20 µA @125 °C | **NOT named** |
| Q1 I_GSS via R1 | ≤1 µA | **NOT named** |

Nothing here is large. The point is that the file's own principle — *"a term
missing from a budget is indistinguishable from a term that is zero"* — is applied
to two terms and silently not applied to three others, and the one term that
actually decides whether Q6 can pass a good board is the one with no datasheet in
the project.

**Recommended disposition:** before the order, add the KNSCHA KNM2-series leakage
spec to `02_parts/KNM2100UF35V149EC0055/part.yaml` (it is a catalogue lookup), or
make Q6 a two-step measurement: read pack current with C1/C2 unfitted, then fitted.
Either turns 256 µA of "allowance" into a number, and turns the 1.00-1.45 mA
indeterminate band into a decision. Add the three unnamed terms to the enumeration
so the next reader does not have to re-derive that they are small.

# P2-1 — R42 cannot do what the release says it exists to do

`03_tscircuit/src/usb_hub_3s_v2.tsx` (R42 block): *"fit R42 and the rail drops to
~5.25 V, landing exactly on U12's 5.25 V V_RWM."* `DETAIL_DESIGN` sec.4: R42 is
*"what … exists to retire"* the U12 exceedance if gate Q9 says so.

That is true of the **nominal** only. Re-derived with the same arithmetic the
release uses everywhere else (Vref ±1.5 %, R_top ±0.1 %, R_bot ±1 %;
R12 ∥ R42 = 4.12 k ∥ 160 k = 4.0166 kΩ):

    worst max, R42 fitted = 1.215 x 1.015 x (1 + (4.0166 x 1.001)/(1.21 x 0.99))
                          = 5.372 V   ->  still +122 mV over 5.25 V
    worst max, as shipped                                  = 5.479 V  (+229 mV)

R42 **halves** the exceedance; it does not retire it. Gate Q9's decision rule is
written against an outcome the strap cannot deliver. (The min corner the tsx quotes,
5.125 V, reproduces exactly — the arithmetic is right, the conclusion drawn from it
is not.)

# P2-2 — U12 verdict: the shipped choice IS defensible, but the leakage bound in the record is optimistic by ~10×

Derived independently from `02_parts/USBLC6-2SC6/USBLC6-2SC6_ST.pdf` (ST doc 11265
rev 5, read p.1-3):

* **Table 1 "Absolute ratings" carries V_PP, T_stg, T_j, T_L and nothing else** —
  there is no V_BUS voltage limit. DETAIL_DESIGN sec.5.3 is correct on this.
* 5.25 V is the **V_RM test condition** for I_RM (Table 2: 10 nA typ / **150 nA
  max**). The only V_BUS rating is **V_BR ≥ 6.0 V at I_R = 1 mA** — a minimum with
  no typ and no max.
* At the 5.479 V no-load all-tolerance corner the device is ≥ 521 mV below its
  guaranteed breakdown floor. The exposure is elevated leakage, not conduction.
  V_BR also moves the safe way with temperature (a ~6 V TVS has a positive tempco).
* **Verdict: shipping above 5.25 V is defensible**, and the "ACCEPT + MEASURE"
  decision is the right one.

**The one correction.** sec.5.3 bounds the leakage as *"150 nA × 30 ≈ 4.5 µA ≈
25 µW"*. That applies Figure 4's ~30× temperature factor but **not** the voltage
extrapolation from 5.25 V to 5.479 V. Fitting an exponential between the datasheet's
two published points (150 nA @ 5.25 V, 1 mA @ 6.0 V) gives ≈1 decade per 197 mV, so
5.479 V is ≈**2.2 µA at 25 °C** and ≈**65 µA at 125 °C** — about 14× the stated
bound, 0.36 mW. Still thermally irrelevant in a SOT-23-6, so the conclusion is
unchanged; but the number in the file is not a bound, and gate Q9 will be graded
against it.

# P2-3 — "Over-current = F2" names the wrong element for the most likely fault

`ARCHITECTURE.md` and `DETAIL_DESIGN` sec.5.1 both assign over-current protection
of the C port to F2. On a **downstream VBUSC short**, F2 cannot trip:

    buck-C valley current limit  = 0.110 V / 10 mOhm = 11.0 A   (TI eq.10, TYP)
                                 ~ 10.4 A corrected for sec.2.5a's +5.6 % sense error
                                 = 9.4 A at the datasheet MIN V_CS(TH) (94 mV)
    F2 SMD2920-700 I_trip        ~ 14-15 A   (02_parts/SMD2920-700/part.yaml)
    LM5116 hiccup                = UVLO pulled to 200 mV after 256 cycles
                                 = 1.02 ms at 250 kHz

The buck limits below F2's trip point and then hiccups, so the **average** fault
current collapses far below F2's 7 A hold. F2 still earns its place in the two
cases it was sized for — a buck high-side short (the pack drives ~80 A through F1
and F2 against D5's ~10 V clamp) and a sustained external back-feed — so **no
hardware change is implied**. But the sentence a reader will act on names the
element that does not act.

Same file, adjacent error: `02_parts/SMD2920-700/part.yaml` states *"the buck-C
current limit (~7A via RS2=10mOhm)"*. TI eq. (10) with the same shunt gives
**11.0 A**, which is what `DETAIL_DESIGN` sec.2.5 says. Two documents in this repo
carry two different current limits for the same 10 mΩ shunt.

# P2-4 — V_CS(TH) is used at its TYPICAL only; the datasheet window is ±15 % and it changes sec.3's headline claim

SNVS499I §5.5 gives **V_CS(TH) = 94 / 110 / 126 mV** (VCCX = 0 V, RAMP = 0 V).
`DETAIL_DESIGN` sec.2.5 uses "0.11 V (TI's stated value)" without noting it is the
typical, and sec.3 then claims that at the 8.16 A hardware ceiling (3 ports ×
2.72 A) *"nothing intervenes"* because 8.16 A is *"still below buck-A's 11.0 A
valley limit (≈10.5 A corrected)"*.

At the datasheet **minimum**:

    valley limit        = 0.094 V / 10 mOhm                     = 9.40 A
    with the +4.8 % sense-loop error measured in sec.2.5a       = 8.97 A effective
    actual valley at the 8.16 A ceiling, V_IN 12.6 V (I_pp 1.774 A)
                        = 8.16 - 0.887                          = 7.27 A   -> no trip, 19 % margin
    but TI eq.(11) at that load:  R_S <= 0.094 / (8.16 + 2.2876) = 9.0 mOhm  <  the fitted 10 mOhm

So the direct valley comparison still clears with 19 % margin — the design is
fine — but **TI's own sizing constraint is violated at the port ceiling once the
minimum threshold is used**, and the honest worst-case limit is **8.97 A, i.e.
1.10× the reachable 8.16 A**, not the "1.75×" the document quotes against 6 A.
What actually happens at the ceiling is a whole-rail hiccup, not a per-port limit.
Recorded, not a change: three ports simultaneously at 2.72 A is not a real
operating point, but sec.3 says the case *"is reachable, not merely unlikely"* and
then grades it at a typical.

# P2-5 — Q2-Q5 have no dissipation line anywhere, and `eff: 0.9` has no derivation

`DETAIL_DESIGN` derives losses for Q1 (0.218 W), RS1/RS2 (0.36 / 0.25 W), L1/L2
DCR (0.486 / 0.338 W) and Q6 (0.11 W) — and has **no line for Q2-Q5**, the only
parts on the board that carry switching loss. The file's own rule is *"a value in
the schematic with no line here is UNJUSTIFIED"*, and thermal soak is bench gate
Q4, which will have nothing to compare against.

Derived here so the number exists (buck-A, 6 A, V_IN 12.6 V, D = 0.409):

    HS conduction : I_rms = 6 x sqrt(0.409) = 3.84 A ; 3.84^2 x 4.6 mOhm(hot) = 0.068 W
    HS switching  : 12.6 V x 6 A x 20 ns / 2 x 250 kHz                        = 0.189 W
    HS Coss       : 0.5 x 2 nF x 12.6^2 x 250 kHz                             = 0.040 W
    HS total ~ 0.30 W ;  LS total ~ 0.20 W (conduction 0.098 W + deadtime/Qrr)

AON6354 R_thetaJA (datasheet p.1) = **40 typ / 55 max °C/W** steady-state ⇒
ΔT ≤ 17 °C, T_j ≤ 57 °C at 40 °C ambient. **The parts are comfortable.** Summing
the derived terms gives ~1.6 W of loss on a 30.9 W rail ⇒ ~95 % efficiency, so the
`eff: 0.9` in `power_tree.yaml` is conservative — also never shown.

**Same class:** `C49`/`C50` (2 × 10 µF on VBUSC, C77100 = GRM32DR71E106KA12L,
25 V X7R) and `C33`/`C34` have **no line in DETAIL_DESIGN and no row in its sec.7
traceability table**, and C77100 has **no `02_parts/` entry** while its sibling
C77102 does. The DC-bias derating sec.2.3/2.4 computes for every other bulk
capacitor was never computed for the VBUS bulk.

# P2-8 — Hot-plug inrush is never analysed, and the answer is sensitive to a parameter nobody has bounded

There is no inrush limiting anywhere between the XT60 and 264 µF of input bulk
(C1 + C2 = 200 µF polymer at ~15 mΩ ESR each, plus ~64 µF effective MLCC). On
connection the pack-lead inductance and the total series resistance form an
under-damped LC:

    Z = sqrt(L/C) = sqrt(100 nH / 264 uF) = 19.5 mOhm
    zeta = R_series / 2Z
      R_series ~ 30 mOhm (pack ESR + leads + Q1 body diode + cap ESR)  -> zeta 0.77 -> overshoot   2 % -> VIN 12.9 V
      R_series ~ 10 mOhm (short leads, low-Z pack)                     -> zeta 0.26 -> overshoot  43 % -> VIN 18.0 V

D1's standoff is 15.0 V and its V_BR window is 16.7-18.5 V, so on a stiff pack
**D1 may conduct on every connection** and Q1's V_GS momentarily reaches −18 V
against a ±20 V rating (with D2 clamping above that). Nothing is over-stressed at
either end of the range, and D2 is exactly the part that covers it — but the
routine hot-plug case is analysed nowhere, D1 would be a wear item rather than a
fault-only part, and no bench gate scopes VIN on connection. **Cheap fix:** add a
scope-VIN-on-first-connection step to gate Q4 and record the overshoot.
