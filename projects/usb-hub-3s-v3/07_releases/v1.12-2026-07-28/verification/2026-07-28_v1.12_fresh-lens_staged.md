**5.600000 mm in both, to the nanometre.** The structural argument holds too: I

**Was keeping the body fixed right? Yes — and here is the number the release did

to **0.030 mm**. So the body graphics were *already* correctly sited against the

body outline that is right. Decision 1 is correct, and this is stronger evidence
for it than the release's own argument.

overmould, not less. Nothing of J5 is put at risk: the rear shell-slot pad's south
edge sits 1.07 mm inboard of the board edge. Keeping it is the right call for a
fix-pass (re-siting a connector is a mechanical change with its own review), and
the release discloses it in four places. Only the word "deliberate" overreaches
(F-9).

## 3. Decision 2 — ⌀0.65 → ⌀0.60. Numbers confirmed; I argued the other side and still agree

Re-measured two ways that share no code — KiCad's shape engine (bisected
`SHAPE::Collide`) and my own closed-form rounded-rect distance:

```
v1.11 (old geometry, ⌀0.65)   KiCad 0.4200 mm   analytic 0.4200 mm   (land A1)
v1.12 (corrected,  ⌀0.60)     KiCad 0.2194 mm   analytic 0.2194 mm   (land A1)
counterfactual (corrected, ⌀0.65) = 0.2194 − 0.025 = 0.1944 mm
```

Nearest feature is the A1/B12 roundrect corner (arc centre (−3.05, −3.25), r 0.15),
not the flat edge — the gate's parenthetical is correct. **All three claimed
figures reproduce exactly.**

**The case for keeping ⌀0.65, made properly.** The post is Ø0.50; ⌀0.60 leaves
0.050 mm radial, ⌀0.65 leaves 0.075 — a 33 % cut in seating slack. The sheet's
title block sets the general tolerance for 5–10 mm dimensions at **±0.15**, and the
`5.78` post span carries no explicit tolerance; at that extreme each post is
displaced 0.075 mm outward, which exactly consumes ⌀0.65's slack and doubly
exceeds ⌀0.60's. JLC's NPTH drill tolerance (~±0.05) and hole-position tolerance
(~±0.075) eat further into 0.050. And a post that will not enter lifts the part off
all twelve lands — the worst failure available, as the release itself says. There
was also an unexamined third option: keep ⌀0.65 and carry a DRU exception for this
one NPTH-to-pad pair (0.1944 is still 1.5× the 0.127 mm clearance this board
routes at), or shorten the A1/B12 land at the heel.

**Why I nonetheless agree with ⌀0.60.** The ±0.15 general tolerance does not govern
a *toleranced mating feature*: the sheet dimensions `2-Ø0.50` posts in the front
view and gives `Ø0.60` as the mating hole in the layout view — that pair **is** the
vendor's stated fit, and the vendor knows its own moulding tolerance. Decisive on
top of that: **JLC's own C5337088 package ships ⌀0.59999**, which I verified myself
(`HOLE~…~1.1811` × 0.254), and JLC both drills this board and places this part.
JLC's mesh independently confirms the posts as **Ø0.500 at x = ±2.890**, so the
0.050 mm radial figure is the vendor's intended fit rather than an accident, and the
posts project only 0.870 mm into a 1.6 mm board — locating pins in a blind
engagement, not a press fit. The lateral-play argument also checks out at the true
geometry: JLC's mesh gives the 8 narrow tails at **0.200 wide** on 0.30 lands
(0.05 mm/side) and the 4 merged tails at 0.450 on 0.60 lands (0.075/side). Trading a
vendor-sanctioned dimension for a bespoke DRU exception would be the wrong direction
under this repo's "waivers need evidence" rule.

**One correction to the reasoning, not the decision (F-5).** The release presents the
DRC failure as deciding and the vendor/JLC agreement as "two secondary reasons". That
inverts the evidential weight: `min_hole_clearance: 0.200` is a project convention with
no fab provenance, and `fab_tiers.yaml` models no hole-to-copper capability at any
tier. The arguments that actually carry ⌀0.60 are the vendor's nominal and JLC's own
package. Worth saying, because a future reader will otherwise treat 0.200 as a fab
number.

same page, in the top view, next to the housing depth: **`0.40 ± 0.10`**.

Two independent confirmations that 0.40 is the exposed tail: `7.35` (housing) + `0.40`
= **7.75**, and JLC's own 3D model for C5337088 measures **8.9400 × 7.7500 × 4.2900 mm**
— 7.7500 exactly. I then measured the lead directly out of that mesh, anchoring the
frame on three features I could identify unambiguously and which all agree with the
footprint to ≤0.005 mm:

```
alignment posts   x -3.140..-2.640 and +2.640..+3.140  ->  Ø0.500 at x = ±2.890 (span 5.780)
                  y +2.255..+2.755                      ->  ALIGNMENT LINE at y = +2.505
front shell leg   centre y = +3.005  = 0.500 N of the alignment line   ✓ footprint 0.500
rear  shell leg   centre y = -1.175  = 3.680 S                          ✓ footprint 3.680
shell legs        x = ±4.320  -> span 8.640   (vs the 8.650 SLOT centres — the release's
                                               8.64-is-the-leg-span reading, confirmed)
12 contact tails  4 × 0.450 wide (x ±3.20, ±2.40) + 8 × 0.200 (x ±1.75, ±1.25, ±0.75, ±0.25)
                  y +3.575 (housing rear face) .. +3.975 (tip)  =  0.400 mm exposed
                  -> the lead spans 1.070 .. 1.470 mm north of the alignment line
```

| | land | lead | heel fillet | toe fillet | lead on land |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vendor / **v1.12** | 0.500 .. 1.640 | 1.070 .. 1.470 | **0.570** | **0.170** | **100 %** |
| **v1.11 as sealed** | 0.720 .. 2.170 | 1.070 .. 1.470 | **0.350** | **0.700** | **100 %** |

*(0.570 + 0.400 + 0.170 = 1.140 — the vendor's land is heel fillet + lead + toe fillet,
which is what a land pattern is.)*

**So in v1.11 the lead was entirely on its land, with 0.350 mm of heel fillet.** No
contact was "0.220 mm inboard of its land"; nothing hung "past the heel onto solder
mask"; 80.7 % is a land-versus-land ratio wearing a lead's label. The CHANGELOG's
"solder-bridge invitation at the heel and a starved joint at the toe" also inverts the
direction its own `lead_overlap.txt` gives — the surplus is at the **toe**.

**This does not change the verdict, for three reasons:**

1. The land pattern is now the vendor's. That is correct on its own terms and needs no
   severity story.
2. **The CPL datum defect is independent, real, and sufficient on its own** — v1.11 told
   the machine to set the part 0.2025 mm north of where its own Ø0.50 posts sit in
   ⌀0.65 holes with 0.075 mm of radial clearance. I verified it end-to-end including
   the method (§5). The MANIFEST is already careful here ("an INDEPENDENT failure mode,
   not one that adds to the land error … which you get depends on whether the posts or
   the pick-head win"), and that carefulness is well placed.
3. **v1.11's genuine land defect, stated correctly, is still worth this release:** a
   **fillet-balance inversion** — heel 0.350 instead of 0.570, a 39 % smaller heel
   fillet, and the heel is where a gull-wing joint's strength lives — plus **27 % more
   paste per land** (1.45 vs 1.14) with **1.050 mm of aperture length carrying no lead
   (72 % of the aperture, vs 0.740 mm / 65 % now)** on a 0.5 mm-pitch row. That is a
   real assembly-yield argument, correctly signed, and it survives the correction.

The `descr` is the urgent part: it is inside `04_kicad/`, inside this release's
`source/`, and inside every future release's, and it currently states a physical claim
about C5337088 that C5337088's own drawing contradicts.
