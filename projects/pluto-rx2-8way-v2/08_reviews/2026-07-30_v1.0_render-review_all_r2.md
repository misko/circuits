## What I see, per view

### `pdf/schematic.pdf` — the human schematic

One landscape sheet, 900 × 450 pt (12.5 × 6.25 in), no frame, no title block.
Drawing style is tscircuit's own renderer (Producer: `cairo 1.18.0`, no Creator —
unlike `pcb_layers.pdf`/`assembly.pdf`, which both declare `Creator: PCBNEW,
Producer: KiCad PDF`). Yellow-filled component boxes with teal refdes, hexagonal
net-label flags, green wires, dark-red symbol graphics.

The circuit as drawn: an RP2040-Zero (`U_MCU`, drawn as an anonymous 23-pin box)
feeds its 3V3 pin through a ferrite (`FB_3V3`) to a 3.3 V rail decoupled by
`C_BULK` 4.7 µF, `C_SW2` 1 µF and `C_SW1` 100 nF, which powers `U_SW` (drawn as an
anonymous 25-pin box). Four GPIOs drive `SEL_V1..SEL_V4` through 47 Ω series
resistors into `SW_V1..SW_V4`, each with a 10 kΩ pulldown, into the switch's
V1..V4. Eight switch RF ports go to `ANT1..ANT7` and `RX1_TAP`; the common port
goes to `RX2_OUT`. `J_ANT8` is wired straight to `J_RX1`, with a 220 Ω + 220 Ω
resistive tap into the switch's RF8. A GPIO drives a status LED through 680 Ω.

I traced the power path and the signal chain and both are followable **in
principle**. In practice the sheet fights the reader:

- **Every net label, every value, every ground symbol is printed with a line
  through it.** The label text baseline sits on the hexagon outline, so `SW_V3`,
  `SEL_V2`, `N3V3`, `RX1_TAP`, `RX1_TAP_MID`, `220Ω`, `680Ω`, all four `47Ω`, and
  every `GND` read as struck out. It is a renderer-level defect, not a one-off:
  it affects the whole document uniformly.
- **At U_MCU pin 21 / U_SW pin 2, two different nets are drawn as one smear.**
  The `N3V3_MOD` label (anchored at the module's 3V3 pin) and the `ANT2` label
  (anchored at the switch's RF2 pin) are drawn on top of each other. The
  composite reads `N3V3_MOD2` — solid `N3V3_MOD` with a pale ghost `ANT2` showing
  through and the U_SW pin-number `2` abutting the label tip. What the picture
  says is that the module's 3.3 V output goes to an RF antenna port.
- **At R_S3, `LED_STAT_A` and `SEL_V3` print on top of each other** into an
  illegible composite whose tip points at the resistor.
- **At U_SW pins 8–13, six label hexagons are stacked with overlapping outlines**
  (`N3V3`, `SW_V1`, `SW_V2`, `SW_V3`, `SW_V4`, `ANT5`), and the pin-7 ground
  symbol's `GND` text prints into the `N3V3` label: `GN·N3V3`.
- **At U_MCU pins 2–3, `SEL_V2` and `SEL_V3` hexagons overlap each other.**
- **At J_ANT3 pin 1 the `ANT3` label is drawn twice** (a ghosted duplicate offset
  a few pixels) with a green GND wire running through the label body.
- **No title block.** `pdftotext` finds no `pluto`, no `rev`, no `sheet`, no
  `date`, no `2026` anywhere in the file. Nothing on the sheet says which board or
  which revision this is.
- **No part identity for either IC.** `pdftotext` finds zero occurrences of
  `RP2040`, `PE42482`, `KH-SMA`, `BLM21` or `KT-0603`. A reader cannot tell what
  the switch is, what the MCU module is, or that the board's power and programming
  both arrive over the module's USB-C.

Against the netlist: I parsed all 40 nets node-by-node and every connection I
could resolve in the drawing matches. The 15 `unconnected-(U_MCU-…)` nets match
the small open-circle pin-end markers drawn on GP5–GP15, GP26–GP29 and the 5V
pin. `U_MCU.22 (GND)` is drawn as a wire running across to `U_SW.1 (LS)` rather
than to a ground symbol — legal, and correct (both are on GND), but it makes the
module's ground look like a signal. **No drawn connection contradicts the netlist;
one drawn *label* does** (the `N3V3_MOD`/`ANT2` smear above).

Two pin ties that look alarming and are correct: `U_SW.1 (LS)` → GND and
`U_SW.20 (NC)` → GND. `02_parts/PE42482A-X/part.yaml` records that LS has a 1 MΩ
internal pull-up and **must** be grounded (floating it silently swaps RF1↔RF8,
RF2↔RF3, RF4↔RF7, RF5↔RF6), and that pin 20 "may be GND or left open". Both as
drawn are right.

### `pdf/assembly.pdf`

One A4 landscape sheet. The board is plotted at 1:1 in the **top-left corner**;
roughly 83 % of the page is blank white. Lines are pale grey and pale yellow on
white — printed, most of it will be invisible.

At 600 dpi the detail is legible, and it is a mess of overlapping text:

- Bottom-left: two `MountingHole_3.2mm_M3` strings print on top of each other →
  `untingHole_3.24mm_M3ngHole_3.2mm_M3`.
- `C9900173620` prints across `J_ANT5` → `C9900ĀNT53620`.
- `C2286` collides with `47Ω`; `680Ω` collides with the `LED_ST`/`R_LED` boxes.
- `FB_3V3` and `C_BULK` are each printed twice, overlapped, with a vertical
  `C3716677` and a vertical `4.7uF` running through them.
- The four `47Ω` values of R_S1..R_S4 collide with U_MCU's pad numbers 1, 2, 3, 4;
  the R_S* part outlines print as an illegible stack.
- Parts are primarily labelled by **LCSC code**, not refdes (`C504007` × 10,
  `C5121458`, `C2286`, `C137948`, `C3716677`) — harder to cross-reference to the
  schematic than refdes would be.

Two genuinely useful things this drawing does show: U_MCU's pad numbering
(1–9 down the left column, 10–14 across the top, 15–23 down the right, with pad 1
at the bottom-left in the as-placed 180° orientation), and a dashed F.Fab
rectangle for the USB-C receptacle that **crosses the south board edge by about
1.1 mm** — so the plug can seat. Good.

The four mounting holes are labelled H1 (NW), H2 (NE), H3 (SW) and H4 — and H4 is
inboard on the south edge, not at the SE corner. The SE corner is empty.

### `pdf/pcb_layers.pdf`

Eight pages. On every page the board occupies about 3 % of an A4 landscape sheet.
`pdftotext` over the whole file returns **only the board's own silkscreen
strings** — there is no layer-name caption on any page. Nothing tells the reader
whether they are looking at F.Cu, In1, In2, B.Cu, F.Silkscreen, B.Silkscreen,
F.Mask or B.Mask. By colour and content the order appears to be exactly that:
page 1 F.Cu (traces + planes), pages 2–3 solid inner planes with via antipads,
page 4 B.Cu plane, page 5 F.Silkscreen in pale yellow on white (near-invisible
when printed), **page 6 blank** (there is effectively no bottom silkscreen),
pages 7–8 the two mask layers.

### `verification/render_top_bare.png` — the no-components truth view

50.0 × 73.0 mm rectangular board. Ten SMA jack lands in a rosette around a central
QFN. Dense ground stitching across the whole board — thousands of vias. RF traces
run radially from the QFN to each jack as coplanar waveguide: a narrow copper line
with a clean no-copper gap either side, in a solid pour. This looks like a
carefully done RF layout.

The QFN's exposed pad carries four vias. The eight small parts around it
(R_PD1..4, C_SW1, C_SW2) sit in a tight three-row cluster south of the QFN.

The silkscreen legend block in the lower-left is the best thing in this release:

```
pluto-rx2-8way-v2  8-WAY AoA
70 MHz - 6 GHz
RX ONLY - NO TRANSMIT
PASSIVE ANT - 0 VDC MAX
ANT8 = RX1 TAP -20.26 dB
RX1/RX2 = SDR OUTPUTS
U_MCU HAND-SOLDER ONLY
USB-C = PWR + BOOTLOADER
```

That block answers, on the board itself, every question I formed while reading the
netlist — including the one asymmetry a user would otherwise trip over
(`J_ANT8` is *not* switched; it passes straight through to `J_RX1` and only a
−20.26 dB resistive tap reaches the switch's RF8).

What is wrong on this face:

- **Only three mounting holes are at corners.** NW, NE and SW are at a consistent
  3.8–4.0 mm inset; the fourth sits on the south edge 12.7 mm right of the SW
  hole. The SE corner has nothing.
- **`R_PD2`'s legend is closer to `C_SW1` than to `R_PD2`.** The six parts around
  the QFN are visually identical land pairs; five legends sit beside their own
  part, but `R_PD2` is pushed up beside the QFN, ~4.4 mm from R_PD2 (41.5, 52.6)
  and ~3.5 mm from C_SW1 (42.8, 52.6).
- **Refdes silk prints over pads** for `R_PD3`, `C_SW2`, `C_SW1`, `R_LED` and
  `R_S3`. The fab clips silk off pads, so those legends will arrive partially
  erased.
- **The SMA legends interleave two naming systems in the inter-connector gaps.**
  Reading the top band left to right you get `ANT8`, `J_RX2`, `J_ANT8`, `R_T1`,
  `R_T2`, `RX1`, `J_RX1` across three connectors. Measured ownership leads are
  thin: `ANT8` is 6.2 mm from J_ANT8 and 8.1 mm from J_RX2 (1.9 mm lead);
  `J_RX2` is 5.2 mm from J_RX2 and 6.4 mm from J_ANT8 (1.2 mm lead). policy_audit
  reports its own thinnest lead as 1.45 mm.
- **`R_T1` and `R_T2` sit in the 5.9 mm slot between the J_ANT8 and J_RX1
  flanges**, inside both jacks' coupling-nut swept circles (0.45 mm to each).

### `verification/render_bottom_bare.png` and `twin_bottom.png`

No bottom-side components. No bottom silkscreen — no board name, no revision, no
warning. A solid ground pour with dense stitching. Ten SMA footprints present as
an exposed RF centre pad surrounded by four large exposed ground posts, all
mask-relieved. The mirroring is consistent and confirms the missing SE hole from
the other side.

### `twin_top.png`, `twin_iso_nw.png`, `twin_edge_west.png`, `twin_edge_east.png`

Ten identical gold SMA jacks stand in a rosette; the four at 45° (ANT1, ANT3,
ANT5, ANT7) and six square-on (ANT2, ANT4, ANT6, ANT8, RX1, RX2). All are
**vertical** jacks — every cable leaves perpendicular to the board. That is the
right choice for an AoA array and there is no connector facing the wrong way.
No body sits off its pads; nothing overhangs the board edge; nothing looks
physically impossible.

The RP2040-Zero renders with **no body** — bare gold castellated pads in two
columns of nine plus five at the north end, with a silk outline. Per
`missing_models.txt` ("bodies mounted: 27/27") and `fab/cpl.csv` (27 placements,
no U_MCU), this is a *not-populated* part, not a missing model. The board's own
silk says `U_MCU HAND-SOLDER ONLY`. Correct, not a defect.

The edge views show what the top views cannot: **the SMA jacks are through-hole
and their leads protrude about 2 mm below the board.** The board cannot lie flat,
and with ten exposed RF centre pads on the underside it must not lie on anything
conductive. There is no silk on that face to say so.

Cosmetic model artifact, noted so it is not mistaken for a finding: the JLC SMA
models carry an `LCEDA` watermark on the flange, visible in `twin_edge_east.png`.

### `verification/twin_top_courtyard_overlay.png`

Every SMA shows magenta (measured) inside green (expected) inside red
(courtyard), concentric. `twin_overlay.md` grades 11 refs at a worst centre delta
of 0.079 mm and 0.000 mm outward excursion, 0 courtyard excursions, calibration
anisotropy 0.9994. **The renders are the board**, so the visual review above is
valid rather than void. The remaining 16 refs are declared unmeasurable by size,
not silently passed.

### `verification/overlay_LED_ST.png`

The 0603 LED body with a yellow marking on the **left** terminal and the model's
own marking dots on the **right**. Our silk `K` sits above-left, at pad 1.
`twin_report.csv` states it plainly: our polarity marking is at pad 1 (0.68 mm),
JLC's is at pad 2 (0.23 mm), "the two libraries NUMBER this part's terminals
oppositely, so the pad fit is 180 deg wrong PHYSICALLY and offset 0 is what
places the part correctly." `fab/cpl.csv` uses rotation 0.0 — which is the
correct choice. But the same file also emits
`ROT-DB-SUGGEST … add LCSC row C2286,180`, which contradicts its own conclusion
and would install the LED backwards if anyone acted on it.

### SMA spacing against a 5/16-inch coupling nut

Computed from `fab/cpl.csv`. A 5/16" nut is 9.166 mm across corners, so two nuts
need 9.166 mm centre-to-centre to clear.

| pair | centre-to-centre | nut-to-nut |
|---|---|---|
| J_ANT8 – J_RX2 | 9.933 mm | **+0.767 mm** |
| J_ANT8 – J_RX1 | 11.000 mm | +1.834 mm |
| J_ANT3 – J_ANT4 | 11.136 mm | +1.970 mm |
| J_ANT2–J_ANT3, J_ANT1–J_RX2, J_ANT5–J_ANT6, J_ANT6–J_ANT7 | 11.646 mm | +2.480 mm |
| J_ANT1 – J_ANT2 | 12.028 mm | +2.862 mm |
| everything else | ≥ 13.144 mm | ≥ +3.978 mm |

Every pair clears. The tightest clears by 0.767 mm — 0.38 mm per side. That is
enough for the nuts to coexist and to spin, and **not** enough for a wrench on
any of them. Every cable is finger-tight only, and the outer cables must go on
last. Board-edge clearance is fine everywhere: the tightest is J_ANT8 and J_RX1,
whose nut circles reach 0.42 mm inside the north edge (free air above, no issue).

## READABILITY VERDICT — `pdf/schematic.pdf` (discharges the human-graded S6 item)

**S6: FAIL.**

An engineer *can* follow the power path (module 3V3 → FB_3V3 → 3.3 V rail →
C_BULK/C_SW2/C_SW1 → U_SW VDD) and the signal chain (GPIO → 47 Ω → 10 kΩ pulldown
→ V1..V4; ANT1..ANT7 + the RX1 tap → RF1..RF8; RFC → RX2; ANT8 straight through to
RX1 with a 440 Ω tap). The topology is legible and correct against the netlist.

It fails on three counts that are not stylistic:

1. **One label pair actively lies.** `N3V3_MOD` and `ANT2` are drawn as one smear
   at U_MCU pin 21 / U_SW pin 2. The picture says the 3.3 V rail is wired to an RF
   antenna port. Nothing on the sheet tells the reader otherwise. An engineer
   debugging from this document will chase that first.
2. **Two more label pairs are illegible** (`LED_STAT_A`/`SEL_V3` at R_S3, the
   doubled `ANT3` at J_ANT3.1), a six-deep hexagon stack sits at U_SW pins 8–13
   with `GND` printed into `N3V3`, and every label, value and ground symbol on the
   sheet is drawn with a line through its text.
3. **The document cannot identify itself or its parts.** No title block, no board
   name, no revision, no date; and zero occurrences of `RP2040`, `PE42482`,
   `KH-SMA`, `BLM21` or `KT-0603` in the entire file.

The waiver that was supposed to cover items in class 2 asserts these collisions
exist only in the machine `.kicad_sch` and not in the shipped human render. I
opened the shipped human render. They are all in it, and there are more.

The fix is a label-placement pass and a title block in the tscircuit source — a
small job, but it has to happen before this sheet is anyone's reference document.
