# ADVERSARIAL LAYOUT / THERMAL / POWER-INTEGRITY RE-REVIEW — crow-recorder-central v1.1

## What v1.1 actually changed (and what I re-measured)

v1.1 is a **source-compliance** revision: `layout:` blocks + `keep_short` budgets were
added to `02_parts/*/part.yaml`, and a **P-ADJ** waiver was added to
`03_src/rules/policy_waivers.yaml`. The BOARD is unchanged — I confirmed
`04_kicad/crow_recorder_central.kicad_pcb` is **byte-identical** to
`07_releases/v1.0-2026-07-22/source/…` (both sha256 `f8296e2b64756d1a…`), and
`git status` shows no modification. So every v1.0 electrical defect is still physically
present; the new material is the **grading layer** that now claims to police it. My job
was to (a) re-measure the declared budgets against the board, (b) verify the waiver's
scoping claims, and (c) find what the new budgets DON'T see.

I loaded the sealed board in pcbnew 10.0.4 and measured net pad-spans (the exact metric
P-ADJ uses: `max` pairwise pad distance) plus routed track length and via counts.

## Verdict up front

**ORDER** — the board is the same one two independent v1.0 red-teams already graded
ORDER; v1.1 adds no new copper and therefore no new P0. But the source-compliance layer
is **weaker than it looks**: P-ADJ measures mostly nets that were never at risk,
blanket-waives the two that are, and is structurally blind to the board's two highest-value
PI defects (buck hot-loop, USB pair layer-continuity) — as the waivers themselves admit.
Findings below are about that grading gap plus the unchanged board risk; none is a new
order-blocker.

## Findings

### P1 — AIN_P4 / AIN_P8: two audio channels are 96 mm / 64 mm single-ended antennas (confirmed, unchanged, now waived)

Confirmed on the sealed board: `AIN_P4` span **95.99 mm** / routed **106.33 mm** (3 vias)
against partner `AIN_N4` = 1.51 mm; `AIN_P8` span **63.61 mm** / routed **69.51 mm**
against `AIN_N8` = 1.51 mm. These are PCM1865 ADC inputs on an **audio recorder** —
channels 4 and 8 run ~8× and ~5× over the datasheet-derived 12 mm keep-short as a
long single-ended pickup loop (partner leg 100× shorter → no CMRR benefit). The
electrical consequence is concrete: **2 of the 8 record channels will have materially
worse SNR/THD/hum-pickup than the other 6**, in exactly the corridor the brief flags.

This is v1.0 finding **L3**, re-confirmed. v1.1 does not fix it (sealed board) — it
files it under P-ADJ + PCM1865 `part.yaml keep_short why` and defers the fix (re-place
R80/R81 local to the ADC, or drop the monitor tap) to a real v1.1 **board** rev. Root
cause verified: `AIN_P4` has a 3rd pad (R80) ~90 mm across the board; `AIN_P8` a 3rd pad
(R81) ~60 mm across — the tap resistors, not the ADC, set the span. Deferral is the only
option on a sealed board and matches the prior ORDER disposition; **not an order-blocker,
but the buyer of THIS board gets a 6-good/2-degraded channel count.**

### P2 — P-ADJ is a CHECK-LEVEL blanket waiver: it cannot enforce its own "two-channel, not systemic" scope

`policy_audit.py` computes a `viol` list of every over-budget keep_short net, then calls
`grade("P-ADJ", not viol, …)`. `grade()` (lines 75-87) looks up the waiver by **check id
only** — if `"P-ADJ"` is present with a ≥40-char `why`, the ENTIRE check flips to WAIVED
**regardless of which nets, or how many, are in `viol`**. The waiver text enumerates
AIN_P4/AIN_P8, but the mechanism does not read that enumeration. Consequence: if a future
source change, a re-place, or a net I didn't hand-check pushes a **third** keep_short net
over budget, P-ADJ still reports WAIVED with no new review — the gate silently absorbs it
under the AIN_P4/P8 rationale. I closed the gap for THIS board by measuring all 24 budgeted
nets by hand (only P4/P8 violate), but the gate itself provides that assurance to nobody.
A per-net waiver key (or an allow-list of waived net names checked against `viol`) would
make the "two-channel, not systemic" claim machine-true instead of honor-system.

### P2 — keep_short coverage gap: P-ADJ polices the already-short nets and is silent on the board-spanning sensitive/aggressor nets

The budgeted set (AIN/VREF/LDO/BK_SW/FB/XIN/XTAL2) is dominated by nets that are short by
construction (SW 4.5 mm, FB 7-9 mm, AIN siblings 1-7 mm). Meanwhile the actual long,
board-spanning, PI-relevant nets on this audio board carry **no budget at all**, so P-ADJ
measures nothing on them:

| net | routed | span | vias | what it is |
|-----|------|------|------|------|
| BCLK | 99.34 mm | 63.08 | 6 | I2S/TDM bit clock, XU316→both ADCs |
| LRCK | 99.67 mm | 60.92 | 3 | frame clock |
| MCLK_A | 66.12 mm | 63.93 | 2 | master audio clock |
| BEEP_G1 | 95.77 mm | 91.04 | 2 | FET **gate-drive** (dv/dt aggressor) |
| BEEP_G8/G2/G3/G4/G6/G7 | 62-82 mm | 53-78 | 0-4 | 8× pod-beeper gate nets |

These are the nets whose length actually matters (clock integrity + the beeper gate-drive
aggressors the brief calls out). A green "P-ADJ: board honours every keep_short budget"
line reads as *"the layout is clean"* while the ~100 mm clocks and the 60-96 mm gate-drive
runs were never in the budget's field of view. (To be fair to the board: the clocks run
over the confirmed-solid In1/In4 GND planes, and my segment-proximity scan — below — finds
the gate nets do NOT create new crosstalk corridors, so this is a **gate-scope** finding,
not a new board defect. But the gate's coverage should not be mistaken for a coverage of
what matters.)

**Sub-case — crystal XOUT evades the budget.** The X322524 part.yaml budgets `XIN` (16.7,
pass) and `XTAL2` (6.6, pass) and asserts the oscillator is *"a compact ground-guarded
island, minimal loop, hard against the XIN/XOUT pins."* But the third oscillator net —
`XOUT` (XU316 pin33 → Rf R21 / series R20) — is **not budgeted**, and it measures span
**17.52 mm / routed 24.95 mm with 2 vias, layer-hopping F.Cu(16.2)→In2(8.8 mm)**. The
24 MHz driver-side node is neither compact nor single-layer, contradicting the part.yaml's
own "minimal loop" attestation, and P-ADJ can't see it because the short cap-side net
(XTAL2) was the one named. Low functional risk (24 MHz reference, CL already dispositioned
F7), hence P2 — but it is a concrete example of the budget being placed on the short half
of a loop while the long half runs uninstrumented.

### P2 — P-ADJ's pad-span model is structurally blind to the board's two highest-value PI rules (waivers concede this)

The P-ADJ metric is `max pairwise pad distance` of a single net. Two of the most important
PI rules on this board are, by construction, invisible to that model — and the source now
says so out loud:

- **Buck input hot-loop (v1.0 L2).** `AP61102Z6-7/part.yaml layout.notes` and the P-ADJ
  waiver both state P-ADJ *cannot* gate it: VIN is the board-wide shared 5V rail (span
  ~165 mm), so there is no per-buck input net whose span equals the VIN→Cin→GND loop. The
  actual defect (U10 VIN→C10 = 5.57 mm, U11 VIN→C13 = 6.14 mm, datasheet wants <~2 mm) is
  unchanged on the sealed board and deferred to a board rev. The hot-loop is a **loop-area**
  quantity spanning three nets (VIN, GND, SW-adjacent); a single-net pad-span gate can
  never express it.
- **USB HS pair layer-continuity (v1.0 L1).** Re-measured unchanged: `USB_DP` = F.Cu 6.16
  + **B.Cu 10.40** + In2 4.66 mm (4 vias); `USB_DM` = F.Cu 11.68 + In2 10.47 mm (3 vias,
  **0 on B.Cu**). The two legs share no layer for ~10 mm → broken 90 Ω intra-pair coupling
  + via-stub reflections. `TPD4EUSB30DQAR/part.yaml` concedes this is *"a layer-continuity
  defect P-ADJ's pad-span model does not see."* Total length still matched (21.2 vs 22.2
  mm), so it will likely still enumerate; deferred to board rev.

Both are honestly documented and correctly dispositioned P1/deferred by the prior review;
I raise the **P2** here specifically about the gate: the source-compliance layer should
not leave a reader thinking a passing/`WAIVED` P-ADJ implies the buck loops and USB pair
were evaluated. They were not; they can't be, by this gate.

## Checks I ran that found NO new defect (measured)

- **Beeper-gate → audio crosstalk corridor scan.** I did a same-layer segment-to-segment
  min-distance sweep of all 8 `BEEP_G*` gate nets against every `AUD_*`/`AIN_*` victim at
  <0.5 mm edge. Despite the gate nets being 60-96 mm long, the **only** sub-0.5 mm corridor
  is `BEEP_G7 ↔ AUD_N7 = 0.354 mm centerline` — i.e. exactly the known v1.0 **L4**, and
  nothing new. The long gate runs do not create additional adjacency (they route apart from
  the audio nets except at port 7). L4 remains a P2 (DRC-clean, ADR-0005 slowed edge limits
  dv/dt).
- **Sibling AIN channels within budget** — confirmed (all AIN_P/N except P4/P8 ≤7.24 mm),
  validating the waiver's non-systemic claim.
- **Layer adjacency / stackup** — 6 Cu layers enabled (F/In1/In2/In3/In4/B); the v1.0
  review's In1/In4 95% GND reference planes are unchanged, so every signal layer keeps an
  adjacent GND plane. No new adjacency defect.
- **Switch nodes** — BK1_SW/BK2_SW span 4.47 mm, routed 4.78/4.84 mm, **0 vias**, track-only
  (within the 8 mm budget); small radiating area, unchanged and fine.

## VERDICT

**ORDER** — Would I order THIS (sealed, byte-identical-to-v1.0) board as-is? Yes, for the
same reason both v1.0 red-teams did: no P0, DRC/parity clean at seal, solid GND planes,
real 16-via XU316 EP thermal array, tight track-only switch nodes. v1.1 changed no copper.
The new material (P-ADJ + keep_short budgets + the P-ADJ waiver) is **honest** — the authored
"measured" numbers match my independent pcbnew spans to ≤0.1 mm, and the "two-channel, not
systemic" scoping is true. My findings are that the **gate is thinner than it presents**:
it blanket-waives at the check level (can't enforce its own scope), budgets the short nets
while the ~100 mm audio clocks / 60-96 mm gate-drive / 25 mm crystal-XOUT run uninstrumented,
and is structurally blind to the buck hot-loop and USB pair — the two defects most worth
gating. Those are P2 grading-quality issues plus the unchanged P1 board risks (AIN_P4/P8
degraded channels, buck L2, USB L1), all already dispositioned to the v1.1 **board** rev.
Order the board; carry the four P1s into the board revision, and tighten P-ADJ to a per-net
waiver + budget the long clock/gate/crystal-driver nets before the next board is graded.
