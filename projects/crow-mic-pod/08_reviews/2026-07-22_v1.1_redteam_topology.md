# Adversarial topology / protection / ratings review — crow-mic-pod v1.1

Zero-context re-review. I re-traced every protection chain from the sealed
netlist (`07_releases/v1.0-2026-07-21/source/crow_mic_pod.net`), confirmed each
against pad-net on the physically sealed board via pcbnew, and re-derived every
clamp-vs-protected-part rating pair from `02_parts/*/part.yaml`. The board is
UNCHANGED since v1.0; v1.1 added `layout:` blocks and P-ADJ waivers to source and
the new `03_src/rules/electrical_invariants.yaml` E-INV gate. I read the prior
v1.0 topology memo and deliberately hunted for what it missed. **I found one.**

## Primary evidence (re-verified this pass)

- Beeper clamp cluster, from both netlist and sealed PCB:
  `BZ_P: BZ1.1, D2.1, D3.1, R12.2` and `BEEP_RET: BZ1.2, D2.2, D3.2, J1.6`.
  pcbnew confirms D2 **and D3** each have pad1(K)=BZ_P, pad2(A)=BEEP_RET
  (D3 at (80.80, 85.00), rot 0). BOM/CPL: D2 (SS14) populated, D3 absent (DNP).
- D1 TPD2E2U06: pad3(IO1)=AUDIO_P, pad5(IO2)=AUDIO_N, pad4=GND, pads1/2 NC —
  correct ESD steering clamp at the connector; 68R (R10/R11) sit between the
  op-amp outputs and the clamp. Correct (matches prior).
- J1 RJ45 8 contacts match the ADR-0004 / DETAIL_DESIGN interop map pin-for-pin.
  R15 (shield bond) DNP; SHIELD floats (J1.SH/R15.1/TP6). Correct.
- Rating pairs re-checked: SS14 40V/1A vs 5V/150mA; TPD2E2U06 VRWM 5.5V vs
  2.47V±0.2 bias; CMT-8504 6.0V op-max vs 5V pair; OPA1678 CM/output vs VMID;
  RJ45 1.5A/contact vs ~150mA. No populated part exceeds a limit.

## Findings

### P1 — DNP beeper TVS (D3, SMAJ6.0A) is oriented so it CANNOT clamp the flyback it is documented to clamp

**This is what the v1.0 memo missed.** The prior topology review checked that D3
*shares D2's orientation* and pronounced it "Correct." Orientation-consistency is
not the test. The test is whether that orientation delivers the documented
function — and for a **unidirectional** TVS it does not.

Evidence chain:
- `02_parts/SMAJ6.0A/part.yaml`: `type: tvs_unidirectional`, `pins {1: K, 2: A}`.
  This is the unidirectional SMAJ6.0**A**, not the bidirectional SMAJ6.0**CA**.
- Netlist + PCB: D3 pad1 (**cathode**) on BZ_P (supply side), pad2 (anode) on
  BEEP_RET (switched node) — identical to the D2 schottky.
- Flyback physics: BZ_P is the fixed +5V feed (BEEP_5V→R12→BZ_P); BEEP_RET is
  the low-side-switched node (AO3400A at the central end). Coil current flows
  BZ_P→BZ1→BEEP_RET. When the central switch opens, the coil sustains that
  current and **drives BEEP_RET ABOVE BZ_P** (that is the kick). The schottky D2
  (K=BZ_P, A=BEEP_RET) then forward-conducts BEEP_RET→BZ_P and freewheels the
  coil at BZ_P+0.4V. Correct — D2 is right.
- **But a unidirectional TVS in the SAME orientation is forward-biased by that
  same kick.** With cathode on BZ_P and anode on BEEP_RET, when BEEP_RET flies
  above BZ_P the TVS sees anode > cathode → it conducts as an ordinary forward
  diode at ~0.7–1V, clamping BEEP_RET to ~BZ_P+0.9V ≈ 5.9V. It **never enters
  reverse breakdown**, so it never presents its Vc≈10.3V across the coil.

Consequence: the documented rationale is physically unachievable.
`0001-input-protection.md`, `0002-beeper-clamp.md`, and the SMAJ6.0A part.yaml
all state the TVS option exists to "let the coil de-energize at ~7-10V instead
of 0.4V (faster current decay, crisper burst envelope for the matched filter)."
In the shipped orientation, populating D3 per the swap matrix ("remove D2, add
D3, identical orientation") yields a ~5.9V forward clamp — essentially the same
slow decay as the schottky, **not** a 7–10V fast-decay clamp. The stated benefit
cannot be obtained. For a unidirectional TVS to clamp THIS flyback in breakdown,
its cathode must sit on **BEEP_RET** (the node that kicks high) and anode on
BZ_P — i.e. **opposite** the schottky. Therefore ADR-0002's core premise — "two
positions in parallel, identical orientation (pad1=cathode=supply side), swap =
move one SMA part, no track changes" — is **electrically invalid for the TVS
leg**. A correct dual-footprint design would need the TVS pads reversed relative
to the schottky, which the shared-footprint approach cannot express.

(Alternate reading, equally a defect: if the true intent for D3 were to clamp an
*external positive surge arriving on the beeper feed* — BZ_P driven high vs
BEEP_RET — then this orientation WOULD break down correctly, but then ADR-0002's
"de-energize the coil at 7-10V" text describes the wrong mechanism. Either the
orientation is wrong for the stated purpose, or the stated purpose is wrong for
the orientation. Both are documented-intent-vs-physics defects.)

**Why P1, not P0:** D3 is DNP; the shipped board populates only the schottky D2,
which is correct, so THIS board's beeper protection functions as intended.
Furthermore, even if D3 were populated per the (anticipated) "transducer
waveform experiment" swap, the ~5.9V forward clamp is *below* the CMT-8504 6.0V
operating-voltage ceiling and forward-conduction is benign for the TVS — nothing
is damaged; the option simply fails to do its job silently. No hardware harm, no
release block → P1, a latent-option + false-protection-claim defect for
next-rev correction, not an order-stopper.

### P2 — the new E-INV gate codifies the wrong TVS orientation as "correct"

`03_src/rules/electrical_invariants.yaml` asserts `D3.1 net = BZ_P` with the
rationale: *"D3 must share D2's orientation (cathode at BZ_P) so a future
populate-swap cannot invert clamp polarity relative to the documented matrix."*
The gate's own header states its purpose is to catch the class of defect where
"every artifact agreed; only intent-vs-netlist disagreed" (the usb-hub D1
reverse-polarity lesson). Here the E-INV gate does the opposite: it **enforces**
a physically wrong orientation and would pass a mis-oriented TVS as compliant.
This is the exact failure mode the gate was built to prevent, turned inward — a
gate that validates the defect. It should assert the TVS clamp *direction is
correct for the flyback event* (cathode on the kicking node), not merely that D3
matches D2. Couple this fix with the P1 above.

### P2 — (carry-forward, still open) ADR-0001 §3 asserts a false reverse-swap boundary

Re-confirmed against the sealed netlist: net `5V` = {C6.1, C7.1, J1.4, J1.7,
R1.1, TP4.1, U1.8} and net `GND` = {…, U1.4, C6.2, C7.2, D1.4, …}. On a 5V/GND
reversal, U1's substrate diode conducts and C6/C7 reverse **directly**, not
"only C1 through 100R" as ADR-0001 §3 claims (C1 is on 5VF, one R1 hop away).
Logged as T1 in DISPOSITIONS (deferred). v1.1 did not touch this ADR, so the
false electrical fact still stands in a MANDATORY protection ADR. Physical risk
stays low (RJ45 keying, ADR-0004, makes a single-pin field swap implausible);
this is a documentation-accuracy defect, priority-P1 to correct per the
re-grade note, severity-P2 by live risk.

### P2 — (carry-forward) no populated entry clamp on the 5V / beeper pairs; and the reserve does not help even if populated

Only the audio pair (D1) has a populated entry clamp; the 5V and beeper pairs
ride on cable bypass (C7) and the D2 flyback for a 30–35 ft outdoor run (T2,
waived per ADR-0001 §2/§5). New observation compounding P1: the one reserve TVS
on the beeper path (D3) would, if populated, add **no** flyback protection in
its documented role — so the "reserve exists" mitigation is weaker than the docs
imply. Recommend the explicit ORDER_README risk-acceptance line (still absent)
and folding P1 into it.

## Notes / non-findings

- Op-amp gains (A=1.5, B=−1, diff 3.0 V/V), mic bias (~2.9V, inside 1–10V),
  C1 100µF/16V on 5V (3.2× derate), VMID=2.47V vs OPA1678 CM range — all
  re-derived, all with margin. No new rating violation.
- The v1.1 P-ADJ waiver on `BZ_P` (16.1mm flyback loop vs 10mm target) means the
  kick energy the clamp sinks includes ~16mm of loop inductance; within SS14's
  1A/30A-surge headroom, so no rating issue — but it makes a *correct* fast-decay
  clamp (the intended TVS) more valuable, sharpening the sting of P1.

## Verdict

**ORDER.** The shipped board populates the schottky flyback (D2), which I
verified is correctly oriented and correctly rated; every populated protection
chain traces correctly and no populated part exceeds a limit. The headline
defect (P1) lives entirely in a DNP alternate and its documentation/gate, is
benign even if the swap is performed, and cannot be fixed on a sealed board — it
is a next-rev correction (reverse the D3 pads and fix ADR-0002 / the E-INV
assertion), not a reason to withhold ordering these units as-is.
