## A1 (P1, NEW) — a sensor-pod harness in `J_MODE` bypasses the entire safety chain

**Measured.** `fab/bom.csv:45` — **five** connectors share one part, one footprint and
one unkeyed housing:

    SM05B-GHS-TB, "J_DOOR,J_ESTOP,J_MODE,J_RH_AMBIENT,J_RH_EXHAUST",
    JST_GH_SM05B-GHS-TB_1x05-1MP_P1.25mm_Horizontal, C189896

`ORDER_README.md:940-948` (§10) analyses only **three** of them:

> "J_MODE / J_DOOR / J_ESTOP share the same unkeyed 5-pin JST-GH housing and a
> common 3V3(1)/GND(5) convention. **Pinouts are arranged so any single cross-plug
> is fail-safe** […]"

That claim is false, because the two omitted housings carry **active** harnesses with
pull-ups, and the three modelled ones carry only passive dry contacts. Netlist pinouts:

| | pin 1 | pin 2 | pin 3 | pin 4 | pin 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `J_MODE` | 3V3 | `MODE_RAW` | `KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED` | **`COIL_EN`** | GND |
| `J_RH_AMBIENT` | `3V3_SW_RHA` | GND | `SDA_A` | `SCL_A` | `SHIELD_DRAIN` |

An SHT45 pod harness plugged into `J_MODE`: the pod is powered from `J_MODE.1` (3V3),
and its **module SCL pull-up** lands on **`COIL_EN`**. (Pod pull-ups are documented at
`01_docs/DETAIL_DESIGN.md:116` — *"SHT pods carry module 10k pullups"*; BRIEF.md C7
gives 4.7 k for the Adafruit-class module. The finding holds for either value — see the
175 kΩ bound below — and is *stronger* at 4.7 k.)

`COIL_EN = [J_MODE.4, Q_COILDRV.1, R_COILENPD.1]` — held only by **`R_COILENPD` = 100 k**
(`cooksense.tsx:147`). Divider: 3.3 × 100/110 = **3.00 V on the gate of `Q_COILDRV`**.

Chain from there, all from the netlist: `Q_COILDRV` (2N7002) on → `HS_GATE_COIL` pulled
down against `R_HSG` 100 k (needs only 40 µA sunk) → `Q_COIL` (AO3401A, S = `5V_PROTECTED`)
V_GS ≈ −5 V → **`5V_KEY_RELAY` live**.

**What is bypassed:** `MODE_AUTO_HW`, `WD_OK`, `ESTOP_OK`, `TEMP_OK`,
`MCU_RELAY_ENABLE`, `HOST_AUTH`, `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` — all seven — *and* the
Manual/Auto physical rail cut that the brief calls the manual-mode guarantee
(BRIEF.md:88 "MANUAL = OEM membrane operational + relay power physically disabled").

**How weak the defence actually is.** `Q_COIL` needs `HS_GATE_COIL` below ≈4 V, i.e.
`Q_COILDRV` must sink 40 µA — reachable in 2N7002 subthreshold at V_GS ≈ 1.2 V.
Solving 3.3·100k/(100k+R) ≥ 1.2 V gives **R ≤ 175 kΩ**: *any* external pull-up under
175 k on that pin energises the coil rail. The three other field-cable safety inputs
(`R_DOORPD`, `R_ESTOPPD`, `R_MODEPD`) are all **10 k**. The one pin on the same
connector that directly enables the relay rail is held ten times more weakly, and
(per V12-P2-9) has no ESD device and no series resistor either.

Reverse direction is also not fail-safe: a `J_MODE` harness in `J_RH_AMBIENT` shorts
`3V3_SW_RHA` to GND through the mode switch (pole B, pins 1–2) — a switched-rail short
through `Q_SWRHA` with no current limit ahead of it.

**Physical reachability.** `fab/cpl.csv`: `J_MODE` at (196.75, −60.00) east edge,
`J_RH_EXHAUST` at (186.00, −96.75) south edge — 38 mm apart, both field-accessible,
identical connectors, identical cable.

**What would settle it / candidate fixes (not applied):** (a) extend §10 to name all
five housings and retract the fail-safe claim; (b) `R_COILENPD` 100 k → 10 k, which
raises the required external pull-up to ≤ 17 k and takes the SHT pod's 10 k out of
range only marginally — so (c) the real fix is a series element or a different
housing/keying for `J_MODE`, or moving `COIL_EN` off a field connector entirely.

## A2 (P1, NEW) — four AND-chain permissions have no restrictive default

**Measured** (netlist net→node, full membership):

```
WD_OK         -> TP_WDOK.1, U_AND1.3, U_CAND1.1, U_EXP.8, U_FAULTAND.1, U_OENAND.2, U_WD.1
ESTOP_OK      -> TP_ESTOP.1, U_AND1.6, U_CAND1.3, U_EXP.3, U_FAULTAND.3, U_SCHM.4
MODE_AUTO_HW  -> U_AND1.1, U_EXP.2, U_SCHM.8
DOOR_OK       -> U_EXP.4, U_OSCLR.1, U_SCHM.12
```

**No resistor appears on any of the four.** Each is driven only by one push-pull CMOS
output. Consequences:

- A dead, unfitted or tombstoned **`U_SCHM` (SN74HC14, SOIC-14)** floats `ESTOP_OK`,
  `MODE_AUTO_HW` and `DOOR_OK` *simultaneously*. LVC inputs have no bus-hold; the
  floating level is indeterminate and may read HIGH. `U_AND1 = MODE_AUTO_HW · WD_OK ·
  ESTOP_OK` can then be TRUE **with the E-stop mushroom pressed**. The expander
  readbacks (`U_EXP.2/3/4`) sample the same floating net, so software sees the same
  wrong answer — there is no independent cross-check.
- A dead or unfitted **`U_WD` (TPS3823, SOT-23-5)** floats `WD_OK` into five CMOS
  inputs. The board's primary runaway backstop then reads permissive by default.

**This contradicts the source's own claim.** `cooksense.tsx:550-552`, verbatim:

> "CONTEXT WORTH KEEPING: TEMP_OK was the ONLY permission in the safety chain
> actively pulled toward permissive. **The other twelve are pulled restrictive**, and
> REARM_N is correctly pulled up."

Four of them are pulled *nowhere*. The claim was true of the Pi/expander-driven
permissions (`R_HOSTAUTHPD`, `R_MCUENPD`, `R_KRSTPD`, `R_STOPPD`, `R_CTRREQPD`,
`R_RAEN*PD` — all verified present at 100 k) and was generalised to nets it does not
cover.

**The design already knows this rule.** `R_DECUPD` / `R_DECDPD` (100 k) exist for
exactly this case — added at pin review Q4 because "floating CMOS inputs stay
out-of-spec: belt AND braces" (`cooksense.tsx:310-321`). The same reasoning was not
applied to the four *safety permissions* themselves.

**Cost of the fix:** four 0402 pull-downs. **Caught at bring-up?** Partly — ORDER_README
§7 step 2 exercises the watchdog and step 3 reads TP_ESTOP/TP_TEMPOK/TP_FAULT. An
in-service failure (ESD, thermal, solder crack) is not caught.

## A3 (P1, NEW) — the fault latch can be disabled from software, permanently

**Topology** (`cooksense.tsx:157-163`, confirmed in netlist): a classic /S-/R NAND
latch. `U_LATCHA` = NAND(`FAULT_SET_N`, `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR`) → `FAULT`;
`U_LATCHB` = NAND(`REARM_N`, `FAULT`) → `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR`.

```
REARM_N -> R_REARMPU.1, U_EXP.26, U_LATCHB.1
```

`REARM_N` reaches exactly one driver: **MCP23017 GPA5**. There is no button input, no
connector pin, no test point, no jumper. The brief's requirement (BRIEF.md:85-86,
"recovery = data cleared + temps below reset + E-stop released + **explicit manual
re-arm**") is satisfied by a register write from the same Pi the hardware chain exists
to bound (T6 / brief §12 threat model).

**The defeat.** ORDER_README §7 step 3 says *"Pulse REARM_N low"*. Nothing in hardware
enforces a pulse. Held low continuously:

- `/R` asserted → `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` forced **HIGH** regardless of `/S`;
- with a fault active, both `/S` and `/R` are low — the NAND latch's **forbidden state**,
  both outputs HIGH — so `FAULT` and `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` are simultaneously asserted;
- the latch never latches. `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` is permissive at `U_AND3.6` and
  `U_CAND2.3` at all times.

What survives: the *live* terms (`WD_OK`, `ESTOP_OK`, `TEMP_OK`) still gate the rail
and the contactor while the fault is present. What is lost: **memory**. A fault that
clears — a camera cooling by 1 °C, a watchdog restored, an E-stop released —
re-permits cooking with no re-arm. That is precisely what ADR-0011 §2 and the v1.2
fix (TEMP_OK into the latch SET) were for.

**It also defeats the one genuinely elegant property this design has.** At every
power-up `WD_OK` is LOW during the TPS3823 reset delay → `FAULT_SET_N` low → the latch
is *forced* SET, so the coil rail can never come up after any power interruption
without an explicit re-arm. Verified by inspection of the topology; it is a real
strength, and holding `REARM_N` low removes it.

**Not recorded anywhere.** Not in DISPOSITIONS.md, not in the E-INV set
(`electrical_invariants.yaml` asserts only that `R_REARMPU` is a pull-**up**), not in
ORDER_README. **What would settle it:** either a hardware one-shot / edge-detect on
`REARM_N`, or — cheapest — a stated driver invariant plus a bring-up test that holds
`REARM_N` low across an induced fault and demonstrates the rail does *not* return.

## A4 (P1, NEW omission) — the door gates the press, not the machine

**The disposition, verbatim** (`08_reviews/DISPOSITIONS.md`, row V12-P1-2):

> "**deferred — v1.3** (move R_DOORPU to GND, adopt the E-stop harness convention,
> **and gate selectors/contactor on DOOR_OK**)."

**What landed:** the pull inverted (`R_DOORPD` 10 k to GND — confirmed, v1.3, commit
b9dd4a6). **What did not:**

```
DOOR_OK -> U_EXP.4 (readback), U_OSCLR.1 (one-shot clear), U_SCHM.12 (source)
```

`DOOR_OK` appears in **neither** the coil-rail AND-chain (`U_AND1`/`U_AND2`/`U_AND3`)
**nor** the contactor gate (`U_CAND1` = WD_OK·ESTOP_OK·TEMP_OK, `U_CAND2` =
CTR_SAFE·FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR·CONTACTOR_REQ). Nor is it a fault-latch SET term.

**Against the brief** (BRIEF.md:92): *"Door: … open => abort sequence, **release
PRESS+selectors**, no new START."*

| brief clause | as built | |
|---|---|---|
| release PRESS | `OS_CLR_N` low → one-shot cleared → `K_PRESS` opens | ✔ |
| no new START | one-shot held in reset while door open | ✔ |
| release selectors | selectors stay closed; coil rail stays live | ✘ |
| (implied, C9) drop the contactor | **contactor stays energised with the door open** | ✘ |

Residual hazard for the selectors alone is low — a U or D selector presses nothing
without `K_PRESS`. The contactor gap is the real one: the external cooking contactor
is the board's authority over appliance power, and opening the door does not remove
it, does not latch, and leaves recovery entirely to Pi software.

**Ledger defect:** the row is a single line with a compound disposition, and the
compound was half-executed. Nothing records the remaining half. This is the shape of
defect that A5 describes generically.

## A12 (P1 — P0 if any pin map is actually wrong) — the S-VER waiver is refuted by its own cited evidence

This is the finding I would act on first, and it sits exactly where the audit brief
pointed: `LTV-817S-TA1`, one of the parts carrying no S-VER figure citation, on the
isolation barrier of a cooking interlock.

**The gate.** `verification/policy_audit.md:12`:

> `S-VER | WAIVED | weak/missing figure citations: ['2N7002', 'AO3401A', 'GZ2012D601TF', 'KF350-3.5-4P', 'LTV-817S-TA1', 'SN74HC14DR', 'SN74HC595DR', 'ULN2803ADWR'] (9/35)`

**The waiver**, `03_src/cooksense/rules/policy_waivers.yaml:124-141`, verbatim:

> ```
> - id: S-VER
>   refs: ["06_build/verification/pin_review.md"]
>   why: >-
>     The 8 flagged part.yaml entries carry verified: notes without a
>     figure/page citation string, but every one was INDEPENDENTLY re-verified by
>     the fresh-context PIN REVIEW (06_build/verification/pin_review.md,
>     2026-07-23, 5/5 groups, 0 confirmed pin-map defects) — a stronger check
>     than a citation …
>     LTV-817S-TA1 (817-family invariant pinout, vendor-invariant basis stated
>     in the review) …
> ```

**The cited file.** I read it and diffed it:

    $ diff 06_build/verification/pin_review.md \
           07_releases/cooksense-v1.5-2026-07-27/verification/pin_review.md
    (identical)
    $ grep -c "5/5" 06_build/verification/pin_review.md
    0

Its own words (`pin_review.md`, header and "Limits, stated"):

> "**Status: dossiers REGENERATED for v1.3; the narrative group review is CARRIED
> from v1.0 and is NOT re-run.**"
>
> "The v1.0 review was 5 fresh zero-context group agents over ~50 parts and it
> reported `INCOMPLETE — awaiting 4 group reports`. Carrying an INCOMPLETE review
> forward onto a later board would be worse than not carrying it: it would read as
> coverage. **It is therefore NOT shipped as a v1.3 result.**"
>
> "**If a narrative pin review is a release requirement for this board, this release
> does not satisfy it and should not be read as doing so.**"

**So:** the waiver claims *5/5 groups*; the cited artifact says *1 of 5, incomplete,
not carried*. The waiver calls it "a stronger check than a citation"; the artifact
says it "should not be read as" coverage. The two documents cannot both be true, and
the primary source is the one that says the evidence does not exist.

**Aggravating, in order of severity:**

1. **The evidence path is uncommitted.** `refs: ["06_build/verification/pin_review.md"]`
   points into the build directory. `git log -- .../06_build/verification/pin_review.md`
   returns nothing: **never committed**, and CLAUDE.md forbids committing `06_build/`.
   A waiver whose evidence lives only in a regenerable build output is a waiver with no
   auditable evidence, by construction. (It happens to be identical to the sealed copy
   today — but that is luck, and the sealed copy refutes it anyway.)
2. **The argument offered for the opto is a *family* argument.** "817-family invariant
   pinout, vendor-invariant basis" cannot detect the one defect S-VER exists to catch:
   a mirrored land on the **SMD** (`-S`, gull-wing) variant. `part.yaml` names
   `Package_DIP:SMDIP-4_W9.53mm` — a wide-body SMD land derived from the DIP outline —
   so DIP-vs-SMD is precisely the axis a family-invariance claim is blind on.
3. **The archive itself flags this part as the board's highest-risk orientation.**
   ORDER_README:818: *"`U_OPTO` … the isolation part and the board's only 90/270-class
   rotation. **A rotated opto swaps LED and transistor sides across the isolation
   barrier.**"* — with a three-way operator disagreement (0 / 270 / 90) that was only
   recently resolved by re-fitting.
4. **`KF350-3.5-4P` is in the same waived set** — the isolated terminal block, also
   hand-soldered, also unpopulated, also on the barrier.

**This does not prove a pin map is wrong.** It proves the board is *unproven* at the
exact point the brief said to attack, and that the gate reporting it as handled is
resting on a citation that does not say what it is quoted as saying. **What settles
it:** a figure+page citation per part in `part.yaml` — which is what S-VER asks for and
what a narrative review was substituted for. For `LTV-817S-TA1` specifically, the
check is the Lite-On LTV-8X7S datasheet §8 "RECOMMENDED FOOT PRINT PATTERNS" against
the shipped land, plus the pin-1 index, plus which physical row carries pins 1/2.

Per CLAUDE.md: *"Waivers need evidence, not rationale. A waiver copied from another
board is not a judgement — it is an inherited defect."* This one is not copied from
another board; it is copied from a review that declined to make the claim.

3.3 × 1282/1e6 = **4.2 mV ≈ 0.37 K**. ✔ matches the reviewer, not DETAIL_DESIGN.

**Why it matters beyond tidiness.** `cooksense.tsx:520` sends the reader to
DETAIL_DESIGN for the CH0/CH3 conversion difference:

> "They also bound the ADC input, so the host's conversion for CH0/CH3 differs from

## E. E-TOPO — independent estimate of the AMS1117 dropout at the real load

The gate's arithmetic (power_tree.yaml:110): headroom = 4.500 − 3.399 = **1101 mV** vs
`dropout_mv` **1300** → short **199 mV**. Both numbers are correctly cited (ds1117 p.3,
dropout max at IOUT = 0.8 A; p.2 output envelope, full-temperature bold limits).

**My independent read, in three parts.**

**1. The 4.5 V input floor is self-imposed and contradicted by the board's own order
document.** power_tree.yaml:72 sets `vin_min: 4.5` and says raising it to 4.75 "would be
fitting a number to a gate". But ORDER_README §5.2 *mandates* a 5 V ±5 % SELV supply,
and §0 goes further and requires **≥4.85 V at J_PWR under load**. The gate therefore
grades a supply the order document forbids. That is two homes for one fact, and the
gate reads the one nobody is allowed to buy.

**2. The series drop between J_PWR and the LDO is the genuinely unbounded term.**
At a whole-board worst case of ≈0.45 A (0.3 A logic + 1U+1D+PRESS+STOP coils at
10 mA each, `02_parts/DIP05-1A72-12L` 500 Ω/5 V):

| element | estimate | basis |
|---|---|---|
| F1 MF-MSMF200L-2 | ≈34 mV | ~0.075 Ω hot-side estimate |
| Q_REV AO3401A | ≈38 mV | ~60 mΩ max × 1.4 hot |
| U_EFUSE TPS259573 | ≈20 mV | ~45 mΩ hot |
| **total** | **≈92 mV** | **ESTIMATE — no cited max exists in this tree** |

None of the three has a cited worst-case resistance in `02_parts/`. **So the honest
statement is that the ≥4.85 V requirement is written at the wrong node.** `TP_5VP`
exists and probes `5V_PROTECTED` directly: the bring-up instruction should be
**"TP_5VP ≥ 4.70 V at full load"**, which is directly measurable and removes the
unquantified drop from the argument.

**3. Does the rail hold?** With the ORDER_README's own ≥4.85 V floor:
5V_PROTECTED ≈ 4.758 V; headroom vs `vout_max` 3.399 = **1.359 V** vs the guaranteed
1.300 V → **passes by 59 mV, on the fully-stacked worst case** (lowest sanctioned
supply × highest-trimmed part × the 0.8 A dropout figure applied at 0.3 A). So the
mitigation is arithmetically sufficient, not merely reassuring — worth saying,
because the archive does not say it.

At the ±5 % floor (4.75 V → ≈4.658 V) the shortfall is **41 mV, not 199 mV**. And
against the setpoint rather than `vout_max` (4.658 − 3.300 = 1.358 V) it passes.
Three independently conservative choices are stacked; relaxing any one clears it.

**I will not invent the 0.3 A dropout figure.** The physics says an AMS1117's dropout
is a roughly-fixed base-drive term plus an I·R term, so it falls sub-linearly with
current and ~1.0–1.1 V typ at 0.3 A is the plausible range — but that split is my
model, not a datasheet number, and ds1117 p.6 publishes no dropout-vs-load curve. The
file is right that the fact is OWED. **The measurement that settles it** is exactly the
one already specified (VIN−VOUT at U_LDO at IOUT = 0.3 A at bring-up), and it should be
taken at three temperatures, because dropout on a PNP-pass regulator rises as beta
falls at low temperature and the bench will be at 25 °C.

**The finding the archive does not state — the failure mode is fail-safe, and the
thermal interlock is immune.** If the rail *does* sag:

- Every threshold in the thermal window is **ratiometric to `3V3_ANALOG`**: the sense
  divider (`R_REF`/NTC/`R_CLMP`), the trip divider (`R_TH1`/`R_TH2`), the open-detect
  divider (`R_OPENT`/`R_OPENB`), the comparator supply (`U_COMP.8`/`U_COMP2.8`) and the
  ADC VREF (`U_ADC.15`) all sit on the same net. At VCC 3.0 V the open node is
  3.0×22/32 = 2.0625 V and the open threshold 3.0×100/162 = 1.852 V — the whole window
  scales together and stays inside the LMV393's VCC−0.7 ceiling. **The trip temperature
  does not move.**
- Below ≈2.93 V the TPS3823-33 asserts reset → `WD_OK` low → `U_AND1` false → coil rail
  cut → all relays drop. Fail-safe, and it also SETs the fault latch, so recovery
  requires a re-arm (subject to A3).

**Verdict on E-TOPO: the rail almost certainly holds at the mandated supply, the
residual is a paperwork mismatch between power_tree.yaml and ORDER_README, and the
failure direction is safe.** It is correctly not fixed by inventing a number.

## S. Analysed and found sound (so it is not re-spent)

These were attacked and did not yield. Each is stated with the number that closes it.

**S1 — relay contact rating vs load.** DIP05-1A72-12L contact: 10 W, 200 V, 0.5 A
switch / 1.0 A carry (part.yaml `limits`, DS p.1). Load is the OEM keypad matrix scan —
brief §1 measures ~20–100 Ω pressed, µA–mA class. Derating is three orders of
magnitude. The *real* reed risk at this current is dry-circuit contact film, whose
failure mode is **fail-to-close** (a key that does not register), not weld. A welded
`K_PRESS` — which would convert every selector change into an unbounded press with no
detection — is therefore not a credible mode here, and that reasoning, not the rating,
is why.

**S2 — one-hot prevents U–U shorts.** While a selector is energised its keypad line
joins `U_SEL_BUS`. The '238 is one-hot **by construction** (active-HIGH 3-to-8,
`G2A_N=G2B_N=GND`), so exactly one U line is ever on the bus. Two U lines can never be
bridged through it — which matters because a U–U bridge would make the OEM controller
misattribute a *human* keypress in MANUAL. Confirmed for D likewise.

**S3 — brownout: fail-safe, and stale data cannot fire a press.** DIP05 pull-in ≤3.5 V,
drop-out ≥0.75 V. With `U_ULNA` V_CE(sat) ≈0.85 V at 10 mA, coil pull-in needs ≈4.35 V
of input; hold-in persists down to ≈1.6 V. But 3V3 = VIN − dropout crosses the
TPS3823's 2.93 V at VIN ≈ 4.0–4.2 V, so **the supervisor cuts the coil rail ~2.5 V above
the relays' hold-in floor** — no chatter band exists. On recovery `WD_OK` was low during
the dip, so `FAULT_SET_N` went low and the latch SET: the rail cannot return without a
re-arm. And a press cannot re-fire on stale data because the CD74HC221 needs a *rising
edge* on 1B, which a held-high `PRESS_REQ` does not produce. (The one path that does —
3V3 collapsing far enough to randomise the 595 storage register, then `PRESS_REQ`
coming back 0→1 — is V12-P1-4 reached by brownout instead of by power-up, and V12-P1-4's
software mitigation, ORDER_README §6.3, covers only the deliberate power-up sequence,
not an in-service dip where the Pi never reboots. Flagged as an extension of an already
open P1, not as a new finding.)

**S4 — I2C addressing and pull-up sizing.** No collision: I2C1 = MCP23017 0x20 only
(A0/A1/A2 all to GND, verified); bus A = MLX90640 0x33 + SHT45 0x44; bus B likewise, and
the two 0x33 cameras are on physically separate RP1 buses. Pull-up per bus =
2.2 k ‖ 10 k (camera module) ‖ 10 k (pod module) = **1.57 kΩ** → 2.1 mA sink, inside the
3 mA I²C limit. Rise time at ≈200 pF of cable+device = 0.8473 × 1570 × 200p = **266 ns**:
comfortable at the 100 kHz bring-up (1000 ns limit) and **marginal at 400 kHz**
(300 ns limit) — which is exactly why the design gates 400 kHz behind EMI validation.
Brief §3.10's "selectable 2.2k/4.7k, default OFF" is not implemented (fitted 2.2 k) —
already recorded as V12-P1-8/item 9.

**S5 — MAX31856 front end.** `BIAS` (pin 2) tied to `T_N` (pin 3) is the datasheet's
own recommended connection. Differential filter 2×100 Ω + 100 nF = 20 µs; common-mode
10 nF each = 1 µs; **C_diff/C_cm = 10×**, which is the standard ratio for bounding
common-mode-to-differential conversion from cap mismatch. Pin 6 (DNC) correctly left
unconnected. Open/short thermocouple faults are the part's own function and reach the
expander on `TC_FAULT_N` (GPB6) plus `TP_TCFAULT`. Cold junction is internal to the
package; whether `U_TC` is placed close enough to `J_TC` for the junctions to track is a
placement question — see §G.

**S6 — Pi GPIO boot defaults all agree with the board's pulls.** Checked every assigned
pin against the BCM default pull map (GPIO0–8 pull-up, 9–27 pull-down):
`KEY_RESET_N` (GPIO13, down) → SRCLR asserted ✔; `KEY_LATCH` (12, down) ✔;
`KEY_DATA` (16, down) ✔; `HOST_AUTH` (22, down) ✔; `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` (23, down) ✔;
`STOP_REQ` (26, down) ✔; `WD_PET` (17, down) ✔; `TC_CS_N` (7) and `ADC_CS_N` (8) are in
the pull-**up** group, which deasserts both chip selects ✔; `KEY_CLOCK` on GPIO6 is
pulled up but a static level does not clock. No disagreement found.

**S7 — reverse-polarity chain is correct, and the clamp is on the right side of the
fuse.** `Q_REV` AO3401A high-side (D = input, S = load, G = GND) conducts through its
body diode then enhances at V_GS = −5 V forward, and blocks with V_GS = 0 reversed.
`D_REVCLAMP` (SS34) sits on `5V_FUSED`, **downstream** of F1, so its fault current
passes through the polyfuse — the 2026-07-23 pin-review fix is real and verified in the
netlist. `D_ESD_IN` (PESD5V0S1BA) *is* upstream of F1, but it is bidirectional with a
5.0 V standoff and does not conduct at ±5 V, so it does not reproduce the same defect.

**S8 — SPI bus sharing.** `SPI_MISO` is shared by `U_ADC.12` (DOUT) and `U_TC.11` (SDO);
both tri-state on CS high, and the two chip selects are separate Pi GPIOs on the
pull-up-by-default group. MOSI/MISO orientation verified against both pin maps
(MCP3208 pin 11 = DIN ← MOSI, pin 12 = DOUT → MISO ✔). Differing SPI modes (MCP3208
mode 0, MAX31856 mode 1) are a per-transfer driver setting, not a hardware conflict.

**S9 — the STOP path is topologically what the brief asks for.**
`KP_U6 → K_STOP → RSTOP_MID → R_STOP → KP_D1`, i.e. brief §4's "U6-K_STOP-RSTOP-D1",
on an ungated `5V_STOP` rail with a dedicated `Q_STOPDRV` and its own flyback
`D_KSTOP`. `K_STOP` is verified **absent** from `5V_KEY_RELAY`. K_STOP-live-in-MANUAL is
a deliberate, dispositioned trade (V12-P2-9 / ADR-0011 §4): a Pi holding `STOP_REQ`
high jams the OEM STOP key and blocks the manual fallback the brief promises (C10
"OEM panel usable"), but the jam direction is itself non-permissive. Recorded, not
re-raised.

**S10 — flyback on every coil.** Twelve key relays clamp through the ULN2803A's
internal freewheel diodes with `COM` (pin 10) tied to `5V_KEY_RELAY` on **both**
drivers ✔. `K_STOP`, which has no ULN, has its own `D_KSTOP` SS34 ✔. No unclamped
inductive load found on the board. (The **isolated loop has no clamp** — see §G, and
ORDER_README:1044 already states it.)

   4-layer.)

*(A separate datasheet pass over the `LTV-817S-TA1` and `MAX31856MUD+T` pin maps
against their figures was commissioned alongside this review and had not returned at
the time of writing. **It is not substituted for here** — A12 stands on the waiver
contradiction alone, and the pin maps themselves remain unverified against a figure.)*

Two opto-related items belong to this lens and are stated here:

- **`V_ECO`, the limit that is missing from the part dossier.**
  `02_parts/LTV-817S-TA1/part.yaml` `limits` records `vceo: "35V"` and `viso: "5kVrms"`
  but **not `V_ECO`**, the emitter-collector *reverse* rating, which on the LTV-817
  family is **6 V**. ORDER_README:1002 states that a reversed loop "simply never
  conducts (contactor never closes)". That is true for the *function* and wrong for the
  *part*: the same document sanctions up to 30 V on the loop, and 30 V applied in
  reverse across a 6 V absolute maximum is an out-of-spec condition on a field-wireable
  screw terminal, not a benign no-op. The honest wording is that a reversed loop applies
  up to 30 V against a 6 V rating and may destroy the opto — and a destroyed opto's
  failure direction (open or short) is not established anywhere in the tree.
- **The mitigation may not be sufficient for the mitigation.** V12-P1-6's mandated
  workaround is "drive the contactor through an interposing relay/SSR with a
  high-impedance input", and ORDER_README:1037 correctly demands "DESIGN THE FIELD LOOP
  TO NEED ≤ 3.0 mA". Most SSR input stages are LEDs needing ≥5 mA; a ≤3 mA budget rules
  out the common parts, so "interposing relay/SSR" is not by itself an actionable
  instruction. What is needed is a named part or a stated input-current ceiling.
