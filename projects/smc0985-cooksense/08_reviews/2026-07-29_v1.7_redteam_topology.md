### T-01 (P1) — `D_ESD_IN` is the lowest-breakdown clamp on the board and it sits upstream of the fuse

**Evidence.** `cooksense.net`, net `5V_IN`: `D_ESD_IN.1, F1.1, J_PWR.1` — the ESD diode is on the
*connector* side of the polyfuse. Committed `PESD5V0S1BA_2024-04-26.pdf`, Table 6:

```
VRWM  reverse standoff voltage                        max  5     V
VBR   breakdown voltage   IR = 1 mA; Tamb = 25 °C     min  5.5   max 9.5  V
Rdiff differential resistance IR = 1 mA               max  50    Ω
```

Table 5 rates it for `PPPM 130 W, tp = 8/20 µs`, **non-repetitive** — there is no DC power rating
in the document.

Now the three clamps, ordered by the voltage at which each first conducts:

| element | node | first conducts at | fuse in series? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `D_ESD_IN` PESD5V0S1BA | `5V_IN` | **5.5 V** (V_BR min) | **no — upstream of F1** |
| `D_TVS` SMBJ5.0A | `5V_PROTECTED` | 6.40 V (V_BR min, 25 °C) | yes, and behind the eFuse |
| `U_EFUSE` OVLO cutoff | `5V_RPP` | 5.378 V earliest … **6.231 V latest** (re-derived below) | — |

**Consequence.** For any sustained input overvoltage between 5.5 V and the eFuse's actual trip
(worst case 6.231 V), the SOD-323 is the *first and only* conducting clamp. Worse, the eFuse doing
its job makes this strictly worse: on a wrong 9 V or 12 V adapter — the exact scenario the OV cutoff
exists for — the eFuse **opens**, which disconnects the 600 W SMBJ5.0A entirely, leaving a single
SOD-323 as the only part across the input, with the polyfuse *not* in its current path.

Arithmetic with the datasheet's own R_diff 50 Ω max:

| input | I through D_ESD_IN | dissipation |
|---|---|---|
| 6.2 V | (6.2−5.5)/50 = 14 mA | 87 mW |
| 9 V | 70 mA | **630 mW** |
| 12 V | 130 mA | **1.56 W** |

A SOD-323 with no published DC rating does not survive the last two. If it fails short it presents
a dead short across the SELV input **with F1 not in series**.

**Why it matters.** This is the identical defect class ADR-0021 closed as a P0 for the SMBJ — "a
600 W TRANSIENT part asked to be a DC regulator" — reproduced one component earlier and outside the
fuse, where the ADR's own remedy cannot reach. The board's own rule file states the principle it
violates, `03_src/cooksense/rules/electrical_invariants.yaml:40`:

> "the reverse fault current path supply->J_PWR->F1->clamp->GND must pass THROUGH F1 so the polyfuse
> trips; **on 5V_IN (upstream) the clamp current bypassed the fuse and was bounded only by the supply**"

That reasoning was applied to `D_REVCLAMP` and got the right answer. `D_ESD_IN` sits in exactly the
position the same sentence declares unacceptable. Nothing could have caught it, either:
`02_parts/PESD5V0S1BA/part.yaml` carries **no `limits:` block at all** — the dossier for the one
part directly on the input connector states no stand-off, no breakdown, no clamping voltage and no
power rating.

**Fix.** Move `D_ESD_IN` to `5V_FUSED` (F1 is 70 mΩ max — the ESD path loses essentially nothing),
and/or fit a stand-off above the eFuse's latest trip (a 6.8 V-class or higher bidirectional part).
Add a `limits:` block citing V_RWM / V_BR / V_CL so this is machine-checkable next time.

### T-02 (P1) — the 3V3 rail's E-TOPO pass omits 0.45 A of its own declared downstream load

**Evidence.** From the netlist, net `3V3` carries `FB1.1` (→ `3V3_ANALOG`) and the source pins
`Q_SWA.2, Q_SWB.2, Q_SWRHA.2, Q_SWRHE.2` (→ the four switched sensor rails). Every one of those five
rails is fed **from 3V3**. `power_tree.yaml` then declares:

```
rails:  - name: 3V3   iout_max_A: 0.3   # logic: 595/238/HC14/1G-family/MCP23017/watchdog/one-shot
linear_rails:
  - 3V3_ANALOG                iout_max_A: 0.05   vin_min: 3.201  # = the 3V3 rail's cited output envelope
  - 3V3_SW_A / _B / _RHA / _RHE  iout_max_A: 0.1  (each)  vin_min: 3.201  # = the 3V3 rail's ...
```

Declared load on 3V3 = 0.30 + 0.05 + 4 × 0.10 = **0.75 A**, not 0.30 A.

Re-run both graded terms with the file's own numbers:

**Dissipation.** ds1117 Note 2 (verified in the cited PDF): *"Line and load regulation are guaranteed
up to the maximum power dissipation of 1.2 W for SOT-223"*.

```
PD = (Vin_max − Vout_min) × Iout = (5.250 − 3.201) × 0.75 = 1.537 W   vs 1.200 W  →  128 %  FAIL
```
(the file computes 615 mW / 51 % at 0.30 A.)

**Dropout.** Series chain, the file's own cited maxima: F1 70.0 mΩ (R1Max) + Q_REV 73.5 mΩ +
eFuse 47.0 mΩ = 190.5 mΩ. Board current at the declared envelope = 0.75 (3V3 total) + 0.15 (coil
rail) + 0.02 (5V_STOP) + 0.0013 (TVS leakage) = 0.921 A.

```
drop     = 0.921 × 0.1905 = 175.5 mV
vin_min  = 4.850 − 0.1755 = 4.6745 V
headroom = 4.6745 − 3.399 = 1.2755 V   vs dropout 1.300 V  →  FAIL by 24 mV
```
(the file computes +55 mV at 0.50 A.)

Additional, smaller: the cited output envelope 3.201/3.300/3.399 V is specified at **VIN = 4.8 V**
(ds1117 p.2, verified) — 46 mV *above* the derived worst-case input, so the 3.201 V floor the whole
dropout check leans on is not specified at the corner the board operates at.

**Why it matters.** The board will most likely work: an MLX90640 draws ~25 mA and an SHT45 ~0.4 mA,
so the real switched-rail total is nearer 60 mA than 400 mA. But **there is no cited sensor-current
figure anywhere in the tree**, and the E-TOPO PASS is obtained by excluding 60 % of the rail's own
declared load. A 55 mV margin produced that way is not a margin. Either cite the four sensor loads
down to their real values (with a source), or the AMS1117 fails its own budget on this board's own
declared numbers. This one is free to fix — it is arithmetic and citations, not copper.

### T-03 (P1) — the isolated contactor output cannot deliver its declared current, and is unclamped

**Evidence.** `CONTACTOR_DRV: R_OPTOLED.1, U_CAND2.4(Y)`; `OPTO_LED_A: R_OPTOLED.2, U_OPTO.1(ANODE)`;
`U_OPTO.2(CATHODE) → GND`. `R_OPTOLED` = **330 Ω** (bom.csv). Driver is an SN74LVC1G11 on 3V3.
`02_parts/LTV-817S-TA1/part.yaml` limits: **`ctr: 50-600% (TA1 bin)`**, `vceo: 35V`, no PDF in tree.

LED current, worst case (VOH ≈ Vcc−0.3 at ~6 mA, Vcc_min 3.201 V, V_F max 1.3 V, R +1 %):

```
If = (3.201 − 0.3 − 1.3) / 333 = 4.80 mA
Ic(guaranteed) = 4.80 mA × CTR_min 0.50 = 2.4 mA
```

and the CTR bin is specified at If = 20 mA, where the LTV-817 CTR-vs-If curve derates roughly
0.5–0.7× at ~5 mA → **usable Ic ≈ 1.2–1.7 mA**.

Declared capability (brief §3, `nets.yaml:89`, `cooksense.tsx:858`): *"≤30 V / 50 mA dry contact"*.
Short by **20–40×**. Nothing in `03_src/` or `02_parts/` derives the LED current or the CTR anywhere;
`nets.yaml:89` says only "the opto collector is the limit", which is true and unquantified.

**Second half — no clamp on an inductive output.** `CONTACTOR_C: J_ISOLOOP.1, U_OPTO.4` and
`CONTACTOR_E: J_ISOLOOP.4, U_OPTO.3`. No TVS, no snubber, no series resistor. The load is by name a
**contactor** loop — inductive — and V_CEO is 35 V against a 30 V working loop: 5 V of margin against
a kick of unbounded magnitude. The first coil release can destroy the only device on the isolation
barrier, and it fails in the direction where the loop is either permanently open (loses the contactor)
or permanently shorted (loses the interlock).

**Fix.** Drop `R_OPTOLED` to ~150–180 Ω (the 1G11 sources 24 mA) and publish the guaranteed Ic;
fit a clamp across `CONTACTOR_C`/`CONTACTOR_E`; or restate the output in the ORDER_README as
"≤30 V, ≤2 mA, resistive only — drive an interposing relay's opto/PLC input, never a coil".

### T-04 (P1) — the Ioff buffers and series resistors on the Pi link do not exist, and a doc asserts them

**Evidence.** Brief §3 verbatim: *"Ioff-capable buffers (no back-powering); 22-100R series"*.
`ARCHITECTURE.md`, Power: *"Pi header 5V/3V3 are NC/sense only; **no backfeed either direction
(Ioff buffers)**"*.

The BOM contains **no buffer of any kind** — no '245, '125, '126, LVC1T45, nothing. In the netlist,
of the ~20 signals on `J_PI` only `LC_DAT_PI` and `LC_CLK_PI` carry series resistors (33 Ω,
`R_LCDAT`/`R_LCCLK`). `HOST_AUTH`, `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE`, `STOP_REQ`, `KEY_CLOCK`, `KEY_DATA`,
`KEY_LATCH`, `KEY_RESET_N`, `WD_PET`, `INT_ALERT`, `TC_DRDY_N`, `SPI_MOSI/MISO/SCLK`, `ADC_CS_N`,
`TC_CS_N`, `I2C_SDA/SCL`, `SDA_A/SCL_A/SDA_B/SCL_B` have none.

**Consequence.** cooksense is powered from its own 5 V SELV input, so "board up, Pi down" is a
*normal* bench and bring-up state (it is the G4 gate's state). These board-driven push-pull nets land
directly on Pi 5 GPIOs with nothing in series:

- `INT_ALERT` ← MCP23017 INTA (push-pull at POR: ODR = 0)
- `TC_DRDY_N` ← MAX31856
- `SPI_MISO` ← MCP3208 DOUT + MAX31856 SDO
- four 2.2 kΩ pull-ups (`R_SDAA/SCLA/SDAB/SCLB`) onto GPIO4/5/14/15

each of which injects current into the Pi's GPIO ESD clamps — precisely brief C13
(*"Ioff buffers; Pi rails not sources; relay rail cannot be Pi-powered; verify no phantom rail"*).

The design demonstrably understands the mechanism: `electrical_invariants.yaml:521` chooses a
pull-*down* on `WD_PET` specifically because *"a pull-UP to this board's 3V3 would source current
into an UNPOWERED Pi through its GPIO ESD clamp"*. That care was applied to a 1 kΩ resistor and not
to four push-pull drivers.

**Fix.** 100 Ω in series on every board→Pi output at minimum. And if the buffers are being knowingly
dropped, `ARCHITECTURE.md` must stop claiming a protection element that is not on the BOM — a
waiver needs evidence, and this one is a sentence.

### T-05 (P1) — the watchdog is 5× slower than the commissioned window, undocumented as a deviation

**Evidence.** Brief §3 verbatim: *"Watchdog 300-500ms; heartbeat loss drops relay power"*.
`02_parts/TPS3823-33DBVR/part.yaml`: **`t_watchdog: 0.9 / 1.6 / 2.5 s (FIXED WD time-out, TPS3823;
Sec.6.8 p.8)`** — fixed, not settable.

`cooksense.tsx:320` and `electrical_invariants.yaml:130` both call it "the coarse ~1.6 s WD" and
redirect to the one-shot for the fast path. That redirect is only partly valid: the CD74HC221 bounds
the **PRESS pulse** (286–436 ms) and nothing else. It does not bound the selectors, and — the real
exposure — it does not bound `CONTACTOR_DRV`. After a Pi death, `CONTACTOR_REQ` remains latched in
the MCP23017 (whose RESET̄ is `WD_OK`, per ADR-0020) and `U_CAND1/U_CAND2` hold the external cooking
contactor **energised for up to 2.5 s** worst case. Brief C10 requires the watchdog to drop it.

I found no ADR that waives 300–500 ms. ADR-0011 §8 and ADR-0020 both discuss the WDI pull-down and
the expander reset; neither reconciles the time-out against the commissioned window.

**Fix.** Either an ADR that accepts 2.5 s with the hazard argument written out (the honest option —
the OEM controller survives any keypad input, and 2.5 s of a closed selector presses nothing), or a
settable-window supervisor in the same SOT-23-5/6 budget.

### T-06 (P1) — the door input is unsupervised, and the fitted receiver could not resolve an EOL

**Evidence.** Brief §3: *"Door: external NC reed + **EOL** (or 3-wire Hall), 4-pin connector"*.
Netlist: `DOOR_RAW: D_DOOR.1, J_DOOR.2, J_DOOR.4, R_DOORPD.1, U_SCHM.11(5A)`.

Two problems, both structural:

1. **`J_DOOR` pins 2 and 4 are the same net on the board.** No series end-of-line loop can be wired
   through this connector — the return path is shorted to the sense pin.
2. **The receiver is one Schmitt threshold** (`SN74HC14` 5A→5Y→6A→6Y, a non-inverting double
   inversion), not a window comparator. A supervised loop needs three distinguishable states
   (open / EOL / short); one threshold gives two. And at Vcc = 3.3 V the SN74HC14's threshold band
   is not even a characterised datasheet column (the part is tabulated at 2.0 / 4.5 / 6.0 V), so a
   mid-scale EOL level would sit in the indeterminate region by interpolation.

**Consequence.** A short across the door harness reads DOOR-CLOSED with no detection — and
`J_DOOR` pins 1 (3V3) and 2 (`DOOR_RAW`) are **adjacent contacts on a 1.25 mm JST-GH housing**, in a
steam-carrying cooking appliance. The same shape applies to `J_ESTOP` pins 1↔2 → `ESTOP_OK`
permanently true. Supervision is the named mitigation for exactly this failure and it was dropped.

**In the board's favour, and verified:** the *default* is restrictive. `R_DOORPD`, `R_ESTOPPD` and
`R_MODEPD` are 10 kΩ to GND, so unplugged/broken-wire reads DOOR-OPEN / E-STOPPED / MANUAL. A
floated input is safe here; only an active short is permissive, which is why this is P1 and not P0.

Also unimplemented from the same brief sentence: brief §3 asks the door for a 4-pin connector and the
E-stop for a *"4-pin locking connector"*; both are 5-pin SM05B-GHS-TB.

### T-07 (P1) — no ESD and no series damping on the I2C lines that reach the Pi 5 SoC

**Evidence.** Brief §3: *"Per bus: selectable 2.2k/4.7k pullups (default OFF with Adafruit onboard
4.7k); **22-33R damping; low-C ESD**; test points; bus-stuck recovery"*.

Netlist: `SDA_A: J_PI.7, J_RH_AMBIENT.3, J_THERM_A.3, R_SDAA.1` — external connector to Pi header pin
with **nothing in between**. Identical for `SCL_A`, `SDA_B`, `SCL_B`. The only ESD parts on the whole
board are `D_ESD_IN`, `D_DOOR`, `D_ESTOP`, `D_COILEN`, `D_LCDAT`, `D_LCCLK`.

**Consequence.** An ESD strike on a thermal-head or humidity-pod cable lands on an RP1/SoC pin. The
victim of the omission is the host, not this board — and the host is a Raspberry Pi 5 whose GPIO has
no user-serviceable protection.

Also from the same sentence: the pull-ups are **fixed 2.2 kΩ**, not selectable, not default-OFF, with
no 4.7 kΩ alternative — which also contradicts contingency C7 (*"Board A pullups DNP"*). The 2.2 kΩ
against an Adafruit 4407's onboard pull-up and an SHT45 pod's gives ~1.6 kΩ effective (≈2.1 mA sink,
inside I²C spec but strong), and there is no way to weaken it except desoldering.

Uncovered by the same omission, ranked lower because they sit behind series resistance: the six
thermistor lines that leave the board (`TH_CAM_A/B`, `TH_MOUNT_A/B`, `TH_PORT_A/B`, 1 kΩ each —
brief §3 asks for "per channel … + ESD"), and both thermocouple legs (`TC_POS_IN`/`TC_NEG_IN`,
100 Ω each, against a MAX31856 input abs max of AGND−0.3 … AVDD+0.3, i.e. ~3.6 V, on an unshielded
lead that runs into an appliance exhaust).

### T-08 (P2) — `DETAIL_DESIGN` §1 and the LMV393 dossier are both stale; the tsx is the only correct copy

`DETAIL_DESIGN.md` §1 states the sensing chain as *"3V3_ANALOG → 10k (R_REF0/R_REF3) → TH_CAM node →
external NTC → GND"*, tabulates a 25 °C node of **1.650 V**, and gives the default trip as
**74.9 °C** with a solder-select table (8.2k→81.0 / 10k→74.9 / 12k→69.4 / 15k→63.0 °C).
**`R_CLMPA` / `R_CLMPB` — 22 kΩ from `TH_CAM_A`/`TH_CAM_B` to GND — do not appear in that document
at all.** They are in the netlist and the BOM (`22kΩ,"R_CLMPA,R_CLMPB",R_0402_1005Metric,
0402WGF2202TCE,C25768`), and they change every number:

```
V_TH   = 3.3 × 10/78 = 0.42308 V
R_b    = 10k × 0.42308/(3.3−0.42308) = 1470.6 Ω          (= R_NTC ‖ 22k, not R_NTC)
R_NTC  = (1/1470.6 − 1/22000)^-1 = 1575.9 Ω
ln(0.15759)/3987 = −4.63443e−4 ;  1/T = 3.354016e−3 − 4.63443e−4 = 2.890573e−3
T      = 345.95 K = 72.80 °C          (document: 74.9 °C)
```

and the whole table shifts: 8.2k → 79.3 °C, 12k → 67.1 °C, 15k → 60.0 °C. The 25 °C node is
3.3 × (10k‖22k)/(10k + 10k‖22k) = **1.344 V**, not 1.650 V — which also means the host's ADC
conversion for CH0/CH3 differs from the other six channels, as the tsx notes and DETAIL_DESIGN
does not. 72.80 °C is still inside the brief's 70 (stop) / 75 (hard) band, so this is a documentation
defect and not a functional one — **and `cooksense.tsx` already carries the correct 72.80 °C**
(the "RESCALE" comment block). DETAIL_DESIGN was simply never updated.

Same mechanism in the hysteresis paragraph: DETAIL_DESIGN computes *"R_HYS 1M against the 10k source
impedance ≈ 33mV at the node ≈ ~2C"*. At the trip point the node impedance is 10k ‖ 1.4707k =
1.28 kΩ, so 3.2 V/1 MΩ × 1.28 kΩ = **4.1 mV ≈ 0.35 °C** — 8× less than documented. Harmless (the
fault latch catches the first trip), but the number is wrong.

`02_parts/LMV393IDR/part.yaml` is worse than stale — it is actively misleading. It states
`R_OPENT/R_OPENB (6.2k/100k)`, `TCAM_OPEN 3.107V`, concludes the open-detect's effective trip point
is *"about +1.6C (worst case) to −1.6C (typ)"* and instructs:

> *"Disposition this in an ADR or move the open-detect to a rail-to-rail-input comparator
> (e.g. TLV3201/TLV7011 class) **before seal**."*

The board ships `R_OPENT` = **62 kΩ** (`0402WGF6202TCE`, C37825) → `TCAM_OPEN` = 2.037 V, and
`R_CLMPA` bounds the open-circuit node to 2.269 V — both comfortably inside VICR (see Refutations).
The dossier's blocker is already closed, it never mentions `R_CLMPA`, and a reviewer who trusts
`02_parts` over the tsx reaches the wrong conclusion twice: first that the board is broken, then
possibly that `R_OPENT` should go back to 6.2 kΩ.

### T-09 (P2) — the declared 2.0 A rail budget exceeds the eFuse current limit, which is nowhere derived

`R_ILM` = 1.2 kΩ. SLVSE57C EC table, read directly:

```
ILIMIT  RILM = 487 Ω    3.87  4.17  4.42  A
        RILM = 1780 Ω   1.09  1.17  1.24  A
        RILM = 4420 Ω   0.46  0.49  0.52  A
```

The I·R product runs 1885–2166 A·Ω across those rows, so at 1.2 kΩ:
**I_LIMIT ≈ 1.58 A min / 1.71 A typ / 1.79 A max.**

`power_tree.yaml` declares `5V_PROTECTED … iout_max_A: 2.0` (*"whole-board worst"*) and
`nets.yaml` PWR_IN says `current: "2A worst-case"` / *"~1-2A worst"*. The rail cannot pass its
declared budget — the eFuse limits first, at ~80 % of it. Not operationally fatal (real load is
0.5–0.9 A), but note what is missing: **`power_tree.yaml` never states the current-limit setpoint at
all.** The one protection number that bounds every downstream rail is absent from the file whose job
is to hold the power tree, while the OVLO setpoint beside it gets fourteen lines of worst-case
derivation. Setting `R_ILM` deserves the same treatment as setting `R_OVB` — ADR-0021's own lesson.

### T-10 (P2) — eFuse/TVS ordering is 51 mV at −20 °C and inverts below it

Re-derived from SLVSE57C (`VOVLO(R) 1.13 / 1.2 / 1.27 V`, `VOVLO(F) 1.03 / 1.1 / 1.17 V`) with
±0.5 % legs and ±100 ppm/°C over −20…+70 °C (±0.45 %):

```
ratio_max = 26.348 / (99.05 + 26.348)  = 0.210114  →  earliest trip = 1.13/0.210114 = 5.378 V
ratio_min = 25.842 / (100.95 + 25.842) = 0.203815  →  latest trip   = 1.27/0.203815 = 6.231 V
```

Both agree with the dossier's 5.3682 / 6.2394 within the I_EN term (±2.07 mV of threshold error on a
20.698 kΩ source). **The ADR-0021 arithmetic checks out.** Three consequences the ADR states less
completely:

- **Nuisance-trip margin** = 5.378 − 5.250 = **128 mV (2.4 %)**, on a supply the buyer must hold to
  ±3 % *measured at the connector under load*. Thin, but positive, and correctly signed.
- **Cold ordering.** Against SMBJ5.0A V_BR min the margin is 169 mV at 25 °C, but the dossier's own
  TC (+0.041 %/°C) puts V_BR min at 6.2819 V at −20 °C → **51 mV**, and at −40 °C at 6.0975 V, which
  is **below** the 6.231 V latest trip: outside −20 °C the ordering inverts and the TVS conducts
  before the eFuse cuts off. The board is bounded to −20 °C only by the DIP05 relay's
  `t_op: -20..+70C`. That relay spec is therefore load-bearing for protection ordering and should be
  stated as an operating limit in the ORDER_README, not left buried in a relay dossier.
- **OV re-enable inside the legal window.** `VOVLO(F)` min 1.03 V ÷ 0.205342 = **5.016 V**. After an
  OV event, a supply anywhere in 5.02–5.25 V — i.e. *in specification* — may leave the board latched
  off until input power is physically removed. Fail-safe (a dead board opens every contact), but the
  buyer must be told, because the symptom is "board bricked at a legal voltage".

### T-13 (P2) — the 595 storage register is never cleared in hardware

`KEY_RESET_N: J_PI.33, R_KRSTPD.1 (100k→GND), U_SR1.10(SRCLR_N)`. On a 74HC595, SRCLR̄ clears the
**shift** register only; the **storage (output) register** is unaffected and has no defined power-up
state. `SR_OE_N` = NOT(`MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` · `WD_OK`) via `U_OENAND`, so the instant the Pi raises
`MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` with `WD_OK` high, whatever is in the storage register — random at power-up, stale
after a watchdog trip — drives `DECU_A/B/C`, `DECD_A/B`, both `*_G1_RAW` enables and `PRESS_REQ`.
`MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` gates `SR_OE_N` **and** sits in the AND chain, from the same Pi pin, so there is no
hardware ordering to exploit.

Brief §3 requires: *"a normal watchdog pulse must not re-energize stale relay data"*. What actually
saves it is the fault latch, not the reset: `R_FSETNPD` / `R_FLCPD` put the latch in FAULT at power-up
and at every WD/TEMP/ESTOP event, so `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` = 0 and the coil rail is dead until an
explicit re-arm. The requirement is therefore met by **procedure**: the driver MUST clock zeros and
pulse RCLK *before* raising `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE`. Nothing in hardware enforces that, and nothing I was
given states it as a bring-up step. Either write it into the ORDER_README as a hard step, or fix it in
hardware (gate `SR_OE_N` with `FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR`, or derive one RCLK pulse from the re-arm one-shot).

### T-14 (P2) — six HC inputs float by default; a floating `PRESS_REQ` can swallow the Pi's latch pulses

In the default power-up state `SR_OE_N` is high (`R_OE` 10 kΩ to 3V3), so the 595's QA–QH are Hi-Z and
six nets are driven by nothing: `DECU_A`, `DECU_B`, `DECU_C`, `DECD_A`, `DECD_B`, `PRESS_REQ`.

The two decoder **enables** were given pull-downs (`R_DECUPD`, `R_DECDPD`) and the tsx comment
explicitly accepts *"the (also floating) address pins"* on the grounds that a pulled-low E3 disables
all eight outputs. That reasoning is correct and closes the phantom-select hazard. Two things it does
not close:

1. Floating inputs on Nexperia 74HC238 and on the CD74HC221 are a datasheet violation in their own
   right — elevated I_CC and possible oscillation, on a board whose 3V3 budget is already in question
   (T-02).
2. **`U_ONESHOT.2` (B1) = `PRESS_REQ`, floating, can trigger the one-shot.** `A1_N` is tied to GND, so
   any rising edge on B1 fires it. `PRESS_TIMED` then goes high for 286–436 ms, and `PRESS_TIMED_N`
   gates `U_LATCHG` — i.e. **RCLK is blocked**. A drifting or oscillating B1 keeps RCLK gated most of
   the time, so the Pi's `KEY_LATCH` pulses are silently lost during startup with no diagnostic. The
   coil rail is dead in this state (all seven AND terms cannot be true before re-arm), so this is a
   functional trap, not a hazard.

Cost of the fix: one 100 kΩ pull-down on `PRESS_REQ`, and five more on the address lines to make the
'238 inputs legal.

### T-15 (P2) — the documented STOP break-before-make is not implementable

`DETAIL_DESIGN` §3: *"raise STOP_REQ → **PRESS one-shot cleared, both decoders disabled** (U/D coils
drop) → wait ≥1ms relay release → **K_STOP closed**"*, with the bold steps presented as
hardware-enforced.

In the netlist `STOP_REQ` drives, in parallel and with no delay element between them:
`U_STOPINV.1/.2` (→ `STOP_REQ_N` → `U_DECUEN.3`, `U_DECDEN.3`, `U_OSCLR.3`) **and `Q_STOPDRV.1`, the
K_STOP gate, directly**. The Pi cannot insert the ≥1 ms, because K_STOP's driver hangs off the same
net that disables the decoders.

In practice reed release (~0.2–0.5 ms) beats reed operate (~0.5–1 ms), so the overlap is probably
benign, and a scanned matrix seeing two closures for <1 ms is ghosting, not a hazard. But the sequence
as written cannot be executed, and it should not be documented as enforced. Either add an RC delay to
`Q_STOPDRV`'s gate or rewrite the paragraph to say the three actions are simultaneous and why that is
acceptable.

### T-16 (P2) — the gated coil rail has no host-readable sense

Brief C13 requires *"verify no phantom rail"*; brief §3 requires *"power-good"*. The MCP23017 is
completely allocated:

- GPA0–7: `RAIL_EN_A/B/RHA/RHE`, `CONTACTOR_REQ`, `REARM_N`, `BOARD_ID0/1`
- GPB0–7: `EFUSE_FLT_N`, `MODE_AUTO_HW`, `ESTOP_OK`, `DOOR_OK`, `TEMP_OK`, `FAULT`, `TC_FAULT_N`, `WD_OK`

There is **no readback of `COIL_EN`, `5V_KEY_RELAY` or `5V_STOP`**. `TP_5VKR` is a bench pad only.
So the single property the whole AND chain exists to produce — coil power actually removed — is the
one property no field or in-service test can confirm. The expander is full, so the gap is structural,
not an oversight of a spare pin.

Related single-fault note: `J_MODE` carries `KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED` out on pin 3 and `COIL_EN_IN` back on
pin 4. A harness fault bridging pin 1 (3V3) to pin 4 energises the coil rail with all seven AND terms
bypassed, and no readback on the board would reveal it. (Adjacent-pin shorts are *safe* — see
Refutations; this needs a two-wire fault or a mis-built harness.)

## Refutations — hypotheses I tested and could NOT confirm

A refutation is a result. Each of these was a candidate defect that the netlist disproved.

1. **Reverse-polarity P-FET is backwards.** It is not. `Q_REV` drain = `5V_FUSED` (input side),
   source = `5V_RPP` (load side), gate = GND. The P-channel body diode (anode at drain) conducts
   input→load, V_GS = −V_rail turns the channel fully on, and under reversal V_S floats so V_GS → 0
   and both channel and body diode block. This is the correct orientation, not the common swap.
2. **Reverse crowbar upstream of the fuse.** `D_REVCLAMP` cathode on `5V_FUSED`, anode on GND —
   *downstream* of F1, so reverse fault current passes through the polyfuse. Correct, and it is
   exactly why T-01 is a finding.
3. **TVS directionality / bidirectional part fitted.** `D_TVS.1(K)` on `5V_PROTECTED`, `.2(A)` on GND,
   unidirectional `SMBJ5.0A` (not the `CA` sibling). Correct.
4. **Missing coil flyback on 11 relays.** There is no discrete diode, and none is needed:
   TBD62083AFWG carries internal OUT→COM clamp diodes (verified from the p.2 equivalent circuit in
   its dossier, not from the pin name), and **both** COM pins (`U_ULNA.10`, `U_ULNB.10`) are on
   `5V_KEY_RELAY` — the *switched* rail — so a gated-off rail still clamps and the kick does not
   return into a rail the safety chain just deliberately killed. `K_STOP` has an explicit `D_KSTOP`
   (SS34) cathode to `5V_STOP`. Correct in both cases.
5. **The open-thermistor detector is inert (its own dossier says so).** It is not. `R_CLMPA/B` (22 kΩ)
   bound the open-circuit node to 3.3 × 22/32 = **2.269 V** (worst-case 2.283 V) and `R_OPENT` 62 kΩ
   puts the threshold at 3.3 × 100/162 = **2.037 V** — both below the LMV393 VICR ceiling of
   VCC−0.7 = 2.501 V at the worst-case rail, with 218 mV to spare. The comparator therefore does a
   *normal* compare, not the out-of-common-mode case table. Nuisance trip for a *healthy* thermistor
   is at R_NTC = (1/16.13k − 1/22k)⁻¹ = 60.5 kΩ ≙ **−10.4 °C**, not the +1.6 °C the dossier claims.
   Because both dividers reference `3V3_ANALOG`, the margin is ratiometric and rail-independent.
6. **A cold room locks the board out.** Refuted by the same arithmetic — the lockout is at −10.4 °C,
   not the +14.7 °C I first computed by (wrongly) ignoring `R_CLMPA`.
7. **Open/unplugged camera thermistor reads "cold" = permissive.** No longer true.
   `U_COMP2`'s two outputs wired-OR onto `TEMP_OK` (four open-drain outputs, one 10 kΩ pull-up), so an
   open, shorted or unplugged head drives `TEMP_OK` low → `U_FAULTAND` sets the fault latch → coil rail
   dead and contactor permission removed. Both cable failure modes are covered in hardware.
8. **Two selectors can close at once.** They cannot. Both `SN74HC238`s have G2A̅/G2B̅ hard-grounded and
   G1 gated, so exactly one output is high when enabled; and `U_DECU` addresses 6/7 (Y6/Y7 unconnected)
   give a genuine "no U selected" state. One-hot is by construction, for any 595 pattern.
9. **An adjacent-pin short on `J_MODE` defeats the Manual rail cut.** It does not. Pole A's physical cut
   sits in series with `KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED`, which *already contains* `MODE_AUTO_HW` from pole B — so a
   3↔4 bridge merely closes the AUTO throw while `MODE_AUTO_HW` remains false in Manual and the AND
   chain still holds the rail off. Genuinely redundant, and the redundancy is in the netlist.
10. **`J_ISOLOOP` pins 2/3 shorted defeats the E-stop's second pole.** It does not. Field wiring is
    1 = opto collector → E-stop pole B in, 2 = pole B out, 3 = contactor feed, 4 = opto emitter, with
    2/3 deliberately two screws on one net *"so a single loosening screw must not be able to drop both
    the E-stop return AND the contactor feed"*. Coherent and documented.
11. **E-OFF is asserted, not real.** It is real. `source_type: external_5v_selv`, no battery / cell /
    pack / supercap anywhere in the BOM (CE1 is a 220 µF electrolytic), and stored energy is µJ of bulk
    plus coil inductance that collapses with input. More importantly the declared off-control is a
    **genuine series element in the netlist, not a claim**: `J_MODE` pin 3 (`KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED`) →
    external DPDT pole A → pin 4 (`COIL_EN_IN`), with `R_COILENPD` 680 Ω pulling `COIL_EN_IN` low the
    moment the switch opens or the connector is unplugged, and `R_COILENS` 680 Ω keeping the connector
    pin off the FET gate. Unplug `J_PWR` and everything is dead; select MANUAL and the coil rail is
    physically cut. **E-OFF PASSES.**
12. **A permissive default somewhere in the safety chain.** I traced all seven AND terms, the
    fault latch, the contactor gate and every external input, and found none:
    - `KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED` = MODE_AUTO_HW · WD_OK · ESTOP_OK (`U_AND1`) · TEMP_OK · MCU_RELAY_ENABLE ·
      HOST_AUTH (`U_AND2`) · FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR (`U_AND3`) — all seven brief terms present.
    - Fault latch: cross-coupled `SN74LVC1G00` (`U_LATCHA`/`U_LATCHB`); `FAULT_SET_N` =
      WD_OK · ESTOP_OK · TEMP_OK (`U_FAULTAND`); `R_FSETNPD` ↓, `R_FLCPD` ↓, `R_FAULTPU` ↑ put it in
      FAULT at every power-up, requiring an explicit re-arm.
    - Re-arm: `REARM_N` pulled **up** (`R_REARMPU`), edge-detected by one-shot section 2 (7 ms,
      `R_OS2` 10 k / `C_OS2` 1 µF) so a held-low line cannot hold the latch clear; and `R2̅` = `WD_OK`
      so a re-arm during a watchdog fault is ignored in hardware.
    - All eleven Pi/expander authorization lines pulled to GND, `REARM_N` up — verified pin by pin.
    - Every external input restrictive on loss of drive: `ESTOP_RAW`/`DOOR_RAW`/`MODE_RAW` 10 kΩ ↓,
      `COIL_EN_IN` 680 Ω ↓, `WD_PET` 1 kΩ ↓ (the datasheet-prescribed value, not 100 k — the
      TPS3823 self-pulses on a high-impedance WDI).
    - `TEMP_OK`'s pull-up is on `3V3_ANALOG`, the rail its own comparators run from, so an open `FB1`
      drives it **low** rather than high.
    The one residual is inherent to an open-drain wired-AND: an unpopulated or failed-high-Z LMV393
    leaves `TEMP_OK` permissive with no detection, which is why the host's ADC cross-check must stay
    mandatory. Honest observation, not a defect in this topology.
13. **STOP does not survive the faults that kill the key rail.** It does. `5V_STOP` = `5V_PROTECTED`
    via `R_STOPRAIL` (0 Ω), deliberately ungated; `Q_STOPDRV` is a dedicated 2N7002 driven straight
    from `STOP_REQ`; `D_KSTOP` clamps to `5V_STOP`. `K_STOP` closes whenever the board is powered and
    the Pi asks, independent of WD/TEMP/ESTOP/latch/Manual.
14. **PRESS pulse can exceed the 500 ms hard limit.** It cannot. CD74HC221 non-retriggerable,
    `R_OS` 510 kΩ (code-pinned C137961 precisely because the auto-picker offered three 390 kΩ parts
    for "510k") / `C_OS` 1 µF → 286–436 ms. X5R DC-bias capacitance loss *shortens* it (the safe
    direction): at −25 % C the minimum is ~239 ms, still above the 100–200 ms the OEM controller needs.
15. **The relay's coil and contacts share a footprint end.** They do not. `DIP05-1A72-13L` puts
    COIL_A/COIL_B on DIP grid positions 2 and 6 and CONTACT_B/CONTACT_A on 8 and 14 — opposite rows,
    minimum coil-pad to contact-pad spacing √(2.54² + 7.62²) = 8.03 mm by grid geometry alone.
16. **Coil pull-in margin is thin on a gated rail.** It is not — it is the best-margined number on the
    board. `5V_KEY_RELAY` floor 4.74 V, minus the TBD62083's 22.8 mV max (R_ON 3.25 Ω max, constant
    across the datasheet's three current points, so it extrapolates *down* to a 7–11 mA coil legitimately),
    against a pull-in max of 3.5 V → **1.22 V of margin**, i.e. an IR budget of ~110 Ω per coil path.

| rail | setpoint floor | load brownout | margin | Imax | total IR budget | real R in path | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `3V3` | 3.201 V | 3.000 V (MAX31856) | 201 mV | 0.75 A declared | **268 mΩ** | <40 mΩ board copper (0.3 mm class) | IR fine; **binding term is the LDO itself — see T-02** |
| `3V3_SW_*` | 3.201 − 10 mV (FET) | 3.0 V (4407 module) | ~190 mV | 0.10 A | **1.9 Ω** | ~0.25 Ω (500 mm 28 AWG JST-GH pair + 2 contacts) | **PASS, 7.6× margin** |
| `5V_KEY_RELAY` | 4.74 V | 3.50 V (DIP05 pull-in) | 1.217 V | ~11 mA/coil | **110 Ω** | mΩ | **PASS, huge** |
| `5V_STOP` | 4.754 V | 3.50 V | 1.254 V | ~11 mA | **114 Ω** | 0 Ω link | **PASS** |
| `5V_PROTECTED → J_LOADCELL.1` | 4.754 V | 2.6 V (HX711 min) | 2.15 V | ~10 mA | **215 Ω** | JST-XH + cable, ≪1 Ω | PASS low-side; **the exposure is the HIGH side — T-12** |

**The 5 V input path itself.** The design transfers cable and connector IR to the supplier by
specifying 4.850–5.250 V *at the connector, under full load* (D11 / ADR-0021). Judged on real
hardware: a 2-pin Micro-Fit 3.0 (~10 mΩ/contact → 20 mΩ for the pair) plus 1 m of 20 AWG twin
(2 × 33.3 mΩ/m = 67 mΩ) ≈ **87 mΩ → 80 mV at 0.92 A**. A 5.00 V brick therefore lands at 4.92 V —
inside. A 5.00 V −5 % brick (4.75 V, an utterly ordinary part) lands at **4.67 V** — outside, and the
LDO drops out. So the specification is *necessary*, and the "buy ±3 % / 5.1 V nominal and MEASURE it
at bring-up" instruction is the right call.

What is missing is enforcement. **Nothing on this board detects an out-of-spec supply:** the eFuse
variant is OVLO-only (there is no UVLO on `EN_OVLO_N` — the divider is a cutoff, not a window), there
is no power-good on `5V_PROTECTED`, and `EFUSE_FLT̄` does not report undervoltage. The only supervisor
is the TPS3823 on **3V3**, which cannot see a 5 V droop until the LDO has already dropped out, and
then only below 2.83–3.00 V (T-18). An out-of-spec brick therefore produces silent, unsupervised 3V3
sag — and it compounds T-02, where the declared load already eats the entire dropout margin.

**E-MARGIN verdict:** every downstream rail passes with real margin. The input path passes *only*
under a procurement constraint that is unenforced and undetected in hardware, on a rail whose LDO
headroom is 55 mV at the file's own optimistic load and **−24 mV at its own declared load**.

## E-OFF (mandatory section)

**PASS, and verified in the netlist rather than taken from the declaration.** `source_type:
external_5v_selv`; no battery, cell, pack, supercap or coin cell in the BOM; stored energy is bulk /
decoupling (µJ) plus reed-coil inductance that collapses the instant input is removed, with the
fail-safe direction (coils drop, contacts open). Removing `J_PWR` fully de-energizes the board.

The one declared off-control that could have been mere assertion is real: the Manual/Auto pole A is a
genuine series element — `U_AND3.4 → KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED → J_MODE.3 → [external DPDT] → J_MODE.4 →
COIL_EN_IN → R_COILENS → Q_COILDRV gate → Q_COIL (P-FET) → 5V_KEY_RELAY` — with `R_COILENPD` 680 Ω
pulling `COIL_EN_IN` low whenever the switch opens or the connector is unplugged. MANUAL, a broken
wire, and an unmated connector all physically remove coil power. Not a claim; traced.

## What a machine gate structurally cannot see (and did not)

- **T-01.** Every topology assert on the input chain is green: the fuse is present, the P-FET is
  oriented right, the crowbar is downstream, the TVS is unidirectional and on the right node. The
  defect is a *breakdown-voltage ordering* across three parts on two nets, and one of those parts has
  no `limits:` block for a gate to read. This is the same shape as the ADR-0021 P0, which was also
  green on every assert for five releases — the value was the fix.
- **T-02.** `power_topology.py` grades the rail it is handed. It cannot know that the five rails
  declared under `linear_rails:` are all *loads on* the rail declared under `rails:`, because the
  file expresses that relationship only in a comment.
- **T-03.** No gate computes an optocoupler's CTR against its LED resistor. "≤30 V/50 mA" is a string
  in `nets.yaml`.
- **T-04.** A netlist gate cannot see a claim in `ARCHITECTURE.md` about components that are absent.
- **T-08.** The tsx and the invariants are right; a `.md` and a `part.yaml` are wrong. Nothing
  cross-checks prose arithmetic against the netlist's actual resistor values.
- **T-13 / T-14.** Both are about the *undriven* state of nets that are perfectly well connected.
  Parity, DRC and ERC all pass on a net whose only driver is tri-stated.

## Verdict

The safety architecture is the strongest part of this board, and I want that on the record: I traced
all seven AND terms, the fault latch, the re-arm edge detector, the STOP preemption, the contactor
gate and every external input, and **found no permissive default on any safety term**. Decoder
one-hot, the non-retriggerable press bound, the restrictive-by-construction latch, the ungated STOP
rail and the doubly-redundant Manual cut are all real in the netlist, not asserted. Several things
the documents describe as defects (open-thermistor detection, the LMV393 common-mode problem) have
actually been fixed and the documents simply have not caught up.

What blocks the seal is protection and ratings. `D_ESD_IN` is a 5.5 V-breakdown SOD-323
with no DC rating, sitting outside the fuse, and it is the *only* part across the input in the exact
wrong-adapter scenario the whole OV chain exists to survive (T-01). The 3V3 power budget has never
been graded at its own declared load, and when it is, both terms fail (T-02). The isolated contactor
output is short of its declared capability by more than an order of magnitude and is unclamped on an
inductive load (T-03). And `ARCHITECTURE.md` claims Ioff buffers that are not on the BOM, on a link
that can back-power a Raspberry Pi 5 (T-04).

Three of those four are cheap — one resistor value, one arithmetic pass with citations, one clamp —
but T-01 and T-04 are net/copper changes, so they cannot ride a documentation revision.

`VERDICT: DO-NOT-ORDER`
