# cooksense v1.7 — RENDER / ARTWORK review (zero-context lens), 2026-07-28

**VERDICT: DO-NOT-ORDER** — one P0 and four P1s, all in artwork/paperwork, none
requiring a copper change.

Lens: **what a human sees and handles.** Silkscreen legibility and ownership,
connector distinguishability, mask/paste artwork, board outline / notches /
slots, the gerbers themselves, and the assembly paperwork.

Inputs read: `04_kicad/cooksense.kicad_pcb` (read-only),
`06_build/staging/cooksense-v1.7/fab/*`, `06_build/twin_v17f/*`,
`03_src/cooksense/{floorplan.yaml,fix_silk_placement.py,rules/}`,
`01_docs/{BRIEF,ARCHITECTURE,DETAIL_DESIGN,CHANGELOG}.md`, `01_docs/decisions/`,

STATUS, no prior review, no sealed verification folder. Nothing was written to
`04_kicad/` or `07_releases/`; no build was run.

Method for every distance below: `/usr/bin/python3` + `pcbnew` on the board.
Silk text and silk graphics are converted to their **actual stroke polygons**
(`TransformShapeToPolygon`, 0.005 mm error band); part bodies are the **pad
bounding rectangle** (not the footprint bbox, which includes the text and would
bias every ownership measurement toward the owner). Board outline is
`GetBoardPolygonOutlines`, which carries the 12 milled slots as holes. Renders:
`kicad-cli pcb render` and `kicad-cli pcb export svg --layers F.Silkscreen,Edge.Cuts`
rasterised at 47.248 px/mm and cropped to the region under discussion.

## 1. P0-1 — J_ISOLOOP has no identity at the terminal

`J_ISOLOOP` is the only connector on this board whose poles sit on the far side
of the opto barrier: a KF350-3.5 4-pole screw terminal, poles
`CONTACTOR_C / CONTACTOR_LOOP / CONTACTOR_LOOP / CONTACTOR_E`, ADR-0013, an
isolated 30 V contactor loop, explicitly NOT SELV. Everything else on the east
and south edges is SELV.

**Measured, from the board:**

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| J_ISOLOOP silk body | x[191.82 .. 198.97] y[87.92 .. 102.08] (7.15 × 14.16 mm) |
| silk text *inside* that body | **none** |
| its own refdes, printed at | (189.30, 101.00), h = 0.450 mm |
| refdes → J_ISOLOOP body | **4.900 mm** |
| refdes → **J_RH_EXHAUST** body | **1.300 mm** |
| ownership lead | **−3.600 mm** (nearer the wrong connector by 3.6×) |
| pole legend on silk | **none** (0 of 4 poles numbered or named) |
| "…ISOLATED 30V CONTACTOR LOOP -- NOT SELV" caption at | (62.00, 15.40), h = 0.60 |
| caption → J_ISOLOOP centre (195.30, 95.00) | **155.3 mm**, on a 188 × 92 mm board |

Rendered and read directly (`silk_top.svg` → PNG, crop x[188..200.5]
y[84..102.5]): the block is a **bare rectangle with nothing printed in it or
beside it**. The token `J_ISOLOOP` appears at the far left of that crop, sitting
directly beneath the `J_RH_EXHAUST` housing (which spans x[181.15..190.85]), on
the same y = 101.00 baseline as `J_RH_EXHAUST`'s own label at x = 182.00. What a
human reads along the south edge, left to right, is:

    J_RH_AMBIENT        J_RH_EXHAUST     J_ISOLOOP        [ unlabelled box ]

— two designators under one housing, and the hazardous connector unlabelled.
`J_ISOLOOP`'s label is also printed at h = 0.450 against `J_RH_EXHAUST`'s
h = 0.600, so the smaller, subordinate-looking token is the one that is out of
place.

### The recorded reason for not fixing this does not reproduce

`floorplan.yaml` (the J_ISOLOOP caption block) and `fix_silk_placement.py` both
record the same load-bearing measurement:

> "THERE IS NO SILK SITE AT THE BLOCK, and that is MEASURED, not assumed. …
> the nearest visible site for 'ISOLATED 30V' is (165.9,65.2), 41.9 mm away,
> and for the 7-character 'ISO 30V' it is (165.5,79.5), 33.6 mm away.
> The SE corner is saturated"

I rebuilt that sweep independently, with the constraint set the comment states —
pads +0.16 mm, existing silk +0.08 mm, **every courtyard as a filled region**,
plus 0.25 mm board-edge clearance — on a 0.1 mm grid over x[150..200.5]
y[60..102.5], using a summed-area table so every candidate box is tested exactly:

| caption | box tested | free sites | nearest site | distance to J_ISOLOOP centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ISO 30V` | 5.1 × 0.9 mm | 5753 | (189.05, 93.35) | **6.46 mm** |
| `NOT SELV` | 5.8 × 0.9 mm | 4172 | (188.70, 93.35) | **6.80 mm** |
| `30V` | 2.2 × 0.9 mm | 23205 | (190.50, 93.35) | **5.08 mm** |
| `J_ISOLOOP` | 6.5 × 0.9 mm | 3047 | (188.35, 93.35) | **7.14 mm** |

and then **looked at it**: crop x[184..194] y[89..98] of the silk plot shows a
continuous blank band roughly x[185.5 .. 191.7] × y[92.5 .. 95.5] — about
6 × 3 mm of empty silk immediately west of the block, bounded by U_OPTO's box
corner to the NW, J_RH_EXHAUST's corner mark to the S, and J_ISOLOOP's own west
silk line (x = 191.82) to the E.

A 7-character hazard caption fits **6.46 mm** from the block, not 33.6 mm. The
"saturated" conclusion is what turned an at-the-terminal warning into a
corner-of-the-board warning, and it is wrong. (Method difference is the likely
cause and it is worth naming: if the original sweep only excluded courtyard
*outlines* rather than filled courtyards it would have reported free space
*inside* bodies and then been tuned down; I hit exactly that bug on my first
pass — my initial sweep returned a "free" site 0.07 mm from the block centre,
i.e. inside the terminal block — and fixed it by filling courtyard bboxes.)

**Why P0 and not P1.** The consequence class is a technician landing 30 V
isolated contactor wiring, at a terminal whose artwork says nothing, guided by a
designator that is printed under a different connector. The mitigation the
design record believes exists ("the block is named on the board") is not present
on the artwork, and the measurement used to excuse its absence is falsifiable
and false. The fix is a caption in `floorplan.yaml` plus a rebuild — silk only,
no copper, no netlist. A reviewer who weighs "the block cannot be cross-plugged,
it is a screw terminal" more heavily may argue P1; I do not, because the hazard
here is not mis-mating, it is mis-*identification* of which of four adjacent
south-edge connectors is the non-SELV one, and the board currently answers that
question **backwards**.

**What would close it:** (a) a `NOT SELV` / `ISO 30V` caption at ~(189.0, 93.4)
or inside the block's own footprint silk; (b) `J_ISOLOOP`'s refdes re-anchored so
it leads `J_RH_EXHAUST` rather than trailing it by 3.6 mm; (c) pole numbers
1–4 on silk, or an explicit statement that the ORDER_README bring-up section is
the sole pole legend and that the board deliberately carries none.

## 2. P1-1 — the safety designators are the smallest, thinnest text on the board

Histogram of all **248** visible silk texts (height, stroke, count):

| h (mm) | stroke (mm) | count |
|---|---|---|
| 0.45 | **0.13** | **6** |
| 0.45 | 0.15 | 29 |
| 0.60 | 0.15 | 208 |
| 0.70 | 0.15 | 3 |
| 0.80 | 0.15 | 1 |
| 0.90 | 0.15 | 1 |

The six at stroke 0.130 mm are, exactly:

    D_COILEN   D_DOOR   J_DOOR   J_ESTOP   J_MODE   R_DOORPD

which is precisely the set `fix_silk_placement.py` relocates (R-02: J_MODE +
D_COILEN; R-04/pass B: J_DOOR, J_ESTOP, J_MODE; pass C: D_DOOR, R_DOORPD). The
mechanism is in the script itself, lines 286–289 and 337–338:

```python
for sz, rot in ((0.6, 0), (0.6, 90), (0.45, 0), (0.45, 90)):
    t.SetTextSize(pcbnew.VECTOR2I_MM(sz, sz))
    t.SetTextThickness(int(max(0.13, sz * 0.2) * 1e6))
```

At `sz = 0.45` that is `max(0.13, 0.09)` → **0.13**. So the v1.7 repair pass
bought each relocation by shrinking the label, and the floor it shrinks to is
0.13 mm. Three consequences, each measured:

1. **It violates the board's own declared floor.** `floorplan.yaml`
   `silk.min_text_height: 0.6`. 35 of 248 texts are at 0.450 — 14.1 % of the
   silk. (`silk.refdes.min_size: 0.45` permits it, so the two keys in the same
   block contradict each other; `min_text_height` is the one a reader would
   quote.)
2. **It violates the project's own waiver.** `rules/policy_waivers.yaml`
   `SILK-TEXT-THICKNESS` reads: *"text_thickness ignored: 0.15 mm stroke =
   JLCPCB's silk floor (legible at fab)"*. That waiver's entire evidence is that
   nothing on the board goes below 0.15. Six texts now do. The waiver is stale
   as of v1.7 and it is stale on the six labels it most needs to cover.
3. **The DRC rule that would have caught it is `ignore`.** `text_thickness` is
   one of the four classes `apply_drc_policy.py` sets to `ignore`
   (`SILK_IGNORE`), so nothing machine-checked the change the script made.

Independently confirmed in the gerber: `cooksense-F_Silkscreen.gto` declares
`%ADD11C,0.112500*%` and `%ADD12C,0.120000*%` — two silk apertures **below
0.15 mm** — alongside the expected `%ADD10C,0.150000*%`. (0.1125 and 0.120 are
KiCad's sub-stroke apertures for the 0.13 mm strokes' end caps and the
0.15 mm-nominal glyph interiors; the point is that the plot really does emit
sub-0.15 mm features.)

My own recollection of JLCPCB's published silkscreen capability is 0.15 mm
minimum line width and 0.8 mm minimum character height, which would put
0.45 mm / 0.13 mm at 56 % and 87 % of those limits. **I am grading that
recollection ESTIMATED, not CITED** — I have no vendor page in front of me — and
the finding does not depend on it: the artwork already contradicts two numbers
the *project itself* wrote down. The 0.15 mm figure in the project's own waiver
is the citation that matters.

**What would close it:** either raise `refdes.min_size` to 0.6 for the
`SAFETY_CONNECTORS` set and re-run the placement search (the script already
walks four size/rotation candidates; the safety set should be forbidden the
0.45 rungs), or re-state the `SILK-TEXT-THICKNESS` waiver with the actual
measured minimum (0.13) and a *vendor-cited* floor showing it prints.

## 3. P1-2 — J_ESTOP's designator leads its rival by 0.608 mm

The east/south edges carry **four identical, inter-mateable** JST-GH housings —
`J_DOOR`, `J_ESTOP`, `J_RH_AMBIENT`, `J_RH_EXHAUST`, all SM05B-GHS-TB
(C189896), 1.25 mm, 5-position. (`J_MODE` is out of that family in v1.7 —
confirmed in §9.) The documented mitigation is the silk designator. Measured
label-to-body distances, own part vs. nearest rival:

| label | h (mm) | → own body | → nearest **connector** | lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `J_RH_AMBIENT` | 0.60 | 1.300 | J_RH_EXHAUST 9.242 | **+7.942** |
| `J_RH_EXHAUST` | 0.60 | 1.300 | J_RH_AMBIENT ≥ 9.242 | **+7.9** |
| `J_DOOR` | **0.45** | 1.050 | J_ESTOP 2.285 | **+1.235** |
| `J_ESTOP` | **0.45** | 2.300 | J_DOOR 2.908 | **+0.608** |

The inversion is the finding: **the two connectors with no safety role get
0.60 mm text and an 8 mm margin; the door interlock and the E-STOP get 0.45 mm
text at 0.13 mm stroke and a 1.2 mm / 0.6 mm margin.** The mitigation is
weakest exactly where the hazard is greatest.

Read at the artwork (crop x[188..200.5] y[52..90]), the two labels sit in the
2 mm gap between the housings, and four texts crowd that gap: `R_ESTOPPD`
(rotated 90°), `J_ESTOP` (horizontal), `R_DOORPD`, `D_DOOR` (rotated 90°) and
`J_DOOR` (rotated 90°). `J_ESTOP` is placed at (192.00, 75.13) — 0.60 mm above
its own housing's *southern* edge (y = 75.73), i.e. at the very boundary.
`J_DOOR` is at (193.25, 77.76), 0.85 mm below the boundary. A human holding a
harness sees two designators within 2.63 mm of each other in y, one of which is
rotated.

`J_ESTOP` is also, strictly, nearer a non-connector: `R_ESTOPPD` at 2.210 mm
beats it by 0.090 mm. That is harmless (a 0402 is not confusable with a housing)
but it shows the ownership pass grades only `J_`-prefixed rivals, which is the
same narrowing that let `D_DOOR` slip through until pass C was added.

**Attached self-refutation, and it is why this is P1 and not P0:** I read the
pin nets of all four housings to test whether a cross-plug is *harmful*.

```
J_DOOR        1=3V3          2=DOOR_RAW  3=GND    4=DOOR_RAW  5=GND
J_ESTOP       1=3V3          2=ESTOP_RAW 3=GND    4=GND       5=GND
J_RH_AMBIENT  1=3V3_SW_RHA   2=GND       3=SDA_A  4=SCL_A     5=SHIELD_DRAIN
J_RH_EXHAUST  1=3V3_SW_RHE   2=GND       3=SDA_B  4=SCL_B     5=SHIELD_DRAIN
```

Every cross-plug permutation I can construct **fails safe**, because ADR-0019
puts a restrictive-default pull-down on every safety-chain input and "safe" is
LOW: an RH harness into J_DOOR/J_ESTOP lands the sensor's ground on the
interlock input (interlock reads NOT-OK); a door harness into J_ESTOP shorts
ESTOP_RAW to GND through the door switch when the door is *closed* (E-STOP reads
asserted) and leaves it floating to its pull-down when open (still asserted); an
E-STOP harness into J_DOOR pulls DOOR_RAW low and leaves ESTOP_RAW on its
pull-down. No permutation I found produces a *permissive* result, and none
applies an over-voltage to a part. **The silk designator is therefore not the
sole mitigation, contrary to the framing in ORDER_README §10.5** — the
restrictive defaults are the real one, and they hold. The residual harm is a
dead sensor bus or a machine that won't arm, diagnosed at the bench.

## 5. P1-4 — twelve 0.600 mm milled slots with no capability evidence

`GetBoardPolygonOutlines` returns one outer contour and **12 holes**, every one
of them 0.600 mm wide:

| group | y band | count | length |
|---|---|---|---|
| south comb | y[49.30 .. 49.90] | 6 | 8.44 mm (one is 9.60) |
| north comb | y[26.00 .. 26.60] | 6 | 8.44 mm |

plus an east-edge notch in the outer contour at x[191.50 .. 200.00]
y[48.80 .. 49.80]. Outer board = x[12.00 .. 200.00] y[10.00 .. 102.00] =
**188.0 × 92.0 mm**. The slots are correctly expressed as Edge.Cuts geometry and
appear in `cooksense-Edge_Cuts.gm1` (single 0.100 mm aperture), which is the
right place for routed features.

The 0.600 mm figure traces back through `floorplan.yaml` ("Milled 0.6mm slots
reinforce each coil-gap mouth") to BRIEF D7 ("milled slots reinforce the
pocket/gap creepage per the **v1.0 0.6mm slot pattern**") — i.e. it is inherited
from v1.0 as a pattern, never as a manufacturability claim. I grepped
`01_docs/`, `03_src/cooksense/floorplan.yaml`, `rules/policy_waivers.yaml`,
`apply_drc_policy.py` and `skills/jlcpcb-fab/` and found **no** slot-width
capability statement, no vendor citation, and no DRC constraint on minimum slot
width anywhere in the project.

My recollection is that JLCPCB's minimum routed slot width is 0.8 mm (router
bit), with anything narrower quoted as a special process or rejected. **That
recollection is ESTIMATED, not CITED**, and I am not asserting it as fact. The
finding is the *absence*: twelve slots whose width is 25 % below a limit nobody
in this repo has written down, on a feature that is load-bearing for the
isolation comb. If they cannot be milled, the board comes back re-quoted or with
the slots omitted — and slots omitted silently is a creepage defect that
survives every gate this board runs, because DRC does not know why the slots are
there.

**What would close it:** one CITED line in the project record — JLC's capability
page, section and date — stating the minimum slot width and whether 0.60 mm is
inside it. If it is not, this becomes a P0 outline change.

## 9. Self-refutations — things I flagged and then disproved with my own measurement

1. **"The relay bank can be built with a relay 180° out."** I flagged it: the 12
   relays alternate rot 0 / rot 180 in pairs, the pin-1 cue is a ⌀0.6 mm silk dot
   that alternates ends (y = 28.80 for rot 0, y = 47.20 for rot 180), and the
   dot is 4.195 mm from pad 1 rather than adjacent to it. **Disproved by
   geometry:** the four holes sit at DIP 2 / 6 / 8 / 14, i.e. rel. (−3.81,−5.08),
   (−3.81,+5.08), (+3.81,+7.62), (+3.81,−7.62). Rotating that set 180° maps it to
   DIP 13 / 9 / 7 / 1 — **none of which is a hole**. The land is chiral; the part
   physically cannot be inserted backwards. Not a finding.
2. **"U_EFUSE's thermal pad has no paste."** True of pad 9 (0.90 × 1.60,
   `F_Paste = False`) — but the `Texas_DSG0008A_WSON-8-1EP` footprint provides
   the EP paste through **two unnamed sub-pads** (0.73 × 0.64 each,
   `F_Paste = True`, `F_Mask = False`), giving 2 × 0.4672 = **0.934 mm² of paste
   over a 1.44 mm² EP = 65 % coverage** — a normal EP paste reduction. (This is
   also what `tie_efuse_ep.py` exists to net-tie.) Not a finding.
3. **"The J_ISOLOOP pole legend in `floorplan.yaml` contradicts the board."** The
   shorthand `(1 LOOP / 2 RET / 3 CTR / 4 CTR)` looked wrong against the pad nets
   `1 = CONTACTOR_C, 2/3 = CONTACTOR_LOOP, 4 = CONTACTOR_E`. **ADR-0013 lines
   44–47 reconcile them exactly**: pole 1 LOOP_OUT = CONTACTOR_C, pole 2 LOOP_RET
   = CONTACTOR_LOOP, pole 3 CTR_A = CONTACTOR_LOOP, pole 4 CTR_B = CONTACTOR_E.
   The shorthand is correct. Not a finding. (The legend is still on no artwork —
   that part of P0-1 stands.)
4. **"Every refdes is printed twice."** The board carries 259 `gr_text` and 234
   `fp_text`/property references with the same token strings. **Disproved by
   layer:** every `gr_text` refdes is on **F.Fab**, not F.Silkscreen — it is the
   assembly-drawing duplicate that canon P4 requires. Only the footprint
   references print. Not a finding.
5. **"Five inter-mateable GH housings."** The brief names five; v1.7 has **four**.
   `J_MODE` is now `JST_ZH_S4B-ZR-SM4A-TF_1x04-1MP_P1.50mm_Horizontal`
   (C485354) — 1.50 mm pitch, 4 positions — against the SM05B-GHS-TB quartet's
   1.25 mm / 5 positions. Neither pitch nor position count matches; ADR-0018's
   keying holds on the artwork as built. Confirmed on the board, not from the
   ADR.

And one methodological self-correction, recorded because it changed a number:
my first free-site sweep for P0-1 excluded courtyard **outlines** rather than
filled courtyards and duly reported a "free" caption site 0.07 mm from
J_ISOLOOP's centre — inside the terminal block. The 6.46 mm figure in §1 is from
the corrected sweep with courtyard bounding boxes filled. I suspect the same
class of error, in the opposite direction, produced the 33.6 mm figure in
`floorplan.yaml`; that is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

## Verdict

**DO-NOT-ORDER.**

P0-1 alone blocks: the non-SELV 30 V terminal is the one connector on this board
a person can hurt themselves or the SELV domain with, and it is the one
connector with no artwork at all — while the token that names it is printed
under its SELV neighbour. The recorded justification for that state is
reproducibly wrong by ~27 mm.

The four P1s are all cheap and all in the same family: artwork and paperwork that
say something the board does not do. None requires a copper change; P1-4 might,
if the 0.60 mm slots turn out to be unmillable, which is exactly why it needs a
citation before the order rather than after.

The counterweight, honestly stated: the three ungated silk classes that *could*
have been catastrophic — text-on-text collisions, silk into the milled notch and
slots, silk over pads — measure **0 / 0 / 1-at-4.6 %** across 248 texts and 805
graphics. The v1.7 silk pass did real work and the notch defect it was written
for is gone. What it did not do is notice that it was paying for each fix in
stroke width, and that the connector it declined to fix is the dangerous one.
