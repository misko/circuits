# P0 — the series sum is temperature-corrected on one side only, and the file says it isn't

## What is claimed

`03_src/cooksense/rules/power_tree.yaml` (staged copy identical), the `vin_min`
block, and ADR-0027 §Method / §Consequences:

- Corner: *"copper 1.72e-8 Ω·m at 20 C **× 1.216 at 75 C** (alpha 3.93e-3/K — the
  **SAME Ta this rail's thermal half is graded at**)"*.
- *"**SENSITIVITY, so no un-cited input is load-bearing**: at 25 µm plating instead
  of 18 the total is 321.9 mΩ (−2.6 mV of drop); at 25 C copper instead of 75 C it

component resistance — **58.0 % of the 328.29 mΩ total**, MEASURED by me at

## The term that IS load-bearing and IS un-cited

`F1` is a **P**ositive **T**emperature **C**oefficient device. Rising resistance
with temperature is not a parasitic — it is the mechanism the part is sold for.
Its 70 mΩ is a 23 °C number and this board's declared ambient is 75 °C, 52 K
higher, inside the polymer's pre-transition climb. The committed datasheet gives
me **nothing** to bound it with. So I did the only honest thing available and
solved for the break-even:

| F1 multiplier over its 23 °C R1Max | R_total | headroom | vs 1300 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ×1.2 (84 mΩ) | 358.2 mΩ | 1317.5 mV | +17.5 |
| ×1.4 (98 mΩ) | 372.2 | 1311.8 | +11.8 |
| ×1.6 (112 mΩ) | 386.2 | 1306.2 | +6.2 |
| **×1.82 (127.4 mΩ)** | **400.7** | **1300.0** | **0 — BREAK-EVEN**, other terms at the hot corner |
| ×2.0 (140 mΩ) | 414.2 | 1294.9 | **−5.1 FAIL** |
| ×2.03 (142.2 mΩ) | — | 1300.0 | 0 — break-even with *everything else exactly as ADR-0027 declares it* |

MEASURED by me (bisection inside my own solver, 60 iterations, tolerance < 1 µΩ).

**A ×1.82 rise from 23 °C to 75 °C is well inside the plausible range for a
polymer PPTC.** I am not claiming a number — that is the point. Nobody on this
board has a number, and `power_tree.yaml` states in writing that no such
un-cited input exists.

## Why this is a P0 and not a P1

ADR-0027 §Decision 4 sets the standard itself, verbatim: *"ds1117 publishes no
thermal time constant, so the excursion is **NOT BOUNDED BY ANY CITED NUMBER and
must not be claimed** at +3.0 °C."* It then applied that standard by removing the
question (Decision 3's staggering constraint) rather than answering it.

The identical situation on F1 was not treated the same way. It was silently
claimed at R(75 °C) = R(23 °C), and the sensitivity paragraph asserts the
opposite of the truth. This is the **fourth** instance of *a load-bearing sum
with a term missing*, on the **same rail**, in the pass whose entire subject is
that defect class — and this time the missing term is the temperature basis of
the majority of the sum.

The rail may well be fine physically: the 1300 mV dropout is a 0.8 A figure
applied to a 0.2 A rail and the ds1117 General Description says dropout falls
with load. But ADR-0027 explicitly refused to lean on that argument (*"the
alternative is to invent the low-current dropout figure this file has refused to
invent since v1.5"*), so it is not available here either. **On the numbers the
release actually publishes, the dropout verdict is not established.**

**What would close it:** either (a) a cited R-vs-T bound for the MF-MSMF200 from
Bourns, or (b) the same treatment ADR-0027 gave the heaters — declare the
constraint, i.e. state the maximum ambient at which the dropout budget holds and
carry the F1 term explicitly as OWED with its break-even multiplier published, or
(c) the G4 bench measurement of `V_IN − V_OUT` that is already OWED, taken at
temperature. (c) retires it for free alongside the two measurements already
booked.

# P1-1 — `Tj = Ta + PD × θ_JA` treats the board's copper as being at ambient, and it is not

ADR-0026 Decision 2 and its `LDO_IOUT_MAX` bound both evaluate
`Tj = 75 + (0.4098 + 0.0578) × 90 = 117.08 °C`, margin +7.92 °C. That form is
the ds1117's own — and the ds1117 §Thermal Considerations, on the same page,
says: *"For the surface mount package SOT-223 **additional heat sources mounted
near the device must be considered**. The heat dissipation capability of the PC
board and its copper traces is used as a heat sink for the device."*

θ_JA is a junction-to-**ambient-air** number measured with only the device under
test dissipating. Every other watt on this board raises the copper the LDO sinks
into, and that rise is **additive**, not absorbed.

good placement, and it is why the term is 3 °C rather than 10.

# P1-2 — the tab's whole metal path is two 0.15 mm vias, and the options analysis never counted them

**MEASURED geometry of `U_LDO` (SOT-223, `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-223`, F.Cu, at

|---|---|

| pads 1/2/3 (GND / 3V3 / 5V_PROTECTED) | 2.000 × 1.500 = 3.000 mm² each, x = 18.850 |
| F.Cu pour clearance around the tab | **0.200 mm** on all four edges (sampled at 5 µm) |
| F.Cu 3V3 copper attached to the tab | **none beyond the pad** — zero `3V3` track segments anywhere within ±12 mm of the part |
| vias in the tab pad | **2**, at (25.150, 68.900) and (25.150, 70.000), **0.150 mm drill / 0.250 mm OD**, F.Cu→B.Cu |

enumerated.** MEASURED in my model at k_FR4 = 0.40 / h = 10 (the calibrated pair),

|---|---|---|---|

| 4 × 0.15 mm | 77.6 | 114.1 | +10.9 |

| 8 × 0.15 mm | **68.9** | 110.0 | **+15.0** |

Six more vias in an existing pad is **−18.0 °C/W and +8.4 °C of junction margin**

the GND pour. The decision to defer copper was reasonable; **the options table it
was made from was incomplete**, and the item it omitted dominates the two it
listed on cost.

Removing both paths in my model (dielectric only) gives 165.3 °C/W — i.e. the
two barrels are carrying roughly a third of the heat. That is a lot of load on
two 0.15 mm holes.

# P2s

**P2-1 — the ISO_CONTACTOR barrier is exactly its own rule minimum, on every layer,
with zero manufacturing margin.** MEASURED by polygon inflation-bisection over
pads + tracks + vias + filled pours: minimum ISO-to-non-ISO separation is
**2.0000 mm on F.Cu, In1.Cu, In2.Cu and B.Cu** — identical to four decimals,
because the pours are clipped to the 2.0 mm rule offset. `opto_isolation_2mm`
cites *"IEC 60664-1 basic insulation, 30 V working, pollution degree 3 … material
group IIIa"*. A design drawn exactly at a safety minimum has no headroom against
etch/plating tolerance, which on an outer layer moves gaps in the *closing*
direction. Not a defect; a zero-margin condition that should be a declared
number rather than an emergent one.

**P2-2 — 276 via-in-pad instances, 0.15 mm drill, unfilled.** MEASURED: every via
whose centre lies inside an SMD/CONN pad, board-wide — all 0.150 mm drill /
0.250 mm OD, on 168 distinct pads including `U_LDO.4` (×2, the SOT-223 tab),
`U_LDO.1/.2`, `D_TVS.2` (2.50 × 2.30 mm), `D_REVCLAMP.2`, `CE1.2`, `J_PWR.MP`
(×4) and every decoupling capacitor. Board setup is `(tenting (front yes)(back
yes))` with `(plugging (front no)(back no)) (filling no) (capping no)`, and
nothing in `ORDER_README` orders POFV / resin-plugged vias. Open barrels inside
paste-printed pads wick solder during reflow; the exposure is largest on the
big pads (the tab and the two 2.5 mm-class diode pads). This is a house pattern,
not a one-off, so it is a fleet question rather than a board question — but it is
unpriced.

**P2-3 — 56 real silkscreen violations sit behind 9 `ignore` severities.** I
re-ran DRC with every `rule_severities: ignore` flipped to `warning`
(RAW_EXIT=0): **49 `silk_over_copper`, 5 `silk_edge_clearance`, 2 `silk_overlap`,
0 unconnected, 0 parity**. The 49 are the 12 `K_*` reed-relay outlines (4 each)
plus one text; the 2 overlaps are `Q_SWDRVA` against the PCB text
`ANALOG SENSE (3V3_ANALOG)`. The 5 edge clips are the ones worth naming: **two on
`J_MODE` and three on `J_ISOLOOP`** — `J_ISOLOOP` is the isolated contactor
terminal block, whose marking is part of how an integrator identifies the
barrier. Six of the nine ignored checks (`missing_courtyard`,
`footprint_type_mismatch`, `track_not_centered_on_via`,
`footprint_filters_mismatch`, `text_thickness`, `tuning_profile_track_geometries`)
are **inert** — zero hits — which is worth recording, because an exclusion list
whose members are never exercised is indistinguishable from one that hides
something until you run it.

**P2-4 — `linear_rails[5V_PROTECTED].iout_max_A: 2.0` is 5× anything on this
board evaluates.** The dropout solve charges 0.4024 A; E-TOPO derives a 0.2 A
trunk need and prints *"OVER-BUILT (advisory): declared trunk current 2 A … is
>2x the derived need 0.2 A"*. Nothing computes what 2.0 A would do. MEASURED
(IPC-2221 external, 0.500 mm / 35 µm): **ΔT = +20.9 °C**, i.e. a 96 °C trace in a
75 °C ambient; and V(`U_LDO.3`) at 2.0 A of whole-board current would be far
below dropout (break-even is 0.4910 A). The 2.0 A is an eFuse-envelope figure
living in a field named for a load. It is documentation-only and cannot fail a
gate, which is precisely the shape ADR-0026 §Option A was written about.

**Gate-hygiene observation, reported because it bit me and will bit the next
reader, and will bite the next one:** my first DRC run reported **199 violations
and still exited 0** —
`kicad-cli pcb drc` returns 0 on violations unless `--exit-code-violations` is
passed. (Those 199 were `lib_footprint_issues` from my scratch copy's missing

`cooksense.pretty` and the lib table.) A DRC invocation without
`--exit-code-violations` is a gate that cannot fail.

**Method note (canon M1):** my resistance answer and ADR-0027's are both nodal
solves — there is no other way to get an exact answer on a shared trunk — but the
*implementations* are independent: my own graph construction (endpoint-key nodes,
5 T-junction splits detected and applied, union-find pad merging by

partial pivoting, my own resistivity/via model written from the datasheet
figures. 141 nodes solved. The agreement to 0.19 mΩ across four independently
computed segments is therefore evidence, not an echo. The **thermal** model
shares no method with anything in this repo and is calibrated against an
external artefact (the ds1117's own Table 1).

- **The KEYPAD_ISO domain and the H1/H4 fastener-disc creepage geometry.** I

  about a 1.1 s heater pulse.
- **A 2-D/3-D field solve.** No `numpy`/`scipy` on this host, so my thermal model
  is a **radial** network on measured annular areas, not a Cartesian FD grid. It
  cannot see the fact that the LDO sits 17 mm from the north edge of a 49 mm-tall
  pour band and 13 mm from the west board edge — the annular areas capture the
  *amount* of copper at each radius but not its *anisotropy*. A real solver would
  most likely make θ_JA slightly **worse**, not better.

  linear + ≤1 MHz SPI / 400 kHz I2C, and In1 GND is 8465 mm² near-solid, so I
