## OVERALL: **FAIL** — 1 blocking, 6 questions

The blocking defect is *not* the relay land (that is correct — see PIN-1) and
*not* a missing divider on the expander (the divider exists). It is that the
divider that v1.7 added to protect `U_EXP` pad 1 **omits the 100 kΩ pull-up that
actually sources the node**, so the tap sits at **0.833 V** where the part needs
**≥ 2.64 V** to read a logic HIGH. The pin is protected and dead.

### 🔴 **PIN-P0-1 (FAIL, BLOCKING)** — GPB0/`EFUSE_FLT_DIV`: the divider is computed without the pull-up, and the eFuse fault flag can never read HIGH

**Where.** `03_src/rules/electrical_invariants.yaml:794-840` (the "v1.7 / PIN P0-b"
block); `03_tscircuit/src/cooksense.tsx` (`R_FLTDIVT`, `R_FLTDIVB`);
netlist `EFUSE_FLT_N`, `EFUSE_FLT_DIV`.

**The actual circuit, from the netlist:**

```
5V_PROTECTED ──[ R_PG  100 kΩ ]── EFUSE_FLT_N ──[ R_FLTDIVT 10 kΩ ]── EFUSE_FLT_DIV ──[ R_FLTDIVB 22 kΩ ]── GND
                                       │                                     │
                                U_EFUSE.6 (FLT_N, OPEN-DRAIN)          U_EXP.1 (GPB0)
                                  TP_PGOOD.1
```

`EFUSE_FLT_N` has exactly four nodes: `U_EFUSE.6`, `R_PG.1`, `TP_PGOOD.1`,
`R_FLTDIVT.1`. There is **no other pull-up and no other driver** — the eFuse
output is open-drain (`02_parts/TPS259573DSGR/part.yaml` pin 6: "OPEN-DRAIN fault
flag … needs external pullup"), so the *only* thing that can pull the node high
is `R_PG`, 100 kΩ.

**The design's own arithmetic omits `R_PG`.** `electrical_invariants.yaml:800-803`
computes "22k/(10k+22k) × 5.25 = 3.609 V" and "at the NOMINAL 5.0 V rail the tap
is 3.438 V". That treats `EFUSE_FLT_N` as a stiff 5 V source. It is not; it is
5 V behind 100 kΩ.

**Measured (node analysis, series chain 100 k + 10 k over 22 k):**

| rail | V(`EFUSE_FLT_N`) | V(`EFUSE_FLT_DIV`) = GPB0 | VIH(min) 2.64 V | VIL(max) 0.66 V |
|------|------------------|---------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 5.00 V (nom) | 1.212 V | **0.833 V** | ✗ 1.81 V short | ✗ 0.17 V over |
| 5.25 V | 1.273 V | 0.875 V | ✗ | ✗ |
| 5.50 V | 1.333 V | 0.917 V | ✗ | ✗ |

Thevenin at the tap: 0.833 V / 18.33 kΩ, so the ±1 µA input leakage moves it
±18 mV — irrelevant.

**Consequence.** No-fault (flag released) puts GPB0 at 0.833 V, which is *neither*
a valid HIGH *nor* a valid VIL — it sits in the Schmitt's indeterminate band.
Fault (flag pulled low) puts GPB0 at ≈0 V. **Both states read the same or worse:
the board can never report "power good".** Software polling GPB0 sees a permanent
(or randomly latched) "fault". The v1.7 fix removed an over-voltage and replaced
it with a dead readback.

**Self-refutation attempted, and it fails.** If firmware enables the MCP23017's
internal weak pull-up (GPPU, ~100 kΩ to VDD) on GPB0:
- no fault: V = (3.3/100 + 5/110) / (1/100 + 1/110 + 1/22) = **1.216 V** — still
  below VIH and still above VIL;
- fault: **0.212 V**.

Two states, neither a valid HIGH. GPPU does not rescue it. The finding stands.

**Fixes (either is one line of source, both need a rebuild):**
- Move the pull-up: delete `R_FLTDIVT`/`R_FLTDIVB` and pull `EFUSE_FLT_N` to
  **3V3** through `R_PG`. Node then rests at 3.3 V — inside the 3.9 V abs-max,
  above the 2.64 V VIH, and `TP_PGOOD` still probes the real open-drain node.
  (The TPS2595 FLT_N is open-drain, so the pull-up rail is free.)
- Or keep the 10 k/22 k ratio and make `R_PG` **≤ 9.67 kΩ** (solved from
  5·22/(R_PG+32) ≥ 2.64) — costs ~0.5 mA of standing current and is the worse
  answer.

**Note for whoever fixes it:** the assert at `electrical_invariants.yaml:831-835`
(`U_EXP.1` on `EFUSE_FLT_DIV`) will happily go GREEN on this broken board. It
checks that the divider exists, not that the level works. If the fix is the
3V3 pull-up, that assert must be retired, not re-pointed.

### ❓ Q-1 — the docs call pad 1 "GPA0". It is **GPB0**.

`electrical_invariants.yaml:795` ("GPA0 idled at 5.0 V"), `:820` ("GPA0 is
grounded"), `:832` comment "`# MCP23017 pad1 = GPA0`", `:835` ("GPA0 is on the
TAP"), and `01_docs/STATUS-cooksense.md:14` ("PIN P0-b needs U_EXP.1 off the 5 V
…") all name the eFuse readback **GPA0**. Per DS20001952C p.1, **pad 1 is GPB0;
GPA0 is pad 21**, and pad 21 on this board is `RAIL_EN_A` — an *output* driving
`Q_SWDRVA`'s gate.

The copper is unambiguous (the assert is written against `U_EXP.1`, and pad 1 is
where the tap lands), so this is a *documentation* defect, not a wiring one. But
it is a live firmware hazard: anyone writing the driver from these notes reads
`GPIOA` bit 0 and gets the state of the rail-enable output, not the fault flag.
The five sibling comments at `:769`, `:784` etc. correctly say "pad5 = GPB4",
"pad6 = GPB5" — only the P0-b block drifted.

**Recommend:** rename to GPB0 throughout the P0-b block and in `STATUS`.

### ❓ Q-2 — `INTB` (pad 19) is a one-node net

`EXP_INTB` has exactly **one** node: `U_EXP.19`. Per the part's own gotcha,
INTA/INTB default to **active-low push-pull** outputs — so this is an output
driving a stub, which is electrically harmless but means the net exists only to
name a dangling pin. It also means the B-port interrupt (which is where all six
safety readbacks live, GPB1–GPB6) has **no path to the host**; only INTA
(pad 20, A-port) reaches `J_PI.13`. If the intent was interrupt-driven safety
readback, IOCON.MIRROR must be set in firmware or the B-port interrupt is
invisible. Worth an explicit decision + a note, or a deliberate `no_connect`.

### ❓ Q-3 — GPB6 (`TC_FAULT_N`) is the odd one out: no series R, and possibly no pull-up

`TC_FAULT_N` has three nodes: `U_TC.13` (MAX31856 FAULT), `TP_TCFAULT.1`,
`U_EXP.7`. Its five siblings on GPB1–GPB5 each get a 10 kΩ series resistor per
ADR-0020; GPB6 gets nothing. Two sub-questions:

1. If the MAX31856 `FAULT` output is **open-drain**, this net has **no pull-up
   anywhere on the board** and GPB6 floats in the no-fault state (readable only
   if firmware enables GPPU). If it is push-pull, the net is fine.
2. There is **no committed datasheet** for `MAX31856MUD+T` (see Q-6), so I could
   not resolve (1) from repo evidence. Flagging rather than guessing.

The missing series resistor is defensible (`TC_FAULT_N` is not one of the seven
safety permission terms), but the asymmetry should be stated somewhere.

### ❓ Q-4 — `/RESET` (pad 18) sits directly on `WD_OK`: correct, and worth writing down

`WD_OK` is the TPS3823-33 push-pull RESET output (`02_parts/TPS3823-33DBVR/part.yaml`
pin 1), with `R_WDOKPD` 100 k to GND, and it fans out to eight other nodes
(`U_AND1.3`, `U_CAND1.1`, `U_FAULTAND.1`, `U_OENAND.2`, `U_ONESHOT.11`,
`U_EXP.18`, plus `R_WDOKSER` to GPB7 and `TP_WDOK`).

Consequence: any watchdog timeout **holds the expander in reset**, forcing GPA0–7
to inputs, so `RAIL_EN_*` / `CONTACTOR_REQ` collapse to their 100 k pull-downs.
That is fail-safe and almost certainly deliberate — but it also means the
expander loses all register state (IODIR, GPPU, IOCON.MIRROR) on every watchdog
event, and firmware must re-initialise it, not just re-write outputs. No
statement of that requirement was found in the curated inputs.

### ❓ Q-5 — no I²C pull-ups on this board

`I2C_SCL` / `I2C_SDA` each have exactly two nodes: `U_EXP.12/13` and `J_PI.5/3`.
The bus depends entirely on the Raspberry Pi's on-board 1.8 kΩ pull-ups on
GPIO2/GPIO3. `J_PI` pins 3 and 5 are indeed the Pi's SDA1/SCL1, so this works —
but it is an off-board dependency, and it means the board cannot be bench-tested
with an I²C master that lacks pull-ups. The `part.yaml` `layout.notes` already
say "one pull-up pair per bus (not per device)"; recommend recording *where* the
pair physically is.

### ❓ Q-8 — `J_LOADCELL` offers a 5 V pin on a connector whose data lines have no translation

`03_tscircuit/src/cooksense.tsx:739-745`: `J_LOADCELL.1 = 5V_PROTECTED`,
`.2 = 3V3`, `.3 = GND`, `.4 = LC_DAT`, `.5 = LC_CLK`. `LC_DAT`/`LC_CLK` go
through 33 Ω (`R_LCDAT`/`R_LCCLK`) straight to `J_PI.38`/`J_PI.40` — Raspberry
Pi GPIO20/21, 3.3 V, abs-max ≈ 3.6 V. The only protection is
`PESD5V0S1BA` (5.0 V standoff), which **does not clamp a DC 5 V logic high**.

If the external cook-loadcell board is strapped to pin 1 (5 V) — which is what a
5 V-excitation HX711 wants — its `DOUT` is a 5 V push-pull output driven directly
into a Pi GPIO through 33 Ω. That is the same class of defect v1.7 just fixed on
`U_EXP.1`, moved to a connector. It is not *wired* wrong (the 3V3 pin is right
there), so it is a **QUESTION**, not a FAIL — but it needs either a silk/doc
constraint ("power the HX711 from pin 2 only"), a series+clamp to 3V3, or removal
of pin 1.

### ❓ Q-7 — two `part.yaml` schema hazards that a checker would step in

**(a) `SN74HC14DR/part.yaml` pin 12 is named `6A_Y`.** Pin 12 is `6Y` and pin 13
is `6A`; the yaml gives pin 13 the correct `6A` and pin 12 a garbled hybrid. The
netlist has it right (`6Y_12` / `6A_13`), so no copper is wrong — but a
pin-name-based checker matching on `6A` would now match two pins.

**(b) `DIP05-1A72-13L/part.yaml` `pins:` is keyed by PHYSICAL DIP LEAD number
(2, 6, 8, 14) while the footprint and netlist use RENUMBERED pads (1, 2, 3, 4).**
The two numberings *collide*: yaml key `2` means `COIL_A`, while pad `2` is
`COIL_B`. Any gate that reads `part.yaml.pins` and indexes it by footprint pad
number silently mis-maps this part — and would mis-map it in a way that *looks*
plausible (both are coil pins), so the error would not announce itself. It is
harmless today only because the reed coil is non-polar (stated in the dossier's
third gotcha). Recommend the dossier carry both maps explicitly, e.g. a
`pad_map: {1: COIL_A, 2: COIL_B, 3: CONTACT_B, 4: CONTACT_A}` alongside the
lead map, and that the 02_parts contract template gain a rule for renumbered
footprints.
