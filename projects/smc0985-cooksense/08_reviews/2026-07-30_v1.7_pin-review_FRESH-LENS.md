### U_ULNA — TBD62083AFWG octal DMOS sink array — **VERDICT: PASS**

Datasheet: `02_parts/TBD62083AFWG/TBD62083A_20160511.pdf`, p.1 *"Pin connection (top view)"* figure + p.2 *"Pin explanations"* table, both rendered at 110 dpi and read directly.

**Winding, derived before looking at the dossier.** The p.1 figure shows a single row of nine leads along the bottom — `I1 I2 I3 I4 I5 I6 I7 I8 GND` numbered 1…9 left→right — and nine along the top — `COMMON O8 O7 O6 O5 O4 O3 O2 O1` numbered 10…18 right→left. Traverse 1→9→10→18 in the figure's own screen frame (x right, y down): shoelace **−2 ⇒ counter-clockwise**. Dossier traverse pad1(−4.65,−5.08) → pad9(−4.65,+5.08) → pad10(+4.65,+5.08) → pad18(+4.65,−5.08), same y-down frame: shoelace **−8 ⇒ counter-clockwise**. **Same chirality. The dossier frame is a 90° ROTATION of the figure, not a mirror.**

**Pin map, 18/18 exact against the p.2 table:** 1–8 = I1–I8, 9 = GND, 10 = COMMON, 11 = O8, 12 = O7, 13 = O6, 14 = O5, 15 = O4, 16 = O3, 17 = O2, 18 = O1. Note the outputs run in REVERSE order and OUTn faces INn across the package — this is the one property that a mirrored or naively-renumbered land would destroy, and it is intact.

**Function ↔ net, all 18 traced through the netlist (not the dossier):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| IN2 (2) | SEL_U2 | U_DECU.14 (Y1) | OUT2 (17) | COIL_U2_N | K_U2.2 |
| IN3 (3) | SEL_U3 | U_DECU.13 (Y2) | OUT3 (16) | COIL_U3_N | K_U3.2 |
| IN4 (4) | SEL_U4 | U_DECU.12 (Y3) | OUT4 (15) | COIL_U4_N | K_U4.2 |
| IN5 (5) | SEL_U5 | U_DECU.11 (Y4) | OUT5 (14) | COIL_U5_N | K_U5.2 |

The **TBD62084A in the same document needs 7.0 V and would not switch**; the ordered code is `C165895` = TBD62083AFWG, correct.

### U_ULNB — TBD62083AFWG — **VERDICT: QUESTION**

Winding, pin map, footprint, GND and COMMON are **byte-identical to U_ULNA** — same footprint string, same local pad table to the 0.01 mm, same pads 9/10 nets. **Pairwise structural symmetry holds.** Channel mapping again exact:

| in | net | driven by | out pad | net | lands on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IN1 (1) | SEL_D3 | U_DECD.13 (Y2) | OUT1 (18) | COIL_D3_N | K_D3.2 |
| IN2 (2) | SEL_D4 | U_DECD.12 (Y3) | OUT2 (17) | COIL_D4_N | K_D4.2 |
| IN3 (3) | PRESS_TIMED | U_ONESHOT.13 (1Q) | OUT3 (16) | COIL_PRESS_N | K_PRESS.2 |

`U_ULNB pin 4 (IN4): tied to GND` — a deliberately terminated spare.

**FINDING (QUESTION) — `U_ULNB` pins 5,6,7,8 (IN5–IN8): four unused DMOS inputs are LEFT FLOATING while their sibling IN4 is TIED TO GND.** Confirmed from the netlist, not inferred: `U_ULNB.5/6/7/8` sit on `unconnected-(U_ULNB-IN5..IN8-PadN)`. The inconsistency is the finding — one spare input got the treatment the other four did not.

Bounding the impact honestly, both ways:
- **Mitigating, measured:** `I_IN(ON) ≤ 0.1 mA at V_IN = 2.5 V` (p.5 EC, Test Circuit 3) implies an **internal resistive path to GND of ≥ 25 kΩ**, corroborating the resistor drawn to ground on the p.2 *"Equivalent circuit (each driver)"*. A floating input is therefore pulled toward 0 V, not left on a pure CMOS gate.
- **Not mitigating:** Toshiba specifies no "input open" behaviour anywhere in the document. The safe state rests on a figure marked *"may be simplified for explanatory purpose"* plus an inference from a leakage number — not on a guaranteed parameter.
- **Consequence ceiling:** OUT4–OUT8 (pads 15,14,13,12,11) are themselves unconnected, so even a channel that latched on drives nothing. **No coil can be energised by this.**

Not a FAIL. It is a QUESTION because the board treats five identical spare inputs two different ways, and the fix (four nets to GND) is one line.

### K_U1, K_U2, K_U3, K_U4, K_U5, K_U6, K_D1, K_D2, K_D3, K_D4, K_PRESS, K_STOP — DIP05-1A72-13L reed relays — **VERDICT: PASS (all 12)**

Datasheet: `02_parts/DIP05-1A72-13L/datasheet-reed-relay-series-dip.pdf`, **p.3 "Pin-Out (Top View) (2.54 mm [0.10"] pitch grid)", sub-figure 13**, rendered at 110 dpi.

**Read off the figure directly, before opening the dossier.** Sub-figure 13 draws four leads: the **switch** spans the two upper verticals labelled **14** (left) and **8** (right); the **coil** spans the two lower verticals labelled **2** (left) and **6** (right). Sub-figure 12, adjacent in the same figure, is visibly different — leads at **14, 8, 1, 7** — which is why the code matters. Cross-read against sub-figure 12's own numbering, the drawing is a horizontal DIP-14 outline: bottom row left→right = positions 1…7, top row left→right = 14…8. Position 2 is one pitch in from the pin-1 end; position 6 is one pitch in from the far end. **CONTACT = leads 8 & 14 (one row). COIL = leads 2 & 6 (the other row).**

**Land-pattern numbering check — the land uses pads 1–4, the datasheet uses leads 2/6/8/14, and NOTHING in the dossier bridges the two numbering domains.** I derived the bridge from the pad geometry against the DIP-14 grid. A DIP-14 row spans 15.24 mm; positions relative to the body centre are −7.62, −5.08, −2.54, 0, +2.54, +5.08, +7.62. Dossier local pads:

| land pad | local (x,y) | column | y vs DIP grid | ⇒ DIP lead | datasheet function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (−3.81, −5.08) | west | position 2 of that row | **2** | COIL_A |
| 2 | (−3.81, +5.08) | west | position 6 of that row | **6** | COIL_B |
| 3 | (+3.81, +7.62) | east | far end | **8** | CONTACT_B |
| 4 | (+3.81, −7.62) | east | pin-1 end | **14** | CONTACT_A |

Column separation 7.62 mm = the DIP row spacing ✓. The netlist's own `pinfunction` strings independently agree (`COIL_A_1`, `COIL_B_2`, `CONTACT_B_3`, `CONTACT_A_4`) — a second, differently-sourced witness.

**Mirror risk is provably NULL for this part, and the reason is worth recording.** The coil is a bare non-polar reed coil (no internal diode on the -13L) and the contact is Form A (SPST-NO) — both are symmetric two-terminal elements. Under the only mirror the land admits, COIL_A↔COIL_B and CONTACT_A↔CONTACT_B swap, which is electrically identical. The **only** fatal error class here is coil and contact ending up in the same column or interleaved — the code-12 failure — and the geometry above rules it out.

**The land is additionally KEYED BY LEAD POSITION.** Rotating the 4-hole pattern 180° about its centre maps pad1(−3.81,−5.08)→(+3.81,+5.08), pad2→(+3.81,−5.08), pad3→(−3.81,−7.62), pad4→(−3.81,+7.62) — **not one of which is an existing hole.** A relay inserted backwards physically does not fit. On a hand-soldered THT part that is the strongest possible assembly guard.

**Coil and contact domains proven disjoint from the netlist, not asserted.** Union of the nets on all 24 coil pads (K_*.1, K_*.2) = {5V_KEY_RELAY, 5V_STOP, COIL_U1..U6_N, COIL_D1..D4_N, COIL_PRESS_N, COIL_STOP_N} — 14 nets. Union of the nets on all 24 contact pads (K_*.3, K_*.4) = {KP_U1..U6, KP_D1..D4, U_SEL_BUS, D_SEL_BUS, RKEY_MID, RSTOP_MID} — 14 nets. **Intersection = ∅.** Taking the transitive closure of the contact set through every resistor on the board, it does not grow: the only refs touching it are `J_KEY_MATRIX`, the twelve relays, `R_KEY` (0 Ω), `R_STOP` (0 Ω) and three test points. **It contains no GND, no 3V3, no 5V_PROTECTED, no 5V_KEY_RELAY, no 5V_STOP, no 3V3_ANALOG.** The keypad contact domain is a closed 14-net island.

Press topology traced end-to-end and coherent: `KP_Ux —(K_Ux)— U_SEL_BUS —(K_PRESS)— RKEY_MID —(R_KEY 0Ω)— D_SEL_BUS —(K_Dy)— KP_Dy`. Three series relays per keystroke. STOP path: `KP_U6 —(K_STOP)— RSTOP_MID —(R_STOP 0Ω)— KP_D1`, i.e. the STOP key sits at matrix intersection (U6, D1) — **both ends inside the isolated island; K_STOP does not breach it.**

Observation, not a finding: `K_STOP` presents CONTACT_A on the keypad line and CONTACT_B on the mid-node, the opposite assignment from the other eleven. On a Form-A contact this is electrically indistinguishable and appears to be a routing choice.

### J_ESTOP — JST SM03B-SRSS-TB, SH 1.00 mm, 3 circuits (C160403) — **VERDICT: QUESTION**

Datasheet: `02_parts/SM03B-SRSS-TB/eSH.pdf` p.3 *Header* table + side-entry drawing, rendered.

Figure confirms: **SM03B-SRSS-TB, side entry, 3 circuits, A = 2.0, B = 5.0 mm.** A = (n−1) × pitch = 2 × 1.00 ✓ **1.00 mm pitch confirmed**; B = 5.0 mm shroud width and 2.9 mm shroud height confirmed. `No. 1 circuit` is marked at the **row end**, which is what the KiCad footprint uses (pad1 x = −1.00, pad3 x = +1.00). 3 pads + 2 mechanical pegs, both pegs on GND. Ratings 1.0 A / 50 V against a 7 mA load — ample.

**Board pin assignment, from the dossier and confirmed in the netlist:**

| circuit | net | netlist witnesses |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `GND` | — |
| 2 | `3V3` | member of the 76-node main 3V3 rail |
| 3 | `ESTOP_RAW_IN` | `D_ESTOP.1`, `R_ESTOPPD.1` (470 Ω→GND), `R_ESTOPS.1` (680 Ω→`ESTOP_RAW`→`U_SCHM.1`) |

**Which two circuits must the shorting plug bridge — derived from the netlist, not from the ADR.** The only circuit that reaches the safety front end is **3** (`ESTOP_RAW_IN`). It sits on a 470 Ω pull-down, so unfitted it reads ≈0 V and, through the **two cascaded SN74HC14 stages** `1A→1Y→2A→2Y` (verified from the U_SCHM dossier: pin1 `ESTOP_RAW`, pin2 `ESTOP_NI`, pin3 `ESTOP_NI`, pin4 `ESTOP_OK`), yields `ESTOP_OK` LOW = restrictive. To assert `ESTOP_OK` the sense circuit must be pulled HIGH, and the only rail present on the connector is circuit 2. **⇒ The plug must bridge circuits 2–3.** Bridge current = 3.3 V / 470 Ω = **7.0 mA**, an order above the ~1 mA dry-circuit threshold, so the crimps get real wetting current.

**FINDING (QUESTION) — a plug mis-crimped into circuits 1–2 is a DEAD SHORT of the main `3V3` rail to `GND`, with no series element anywhere in the path.** Verified from the netlist: `J_ESTOP.2` is a **direct member** of net `3V3` — there is no series resistor, no ferrite, no fuse between the connector pad and the AMS1117 output that feeds 76 nodes. Circuits 1 and 2 are adjacent on a 1.00 mm pitch, and the plug is a hand-built 2-contact bridge in a 3-position housing, so loading the crimps one position over is a single-step build error. Consequence: the AMS1117-3.3 goes into current limit / thermal shutdown, the whole 3V3 domain browns out, and the failure presents as "the board is dead when the E-stop plug is fitted" — the exact symptom a technician would otherwise attribute to the plug being *absent*.

Also checked, and this part is sound: bridging **1–3** (GND to sense) is harmless — it reproduces the unfitted state, `ESTOP_OK` LOW, board inert.

**Cross-housing behaviour, checked independently.** The plug carries metal in circuits 2 and 3 ONLY; circuit 1 is empty. In any foreign 1.25 mm GH or 1.50 mm ZH shroud, SH circuit 3 is ≥0.50 mm off every post against a ~0.30 mm post and cannot engage; circuit 2 may engage a single post but its only partner is circuit 3, which engages nothing. **A loose plug therefore cannot complete a short in any other housing on this board.** Putting the sense node on circuit 3 and GND on circuit 1 is the correct choice and I reach it independently. The residual is confined to the plug's *own* housing, where the mis-crimp is not defended by geometry — it is defended only by the build instruction.

### J_KEY_MATRIX — JST SM10B-GHS-TB, GH 1.25 mm, 10 circuits — **VERDICT: QUESTION**

`eGH.pdf` p.3: **SM10B-GHS-TB, 10 circuits, A = 11.25, B = 15.75**; A = 9 × 1.25 ✓. Footprint pin1 x = −5.625, pin10 x = +5.625 ✓. Pinout `1..6 = KP_U1..KP_U6`, `7..10 = KP_D1..KP_D4` — monotone, no transposition.

**Strong positive finding, recorded because it is easy to lose:** this connector's two mounting pegs carry **`(no net)`**, uniquely on this board — every other JST housing's pegs are on GND. That is the correct and deliberate treatment: grounding them would have bonded the isolated keypad domain to SELV through the shell. Whoever did that understood the barrier.

**FINDING (QUESTION) — the isolated keypad ribbon shares the 1.25 mm GH family with four SELV sensor pods, and a pod plug part-seated here bonds the isolation domains.** A `GHR-05V-S` pod plug (7.50 × 4.15 mm) enters the GH-10 shroud loosely, cannot latch, but its posts align at **exactly 0 mm offset** — the pitch is identical, so engagement is full, not marginal. Seated at circuits 1–5 it lands:

| pod circuit | carries | lands on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `3V3_SW_*` (switched SELV rail) | **KP_U1** |
| 2 | `GND` | **KP_U2** |
| 3 | `SDA_*` | **KP_U3** |
| 4 | `SCL_*` | **KP_U4** |
| 5 | `SHIELD_DRAIN` | **KP_U5** |

This connects the board's SELV ground and 3.3 V rail directly to the appliance's membrane-keypad matrix — defeating the 1.5 kV coil/contact rating and the 6 mm keypad creepage in one insertion, and pushing board rails into the OEM controller's keypad scan lines. It does **not** reach a permission (no safety input is in the GH family), so the ADR-level claim survives; but *permission* is not the only axis, and **isolation is the axis this board's own DRU exists to defend.** Reported as a QUESTION because it is a harness/assembly hazard, not a pin-map defect, and because 1.25 mm GH is the only pitch on the board that appears five times. If a future revision has one keying change to spend, this is where it buys the most.

### J_PI — 2×20 2.54 mm female stacking socket, BOOMELE `2.54-2*20PPC104` (C35165) — **VERDICT: QUESTION**

This is the one part where the dossier's computed winding differs from every other part on the board — **CW, where all 52 others are CCW** — so it was derived from scratch.

**What the datasheet actually contains.** `02_parts/2.54-2x20PPC104/PC104-2x20_C35165.pdf` p.1 (Zhejiang Bomu drawing) was rendered and read. It gives front/side/PCB-layout views, a 2.54 mm grid, ø1.0 mm holes, an 8.5 mm body with 12.46 mm stack-through tails (23.50 mm overall), and a right-hand table whose `PIN` column runs 1…40 against dimensions A and B. **That table is a family size table, not a pin map** — A(n) = 2.54 n and B(n) = 2.54 (n−1), i.e. the body length and pin span for an n-position 2×N part; for 2×20 it yields B = 48.26 mm, which is exactly the dossier's pad1→pad39 span. **The drawing defines NO pin 1, NO numbering convention, and NO keying.** The connector is genuinely positional. `part.yaml` says so too ("this connector part is itself positional"). There is therefore **no datasheet-vs-board mismatch to grade** on this part — the numbering is defined entirely by the netlist, the silk, and the harness.

**The footprint choice, derived independently.** The board uses `Connector_PinSocket_2.54mm:PinSocket_2x20_P2.54mm_Vertical` on `F.Cu` at (94, 95) rot 90. Read directly from the installed KiCad library files:

| footprint | pad 1 | pad 2 | pad 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PinSocket_2x20_P2.54mm_Vertical` | (0, 0) | **(−2.54, 0)** | (0, 2.54) |
| `PinHeader_2x20_P2.54mm_Vertical` | (0, 0) | **(+2.54, 0)** | (0, 2.54) |

KiCad's socket family is **x-mirrored** relative to its header family. Which one is right is a mechanical question, so I resolved it in world coordinates rather than by convention:

- **Direct stack** (Pi right-side-up BELOW, its J8 male posts entering the board's holes from underneath): the mating contacts are **coaxial**, so the board's hole for Pi pin *n* is directly above Pi pin *n*. Looking down at the stack, the two patterns are **identical**, not mirrored ⇒ the board would need the **PinHeader** chirality. Under the socket footprint the board is mirrored and **every odd/even pair swaps: pad1↔Pi2, pad3↔Pi4, pad5↔Pi6, …**
- **Mating part inserted from ABOVE** (a male DIL plug, or a daughterboard flipped face-down onto the socket): take a `PinHeader` footprint on the mating board and flip it about its y-axis — pad2 moves from +2.54 to −2.54. That flipped pattern **is** the `PinSocket` geometry. ⇒ the socket footprint is correct.

So the footprint is correct **iff** the mating part arrives from above.

**FINDING (QUESTION) — `02_parts/2.54-2x20PPC104/part.yaml` contradicts itself on exactly this axis, in the same file.** Its `mates:` field, at the top, reads:

> `mates: receptacle    # female socket (receptacle) — receives the Raspberry Pi 5 J8 MALE GPIO posts from below.`

"…from below" is the **direct-stack** reading, under which the footprint is mirrored. Its own `gotchas:` block, further down the same file, states the opposite — ribbon sidecar, socket top-mounted, board end is a **male DIL IDC transition plug** — and explicitly notes that under direct stacking "the F.Cu footprint would be mirrored and mis-map every pair."

Everything else in the tree sides with the sidecar reading, and two of those witnesses are on the copper itself: the candidate board carries **silk `PI 40-PIN RIBBON (SIDECAR)` at (112, 90)**, the title block reads **`cooksense  SMC0985KS  sidecar v1.7`**, `floorplan.yaml:321` says `2x20 Pi header (SIDECAR, body ALONG south edge)`, and `layout.source` in the same part.yaml cites the sidecar decision by name. **Under the sidecar interconnect the as-built footprint is CORRECT.**

Graded QUESTION rather than FAIL because (a) the datasheet defines no numbering to contradict, (b) the board's own silk declares the intent unambiguously, and (c) the defect is one stale sentence in a document. **It is stated at full strength anyway, because the failure mode is total.** If anyone reads that `mates:` line and builds a direct stack, the resulting map is:

| board pad | intended | would land on | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 (`I2C_SDA`) | Pi 3 (GPIO2) | **Pi 4 (5V)** | **5 V onto a 3V3-powered MCP23017 SDA — exceeds VDD+0.3 V, destructive** |
| 5 (`I2C_SCL`) | Pi 5 (GPIO3) | **Pi 6 (GND)** | I2C clock hard-shorted to ground |
| 6 (`GND`) | Pi 6 | Pi 5 (GPIO3) | Pi GPIO shorted to ground |
| 1, 2 (NC) | — | Pi 2 (5V), Pi 1 (3V3) | harmless |
| every other pair | — | its row partner | GPIOs shorted to grounds throughout |

The logical map itself is correct, checked pin by pin against `01_docs/pin_map.md` and the Pi 5 J8 standard: power pins 1/2/4/17 NC (board is powered from `J_PWR`, no backfeed) ✓; grounds on 6,9,14,20,25,30,34,39 — all eight present and on GND ✓; HAT-ID 27/28 NC ✓; SPI0 on 19/21/23 with CE0=24 (`ADC_CS_N`) and CE1=26 (`TC_CS_N`) ✓; I2C1 on 3/5, I2C2 SDA on 7 with SCL on 29, I2C3 on 8/10 ✓; 12/18/35 NC ✓. **40/40 logical assignments correct.**

**Second residual, from the drawing:** the 12.46 mm stack-through tails protrude ~12 mm **below** the board, and the socket is **unshrouded** — a 180° ribbon insertion is possible and mis-maps every net. Both are build-instruction items, both already named in `part.yaml`.

### U_COMP / U_COMP2 — LMV393IDR dual comparators — **VERDICT: PASS (both)**

Datasheet: `02_parts/LMV393IDR/SLCS136V.pdf` §4 *Pin Configuration and Functions*, **"LMV393 … D, DDU, DGK OR PW PACKAGE (TOP VIEW)"**, p.3, rendered, cross-read against Table 4-1 on the same page.

Figure: `1 = 1OUT, 2 = 1IN−, 3 = 1IN+, 4 = GND, 5 = 2IN+, 6 = 2IN−, 7 = 2OUT, 8 = VCC+`. **Both dossiers match 8/8.** Note the deliberate asymmetry between the two halves in the figure — channel 1 is (IN−, IN+) descending, channel 2 is (IN+, IN−) descending — and **both dossiers reproduce that asymmetry**, which a naive symmetric pin map would have flattened.

**Polarity of every one of the four comparators re-derived from the divider values in the netlist, not read off the intent.** `3V3_ANALOG = 3.300 V`.

- `TCAM_THRESH = 3.300 × R_TH2/(R_TH1+R_TH2) = 3.300 × 10k/78k = **0.4231 V**`
- `TCAM_OPEN  = 3.300 × R_OPENB/(R_OPENT+R_OPENB) = 3.300 × 100k/162k = **2.0370 V**`
- Sensor node open-circuit level `= 3.300 × R_CLMP/(R_REF+R_CLMP) = 3.300 × 22k/32k = **2.2688 V**`

| comparator | IN+ | IN− | pulls TEMP_OK low when | means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U_COMP ch1 | TH_CAM_A | TCAM_THRESH | TH_CAM_A < 0.4231 V | **over-temperature** (NTC low-side, node falls with heat) |
| U_COMP ch2 | TH_CAM_B | TCAM_THRESH | TH_CAM_B < 0.4231 V | over-temperature, channel B |
| U_COMP2 ch1 | TCAM_OPEN | TH_CAM_A | TH_CAM_A > 2.0370 V | **open / disconnected sensor** |
| U_COMP2 ch2 | TCAM_OPEN | TH_CAM_B | TH_CAM_B > 2.0370 V | open sensor, channel B |

All four outputs are open-drain onto the single `TEMP_OK` node (12 nodes, pulled up by `R_TEMPOK` 10 kΩ to 3V3_ANALOG) — a wired-AND, so **any** one condition drives `TEMP_OK` low. Both failure directions are restrictive and were checked at their limits: **sensor open ⇒ 2.2688 V > 2.0370 V ⇒ TEMP_OK LOW** (margin 232 mV); **sensor shorted ⇒ 0 V < 0.4231 V ⇒ TEMP_OK LOW.**

Common-mode range checked, because this part has a hard ceiling: SLCS136V gives V_ICR up to **VCC − ~0.7 V ≈ 2.60 V**. The highest voltage any comparator input can see is the open-sensor level **2.2688 V**, and the highest fixed reference is **2.0370 V** — **both inside range, with 331 mV of headroom on the worst case.** The `R_CLMP` 22 kΩ leg is what buys that headroom; without it an open sensor would float to 3.3 V and put the input out of common mode, where the datasheet does not guarantee the output state.

Threshold converted to a physical number as a cross-check: 0.4231 V at the node corresponds to `NTC ∥ 22 kΩ = 1.471 kΩ`, i.e. `R_NTC = 1.576 kΩ`, i.e. **≈73 °C** on a 10 kΩ B=3950 NTC — between the brief's 65 °C stop and 75 °C hard limit. Plausible; not graded (design math is an S5 item, not a pin-review item).

Hysteresis present and correctly placed: `R_HYS1`/`R_HYS2` 1 MΩ from `TEMP_OK` back to `TH_CAM_A`/`TH_CAM_B`, i.e. into the **non-inverting** input of the over-temp comparators — positive feedback, the correct sign.
