Engineering review verdict
I would not order or fabricate the released v1.0 board as-is. There is one definite schematic-level blocker in the battery input, and the 100 W buck-boost stage needs a substantial layout and voltage-margin revision before I would trust it at full power. The project's automated verification is unusually thorough, but it demonstrates internal consistency, routing completeness, and manufacturability—not electrical, thermal, transient, EMI, or USB-PD performance. The release guide itself still calls for first-power rail checks, PDO interrogation, UVLO testing, and load testing.
This review covered the authored schematic source, released netlist/BOM, component metadata, exact placement coordinates, copper-zone definitions, routing rules, pin dossiers, and verification reports. I did not have a physical board, oscilloscope captures, thermal data, conducted/radiated-emissions results, or USB-PD compliance measurements.
P0 — Must fix before fabrication
1. D1 defeats the intended non-destructive reverse-polarity protection
The intended architecture is:
`battery → fuse → P-channel reverse-polarity MOSFET → TVS and bulk capacitance`
The accepted design decision explicitly describes the TVS after the P-FET.
The implemented schematic instead connects D1's cathode to `VBAT_F`, which is before Q1, and its anode to ground. Q1 then follows D1. D1 is an unidirectional SMBJ15A.
With the battery reversed, `VBAT_F` becomes negative relative to board ground. D1 is then forward-biased, producing a near-short through the 20 A fuse and the TVS. The P-FET may still protect the downstream converters, but the TVS becomes a sacrificial crowbar, and there is no demonstrated coordination showing that the fuse clears before the TVS or PCB copper fails.
Required correction:

1. Move D1 to `VIN`, after Q1, exactly as the design decision specifies.
2. If suppression is also required on the connector side, use a bidirectional TVS or another topology that does not forward-conduct under reverse battery connection.
3. Specify an exact fuse manufacturer and part number, not merely "20 A MINI," and verify its time-current curve and I²t against D1, Q1, the holder, and PCB copper.
4. Test reverse polarity with a current-limited source before connecting a LiPo.

This is an unambiguous schematic correction, not merely a validation recommendation.
2. The 100 W buck-boost power cell is spread far too widely
The project's own layout requirements say the input and output hot loops must be minimal, with capacitors hard against the FETs. The actual placement does not meet that intent.
From the released floorplan:

* Input half-bridge Q4/Q5 centers are approximately 14.8 mm apart.
* Output half-bridge Q6/Q7 centers are approximately 11.2 mm apart.
* The local PD input capacitors C3/C4 are roughly 14–16 mm from Q4 and approximately 29–31 mm from Q5.
* The inductor is another 13–23 mm south of the half-bridge devices.

Even more concerning, each switch node is implemented as a roughly 35.5 mm-long copper region on both F.Cu and B.Cu. That is a very large high-dV/dt antenna for a converter carrying about 12 A at the low-battery 100 W corner.
The declared stack is F.Cu / GND / VIN / B.Cu. Therefore, as an inference from the stack and zones, the large B.Cu switch-node pours sit next to the inner VIN plane, capacitively injecting switching current into the battery rail. The architecture describes B.Cu as principally GND pour and escape routing, but the implemented B.Cu LX regions consume substantial area.
This layout greatly increases the likelihood of:

* LX overshoot and ringing;
* high common-mode and conducted emissions;
* controller and current-sense interference;
* shoot-through caused by gate/source inductance;
* overstress of the already marginal 30 V MOSFETs;
* unstable or load-dependent behavior at 20 V/5 A.

Required correction: re-layout the entire PD power cell as a compact reference-design-style block. Each high-side/low-side FET pair should be directly adjacent; the local ceramic input capacitor, shunt, high-side FET, low-side FET, and ground return should form the smallest possible loop. The inductor should sit immediately between the two half-bridges. Keep LX copper only as large as needed to connect the MOSFET pads and inductor pad, and remove LX pours from B.Cu.
This is not something I would address by adding more stitching or adjusting one route. It needs a placement-level revision.
3. The 30 V MOSFETs and 30 V-standoff TVSs are not a coordinated protection pair
Q2–Q8 are AON6354 30 V MOSFETs. The manufacturer specifies 30 V maximum drain-source voltage.
D6 and D7 are SMAJ30A devices. A 30 V-standoff SMAJ30A does not start breakdown below the MOSFET's 30 V maximum; its breakdown range is approximately 33.3–36.8 V, and its published maximum clamp is about 48.4 V.
Therefore, the TVS does not guarantee that Q4–Q7 remain below their 30 V absolute maximum. The controller's recorded limits also give a 42 V absolute maximum for the bootstrap pins, so severe LX overshoot can threaten the controller as well as the MOSFETs.
The design follows the IP6559 reference suggestion of a "30 V TVS," but the MOSFET, TVS, layout parasitics, and pulse energy still have to be selected as a coordinated system. Following the reference does not make a 48.4 V maximum TVS clamp compatible with a 30 V MOSFET rating.
There is a related implementation omission: the detail design says four 0 Ω gate-resistor tuning footprints are provided, but the authored schematic drives Q4–Q7 directly from HG/LG. The released BOM contains only R7/R8 as 0 Ω parts, and those are the LM5116 current-sense links—not four buck-boost gate resistors.
Recommended correction:

* Reselect the MOSFET and clamp network together. A 60 V MOSFET is the straightforward robust choice; a 40 V part would require a demonstrably lower clamp and sufficient margin to the bootstrap limits.
* Consider different clamp levels for the input-side and output-side half-bridges.
* Add individual gate-resistor footprints immediately at all four MOSFET gates.
* Tune gate resistance and RC snubbers using measured drain-node ringing, not only the reference values.
* Measure VDS with high-bandwidth differential probes at minimum input, maximum input, startup, shutdown, short-circuit recovery, 5 V output, and 20 V/5 A output.

P1 — Full-power and protection concerns
4. The 100 W low-battery operating point has essentially no thermal margin
At 100 W output, 9 V input, and the project's assumed 93% efficiency, the PD converter draws approximately:
Iin = 100 / (9 × 0.93) ≈ 11.95 A
The chosen L1 is rated at 12 A RMS for a 40 °C temperature rise. The project's own part record acknowledges that the 100 W/9 V corner runs at the inductor's RMS rating.
That leaves little or no allowance for:

* component tolerance;
* elevated ambient temperature;
* enclosure heating;
* core loss at the actual switching waveform;
* reduced airflow;
* adjacent MOSFET and shunt heating;
* operation while the USB-A rail is also carrying 6 A.

The design also leaves the IP6559 NTC function unused and relies on the chip's internal 150 °C overtemperature shutdown. That is fault protection, not sensible continuous thermal derating.
Each power MOSFET drain paddle receives only two 0.25/0.15 mm thermal vias to bottom copper. The automated thermal rule merely verifies "at least two vias"; it does not calculate junction temperature or thermal resistance.
Recommended correction:

* Select an inductor with materially higher RMS-current capability—preferably at least 15 A for a 100 W low-battery operating point.
* Add board-temperature sensing and active derating or shutdown. If GPIO0 cannot simultaneously serve PDO selection and NTC, implement an external NTC comparator that disables `EN_PD`.
* Increase thermal spreading under the MOSFETs, with more filled/capped vias where the package and fabrication process permit.
* Define the intended enclosure and ambient-temperature range before claiming continuous 100 W.
* Treat combined 100 W USB-C plus 30 W USB-A as a separate, more severe validation condition.

5. The advertised PDO set is not deterministically configured
R25 is intentionally DNP because the GPIO0 resistor table could not be found in the public documentation. The design assumes the `-C` variant's open-pin default is the desired 100 W source-capability set and requires a PD analyzer during first power.
That is acceptable for an exploratory prototype, but it is not a production configuration mechanism. A silicon revision, lot change, suffix interpretation, or sourcing substitution could produce a different PDO list.
Before sign-off, obtain written confirmation or the application note from Injoinic and populate a deterministic strap. Record the measured source-capabilities message, including fixed PDOs, PPS range, current limits, and cable-dependent behavior, as release evidence.
6. The board is not a complete 3S battery-protection system
The advertised 8.84 V falling threshold is calculated from typical LM5116 UVLO values. The LM5116 rising threshold itself is specified over approximately 1.170–1.262 V around a 1.215 V typical value.
Using the datasheet minimum threshold while retaining the nominal hysteresis and bias-current assumptions already used in the project, plus the adverse 1% resistor corner, gives an illustrative falling threshold of roughly 8.3 V, or about 2.77 V per cell. This is not a complete worst-case bound, but it demonstrates that 8.84 V is not a guaranteed lower cutoff. The project's calculation presently uses only typical values.
More fundamentally, total pack voltage cannot detect a single weak or imbalanced cell. A 3S pack could still total 8.8–9.0 V while one cell has fallen substantially below the others.
After UVLO, the board also remains connected and is expected to draw about 0.5 mA indefinitely.
The product should therefore be marked and documented as requiring a protected, balanced 3S pack with a proper cell-level BMS. For use with unprotected RC LiPo packs, add a hard-disconnect circuit or latching low-voltage cutoff. At minimum, change the input silk from simply "3S LIPO" to "PROTECTED 3S PACK ONLY."
7. USB-C VBUS and CC fault protection is insufficient for a robust product
D3 is a 24 V-standoff SMAJ24A on VBUS. Its breakdown is roughly 26.7–29.5 V, with a published maximum clamp near 38.9 V. That is above the recorded 25 V absolute maximum of the IP6559 output pins and above the 25 V rating of the output polymer capacitor. D3 can absorb transient energy, but it is not a guaranteed low-voltage OVP device for this circuit.
Q8 is a single N-channel MOSFET with drain on the internal converter output and source on connector VBUS. Its body diode therefore provides a path from external `VBUSC` back toward `VOUT_PD` if the connector is externally driven. The release report explicitly accepts the single-FET reference topology, but it does not provide true reverse-current isolation under abnormal source-to-source or cable-fault conditions.
The CC pins are connected directly to the IP6559 and VCONN switch circuitry. The board relies on the controller's integrated "4 kV" protection, with no dedicated system-level IEC ESD or CC short-to-VBUS protector. Dedicated USB-C protection devices add IEC ESD and short-to-VBUS protection specifically for CC and SBU pins.
For a product rather than a bench prototype, add:

* a USB-PD-rated VBUS OVP/eFuse;
* true reverse blocking, normally using back-to-back MOSFETs;
* dedicated CC1/CC2 ESD and short-to-VBUS protection;
* a connector-side transient design whose maximum clamp remains below downstream absolute maxima.

8. The USB-A current claim remains an acknowledged non-standard deviation
The project correctly documents that BC1.2 provides 1.5 A, Apple-style signaling reaches 2.4 A in the legacy ecosystem, and no USB standard grants 2.5 A through a Type-A receptacle.
The selected KH-AF90DIP-112 drawing does not state a contact-current rating. The project's own part record describes USB-A contacts as 1.5 A-class while accepting 2 A continuous and 2.5 A burst as industry practice.
The electrical protection is otherwise sensible: TPS2557 switches are set to a 2.72–3.29 A limit window, and the TPS2513A advertises recognized charging modes.
Before retaining the "2 A continuous / 2.5 A burst" marking, either select a receptacle with an explicit rating of at least that value or perform connector temperature-rise and contact-resistance testing over insertion life. The board should also be labeled charging only.
P2 — Design robustness and release quality
9. Fuse, inrush, and independent overvoltage protection need more work
The board's high-current copper is described as a 16 A continuous path, while the only input overcurrent device is an unspecified 20 A automotive blade fuse. A 20 A fuse is not guaranteed to open at 20 A, and can carry substantially more current for a meaningful period according to its particular time-current curve. Protection should be coordinated to the conductor and fault-energy limits, not selected only as 1.25 times nominal load.
The design also explicitly accepts direct XT60 hot-plug into at least 200 µF of polymer bulk plus the converter capacitors. That will produce connector arcing and potentially severe inrush unless limited by wiring and cell impedance.
I recommend an input hot-swap/eFuse or controlled precharge, an exact fuse MPN with documented clearing behavior, and an independent USB-A rail OVP stage. A shorted LM5116 high-side MOSFET can otherwise place approximately the battery voltage on `5VA`; the TPS2557 devices provide current limiting but are not an independent 5 V overvoltage barrier.
10. The implemented capacitor set does not match the compensation design documentation
The LM5116 design document calls for four 100 µF 6.3 V X6S/X7R capacitors in 1812—or five capacitors following the TI design. The released BOM instead uses four 100 µF, 16 V, X5R parts in 1210.
That is not automatically wrong, but effective capacitance, ESR, ripple capability, and temperature behavior are different. Because the compensation values were copied from another worked design, the actual plant should be verified with the real capacitors, including DC-bias derating.
There is also a PD-stage decoupling discrepancy. The detail design calls for a local 100 µF input capacitor, 100 nF, and two 10 µF ceramics. The source shows only C3 = 10 µF and C4 = 100 nF locally on `VIN`; the two 100 µF capacitors are farther away at the board input.
Update the design calculations to the exact BOM, add the missing local PD capacitance during the power-cell re-layout, and verify both converters with loop-gain or at least comprehensive load-step measurements.
11. Several release documents contradict the implemented design
Examples include:

* `ARCHITECTURE.md` still describes a TLV431/Q13 UVLO circuit, while the accepted design and schematic use LM5116 UVLO plus the 5VA-derived IP6559 enable.
* `DETAIL_DESIGN.md` says four gate-resistor tuning footprints are present; they are absent.
* The detail document names the non-A TPS2513, while the released BOM correctly changed to TPS2513A.
* The supposedly independent U1 pin dossier reports "MPN unknown," "datasheet none," and "part.yaml none," despite the repository containing those records.
* The release reports DRC 0/0/0 and all-green automated gates, yet still requires all substantive electrical testing after fabrication.

This matters because the D1 problem is exactly the type of internally consistent but functionally wrong connection that DRC and parity checks will not detect.
The release process should have explicit electrical invariants, for example:

* `D1 cathode == VIN`, never `VBAT_F`;
* four PD gate-resistor footprints must exist;
* PDO configuration must be populated or vendor-verified;
* architecture component references must resolve to current schematic references;
* every pin-review dossier must include its MPN and datasheet revision.

Important functional clarification
Despite its name, this is not a USB data hub. The USB-A D+/D− pins terminate in TPS2513A charging-signature controllers, and the USB-C D+/D− pins are used by the charging controller. There is no upstream USB port or hub-controller IC. It is a four-port battery-powered USB charging power distributor.
What is good in the design
Several parts of the engineering process are notably strong:

* The architecture and current domains are explicitly documented.
* The four-layer approach includes a solid inner ground plane.
* Both IP6559 shunts have explicit Kelvin-sense networks.
* Each USB-A port has independent current limiting and data-line ESD.
* The USB-C design includes the e-marker VCONN switching circuit.
* Footprint polarity, connector pinouts, count parity, and manufacturing-tier requirements received substantially more attention than in a typical hobby design.
* The first-power procedure is cautious and correctly starts from a current-limited bench supply.

Those strengths make the project a solid basis for a revised board. They do not, however, cancel the input-TVS error or the buck-boost layout and margin problems.
Minimum v1.1 respin checklist

1. Move D1 after Q1 and coordinate the exact fuse/TVS/fault-energy combination.
2. Re-place the complete IP6559 power cell as a compact unit; eliminate the large B.Cu LX pours.
3. Add four gate-resistor footprints and retune gates/snubber values from measurements.
4. Select MOSFETs and clamps with guaranteed transient margin; strongly consider 60 V power FETs.
5. Add local PD input bulk/ceramic capacitance and revise the thermal-via strategy.
6. Add temperature-based PD derating and require a protected, balanced 3S pack.
7. Lock the PDO configuration with vendor documentation and a deterministic strap.
8. Add USB-C CC protection, VBUS OVP, and reverse-current blocking.
9. Resolve the USB-A connector current rating or reduce the published current.
10. Synchronize architecture, schematic, BOM, and verification dossiers before generating a new release.

Validation required before claiming 100 W
A revised board should pass, at minimum:

* controlled startup, shutdown, hot-plug, and short-circuit recovery;
* PD source-capability capture with e-marked and non-e-marked cables;
* LX1/LX2 and every MOSFET VDS measurement at all input/output corners;
* gate-to-source measurements for ringing and false turn-on;
* 5 V, 9 V, 12 V, and 20 V load steps;
* 100 W continuous thermal testing at minimum input voltage in the intended enclosure;
* simultaneous 100 W USB-C plus 6 A USB-A testing if 130 W aggregate remains a claim;
* UVLO sweeps at temperature and resistor-tolerance extremes;
* USB-A connector temperature rise at 2 A and 2.5 A;
* USB-C VBUS overvoltage, backfeed, CC short-to-VBUS, and ESD testing;
* conducted and radiated EMI pre-compliance testing.

Bottom line: the documentation and automation are impressive, but v1.0 is not ready to be treated as a validated 100 W product. D1 must be corrected under all circumstances, and I would not run the present PD layout anywhere near 100 W without a power-cell re-layout and significantly improved transient and thermal margin.
