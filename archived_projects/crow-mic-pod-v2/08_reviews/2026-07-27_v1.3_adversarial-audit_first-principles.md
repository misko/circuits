## 0. PoE — the ADR-0005 audit (lead item)

**Every RJ45 contact traced to what it reaches** (from the sealed netlist,
`nets.py` output; `<none>` = no net):

| contact | net | reaches | series impedance to the first active thing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AUDIO_P | `{J1.1, D1.3, C5.2, TP3.1}` | **zero** to D1 (TPD2E2U06 ch1); C5 (10 µF) blocks DC to U1's output |
| 2 | AUDIO_N | `{J1.2, D1.5, C6.2, TP4.1}` | **zero** to D1 ch2; C6 blocks DC |
| 3 | 5V_BEEP | `{J1.3, LS1.1, D2.1, D3.1, TP5.1}` | **zero** to the LS1 coil, the SS14 cathode and the SMAJ6.0A cathode |
| 4, 5 | 5V_AUDIO | `{J1.4, J1.5, U1.8, R1.1, R4.1, C7.1, C8.1, C9.1, C10.1, TP1.1}` | **ZERO to U1 pin 8 (V+)** |
| 6 | BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN | `{J1.6, LS1.2, D2.2, D3.2, TP6.1}` | zero to the coil / diode anodes |
| 7, 8 | GND | `{J1.7, J1.8, U1.4, D1.4, MK1.2, C1.2, C3.2, C7.2, C8.2, C9.2, C10.2, R5.2, TP2.1}` | zero |
| 9–12 (LED) | none | nothing | — |
| SH ×2 | none | nothing (floats, ADR-0001 dec. 4) | — |

**Nothing clamps 48 V.** D1 clamps only contacts 1 and 2, at VBR 6.5–8.5 V, and
it is an 8/20 µs pulse-rated SOT-553, not a sustained-power part. There is no
series element anywhere between an RJ45 contact and a semiconductor. **The
answer to "does 48 V arrive at U1's input" is: 48 V arrives at U1's SUPPLY pin,
directly, and the operator must not do that.** ADR-0005 says this and it is
correct.

### Finding POE-1 (P1) — ADR-0005's hazard is real, but its stated MECHANISM is wrong for the most-cited carrier, and its residual-risk list is incomplete

Two corrections, both measured from the shipped design. They cut in opposite
directions; neither removes the constraint.

**(a) A standards-compliant 802.3af/at/bt PSE will NOT energise this pod.**
ADR-0005 and ORDER_README §0 both say flatly "a PoE switch drives 44–57 V into
V+". A compliant PSE does resistive detection first (IEEE 802.3 §33.3.4): it
applies 2.8–10 V across the pair and requires a **23.75–26.25 kΩ** signature
with a signature capacitance **≤ 0.12 µF** before it applies 48 V. This pod
presents, between contacts 4/5 and 7/8:

- DC: R4+R5 = 44.0 kΩ in parallel with the U1 supply branch. U1 is
  **2 mA typ / 2.5 mA max per channel** (SBOS855E Electrical Characteristics
  p.7, IQ row) = 4 mA for the package, so the two-point slope over the
  detection window is ≈ **1 kΩ**, ~24× below the signature floor — read as a
  short/invalid PD.
- Capacitance: C8+C9 = 20 µF nominal on the rail (plus C7+C10 = 0.2 µF), i.e.
  **~100× the maximum valid signature capacitance**, which independently
  disqualifies the port.

The dominant real-world carrier is therefore a **passive / "legacy" / forced-power
injector** — the 24 V and 48 V bricks used in exactly the outdoor CCTV and
wireless-AP installs the ADR names — which skip detection entirely. The
deployment constraint is unchanged; the sentence that justifies it should name
the right device, because "we only use managed switches here" is a false comfort
that the current wording invites.

What a compliant PSE **does** do is apply **up to 10 V** during detection, every
~2 s, indefinitely. That reaches **C1 (100 µF, rated 6.3 V — CL32A107MQVNNNE /
C49066)** through R1 = 100 Ω: a 1.6× overvoltage on an X5R, below its dielectric
withstand but outside its rating, repeated forever.

**(b) The residual-risk paragraph names U1 and D1. The MLCC ignition path is
larger and is not stated.** Under passive 48 V injection on 4/5 vs 7/8:

| part | rating | stress | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** (100 µF, C49066) | **6.3 V** | 48 V through R1 = 100 Ω | **7.6× rating** → fails short; R1 (0603, 100 Ω) then dissipates 48²/100 = **23 W** |
| **C8, C9** (10 µF, C15850) | 25 V | 48 V, cable R only (0.64 Ω) | **1.9× rating** → fail short across a 48 V source with ~0.6 Ω of series resistance |
| C3, C4, C5, C6 | 25 V | ≤48 V depending on path | same class |
| C7, C10 (C14663) | 50 V | 48 V | survive |
| U1 | VS abs-max 40 V | 48–57 V | destroyed (ADR-0005 states this) |
| D1 | pulse-rated | ~13 W Mode-A | destroyed (ADR-0005 states this) |

A passive PoE brick is a 0.5–2 A source. A shorted 0805/1210 MLCC carrying
0.5–2 A at 48 V is the documented MLCC flame-failure mode, and it happens inside
a sealed outdoor enclosure. **ADR-0005's residual-risk paragraph should say
"U1, D1, and C1/C8/C9 — with an ignition path through a shorted MLCC and a 23 W
0603" rather than "U1 and D1 will be destroyed".**

**Disposition asked for:** none of this changes the accepted waiver. It changes
two sentences that ride with the boards. If the array will ever share a rack or
a conduit with an outdoor AP/camera install, that is where the passive injector
lives, and that is the sentence the installer needs.

## 1. P1 findings

### CAL-1 (P1) — the on-board calibration transducer, at its designed drive level, is at or above the preamp's clipping ceiling

This is the finding that matters. It is a system-level defect that no gate on
this board could see, because both halves of it are correct in isolation.

**The source level.** LS1 (`CMT-8504-100-SMT-TR`) is spec'd
**SPL ≥ 100 dB at 10 cm** at rated 5.0 Vo-p / 4 kHz / ½ duty (datasheet rev 1.04
SPECIFICATIONS table, page 1 — re-read from the PDF this session, not quoted
from part.yaml). Measured placement, from the sealed board:

```
LS1 at (33.000, 46.000)   MK1 at (74.000, 26.000)   centre-to-centre 45.62 mm
```

Free-field inverse-square from the 10 cm reference: 100 + 20·log10(100/45.62)
= **106.8 dB SPL at the capsule** at rated drive, **106.5 dB** after the 0.19 V
cable IR drop ARCHITECTURE.md computes for the beep pair. Both source and
receiver are acoustically small at 4 kHz (ka ≈ 0.3), so there is no useful
off-axis correction; a sealed enclosure adds reverberant gain, it does not
subtract. This is the datasheet **minimum**.

**The ceiling.** U1's linear input common-mode window is
**(V−)+0.5 to (V+)−2** (SBOS855E Electrical Characteristics, VCM row, p.7). With
V+ = 5.00 and VMID = 2.500 the positive headroom at INP is **+0.500 Vpk**, which
binds 2.3× before the output swing does ((V−)+0.8 to (V+)−0.8, same table).
`02_parts/OPA1678IDR/part.yaml` computes the resulting ceiling as
**108.9 dB typ / 105.9 at +3 dB / 103.4 worst-case** using the AOM-5024's
datasheet sensitivity of −24 dB re 1 V/Pa.

**That sensitivity is measured at a different operating point than this board
uses** (see GAIN-1): the datasheet figure is taken with **RL = 2.2 kΩ**, this
board uses **R2 = 3.9 kΩ**, and the capsule's own output impedance is 2.2 kΩ.
Terminal sensitivity therefore rises by 3.9/(3.9+2.2) ÷ 2.2/(2.2+2.2) =
**+2.14 dB → 80.7 mV/Pa**, and the ceiling drops by the same 2.14 dB:

| case | VMID | headroom | ceiling, part.yaml's S | **ceiling, this board's S** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nominal, V+ = 5.00 | 2.500 V | +0.500 Vpk | 108.95 dB SPL | **106.81 dB SPL** |
| S +3 dB (mic tol), V+ = 5.00 | 2.500 V | +0.500 Vpk | 105.95 | **103.81** |
| S +3 dB, V+ = 4.75 (−5 %) | 2.375 V | +0.375 Vpk | 103.45 | **101.31** |

**106.8 dB SPL of stimulus into a 101.3–106.8 dB SPL ceiling.** The calibration
burst — the signal the entire array's inter-pod time alignment is built on — is
the loudest thing this pod will ever hear, and it saturates its own channel by
0 to 5.5 dB.

**Three aggravations, all measured:**

1. The mic sensitivity is spec'd at **3 V**, and this board runs the capsule at
   **≈4.95 V**. The datasheet's own "Decreasing Voltage (3VS to 2VS) −3 dB" row
   says sensitivity is supply-dependent and rising. The +2.14 dB above is from
   the load change ALONE; the supply change moves it further the same way.
2. The 150 mA burst and the 107 dB SPL arrive **at the same instant**. Six pods
   beeping together draw ~900 mA from the shared 5 V. Any sag that causes at the
   central lowers V+ at the pod, which lowers VMID, which lowers the ceiling —
   at exactly the moment the SPL peaks. The table's V+ = 4.75 row is not a
   hypothetical corner; it is the burst.
3. There is **no drive-level control anywhere in the system**. The pod's beep
   pair is a bare coil across contacts 3/6 with a low-side switch at the
   central; the drive is 5 V by construction (BRIEF: "5V ~150mA coded 4kHz
   bursts").

**Mitigation quality.** OPA167x has internal phase-reversal protection
(SBOS855E §7.3.1, confirmed) so it clips at the rail rather than inverting —
nothing is damaged. But a clipped burst is level-dependent and **per-pod**
(each capsule's ±3 dB sensitivity tolerance puts its clipping onset in a
different place), which converts a common calibration stimulus into six
differently-distorted waveforms. For an array whose purpose is cross-channel
time-of-arrival, that is the wrong failure to accept silently.

**The fix is already written down and is two resistors.**
`02_parts/OPA1678IDR/part.yaml` names it: re-centre VMID with
**R4 = 33 k / R5 = 18 k → 1.76 V**. Re-derived here with this board's
sensitivity: the ceiling becomes ~113.7 dB nominal and **~110.2 dB worst-case
(+3 dB sensitivity, V+ = 4.75)** — the binding limit moves from the input CM
window to the output swing, which is the correct place for it — clearing the
106.8 dB burst by **3.4 dB in the worst case** instead of missing it by 5.5 dB.
Negative headroom stays adequate (1.26 Vpk vs the 1.11 Vpk output limit).

**Question for the user:** order v1.3 and accept a calibration burst that clips
its own preamp, or spend a v1.4 on `R4 = 33 k / R5 = 18 k`? Everything else on
the board is unaffected — same footprints, same CPL, two BOM values.

### PSR-1 (P1) — the pod has essentially no low-frequency power-supply rejection, and no ripple spec exists on the cable contract

Nobody computed this. Full small-signal nodal analysis of the shipped topology
(`ac2.py`; ideal op-amps, mic as an EMF behind its 2.2 kΩ, 10 kΩ far-end load
per DETAIL_DESIGN.md), rail ripple on 5V_AUDIO → AUDIO_P−AUDIO_N:

| f | Vdiff/Vrail | via the mic-bias RC alone | via VMID alone | **acoustic equivalent of 1 mV of rail ripple** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Hz | −5.02 dB | −3.75 dB | −17.91 dB | **41.5 dB SPL** |
| 50 Hz | −11.09 | −9.91 | −26.99 | **35.3 dB SPL** |
| 60 Hz | −12.54 | −11.36 | −28.70 | **33.9 dB SPL** |
| 100 Hz | −16.78 | −15.60 | −33.35 | **29.6 dB SPL** |
| 120 Hz | −18.32 | −17.15 | −34.97 | **28.1 dB SPL** |
| 1 kHz | −36.66 | −35.49 | −53.48 | 9.7 dB SPL |
| 4 kHz | −48.70 | −47.53 | −65.52 | −2.3 dB SPL |

**One millivolt of 60 Hz on the pod's 5 V rail is acoustically indistinguishable
from a 34 dB SPL tone at the microphone.** Rural ambient at night is
25–30 dBA. And it is **coherent across all six pods** (one supply, one cable
harness) — which is the single worst kind of interference for an array that
cross-correlates channels, because it produces a spurious zero-lag peak.

Two mechanisms, both structural:

1. **Dominant — the mic-bias RC is far too slow to matter at mains
   frequencies.** R1·C1 = 100 Ω × 100 µF puts the corner at 16 Hz, so at 60 Hz
   it delivers only **−11.8 dB**. The R2/Z_mic divider then gives −8.9 dB, and
   the chain gain gives back +9.5 dB. Net −11.4 dB.
2. **Secondary but structural — VMID gets ZERO differential rejection.** The
   measured VMID→Vdiff gain is **2.92 V/V**, against a signal gain of
   **2.97 V/V**: 0.2 dB of rejection. The topology looks like it should cancel —
   stage B's +in sits on VMID, so VMID ought to be common-mode — but stage A's
   gain leg (R7→C4) *returns* to VMID, which makes VMID appear at OUTA with
   gain −0.5 while it appears at OUTB with gain +2. The two legs move in
   opposite directions. It cancels exactly at DC (where R3 holds +inA on VMID)
   and not at all in the audio band (where C2 shunts +inA to the capsule's
   2.2 kΩ). **The "active balanced driver" does not reject its own reference.**
   C3 (10 µF) is what keeps this from mattering more — it filters VMID by
   −38 dB at 60 Hz — but C3's impedance at 1 kHz is 15.9 Ω, not milliohms, so
   VMID is a moderately low-Z node amplified with full signal gain, not a
   ground.

**What is being asked for:** a number. The cable contract in ARCHITECTURE.md
specifies pin↔net↔direction and an IR budget. It does not specify a **ripple
spec on 5V_AUDIO at the pod**. Given the table above, the requirement to keep
supply-borne hum below the microphone's own 14 dB(A) self-noise is
**< 50 µV at 60 Hz at the pod connector**, over 25 ft of cable, from a shared
external switching adapter, on a board carrying two switching regulators. That
requirement has never been stated and almost certainly is not met by accident.
It belongs in the interface table, and the answer may be that the central needs
a per-port RC or a local LDO for the pod rails.

(This is a design-margin finding, not a defect: with a genuinely quiet rail the
board is fine. But nothing in the repo establishes the rail is quiet, and the
board has no local regulation of any kind — D2 in the BRIEF: "the pod holds no
energy source, no converter".)

### DC-1 (P1) — AUDIO_P and AUDIO_N have no DC return path on the pod; the interface contract never says the central must provide one

From the sealed netlist:

```
AUDIO_P  (4)  C5.2  D1.3  J1.1  TP3.1
AUDIO_N  (4)  C6.2  D1.5  J1.2  TP4.1
```

There is **no resistor to ground on either net.** The cable-side plate of each
10 µF coupling cap is held only by D1's leakage (TPD2E2U06, < 10 µA at 5.5 V,
nA in practice) and the caps' own insulation resistance (> 10 GΩ). If the
central's input is **also** AC-coupled, this node is the classic
two-caps-in-series-with-no-bias-resistor float: its DC potential is set by a
leakage ratio, it drifts for minutes after power-up, and it produces a slow
thump into the recorder every time the cable is plugged.

DETAIL_DESIGN.md's output-network table quietly assumes otherwise — "fc into
~10k central = 1.6 Hz" — which only holds if that 10 kΩ goes to something with a
DC path. **That assumption is load-bearing and it is not in the interface
contract.** ARCHITECTURE.md's "Interfaces (the shared cable contract)" table has
no column for the DC termination the central owes each audio leg.

Not order-blocking (the pod needs no change if the central provides it), but it
must be answered against the sibling CENTRAL board before the array is built,
and it should be written into the interface table either way. Note that TP3/TP4
are exposed 1.5 mm pads on a floating DC node — probing them injects leakage.

### MECH-1 (P1) — the enclosure overhang number in ORDER_README is courtyard-derived and understates the real one by 0.55 mm

`ORDER_README.md` §4 tells the enclosure designer: *"The RJ45 mating face sits
**1.05 mm** behind the PCB's own west edge (measured)."* Today's order-readiness
audit re-derived 0.955 mm the same way. Both measure the **courtyard**.

Measured from the footprint's **body outline** (F.Fab — the layer that models
the physical part; `Connector_RJ:RJ45_Amphenol_RJHSE538X`, local body extents
x −4.695…11.815, y −8.000…7.750, mouth on the snap-post face at local y = −8.0):

```
J1 at (19.500, 32.000) rot 90 → local (x,y) maps to board offset (y, −x)
  body mouth face   local y = −8.000  → board x = 11.500
  courtyard face    local y = −8.500  → board x = 11.000  (= courtyard bbox 10.955 + stroke)
  Edge.Cuts west    centreline x = 10.000 ; outer face of the 0.1 mm stroke x = 9.950
```

**PCB overhang past the RJ45 mating face = 1.500 mm to the edge centreline,
1.550 mm to the outer edge.** The 1.05 mm figure is the courtyard, which is
0.5 mm proud of the body by construction.

Why the 0.55 mm matters: the enclosure wall must sit **outside** the PCB edge, so
the plug's insertion recess is overhang + wall thickness. At 1.55 mm + a 2 mm
wall the plug front face is **3.55 mm** inside the outer surface — and a standard
8P8C plug's release latch is a 4–5 mm cantilever on top of the plug. A 3.55 mm
recess makes the latch difficult or impossible to depress for removal. The
board has no notch at the mouth (Edge.Cuts is a plain 80.000 × 45.000 rectangle
— four segments, verified).

**What the enclosure must accommodate** (there is still no enclosure CAD
anywhere in the repo — confirmed by `find`; the only `.step` files are the board
body and JLC component meshes):

- panel cutout ≥ the datasheet's 16.89 × 13.46 mm at the mouth, **plus** clearance
  for 1.55 mm of PCB in front of it, **plus** finger/latch access;
- MK1 is a **9.7 mm dia × 5 mm** capsule at (74.0, 26.0) standing on the board —
  its sound port faces **up**, away from the PCB, so the enclosure needs an
  acoustic opening on the **lid**, not the wall, plus a wind screen for an
  outdoor pole mount;
- LS1 is 8.5 × 8.5 × 4.0 mm at (33.0, 46.0), also top-ported — it needs its own
  vent, and it must not be sealed against the lid;
- H1–H4 M3 holes at (14,14) (86,14) (14,51) (86,51);
- the board is 80.000 × 45.000 mm with 1.6 mm nominal thickness;
- the pod is outdoors: nothing in this design is conformally coated, and the
  audio pair sits on a DC-floating node (DC-1) that leakage across a damp
  surface will bias.

## 2. P2 findings

### GAIN-1 — R2 = 3.9 kΩ is 1.77× the capsule's reference load, and DETAIL_DESIGN's "confirm Idss from datasheet" TODO was never closed

`DETAIL_DESIGN.md` derives R2 = 3.9 kΩ with the parenthetical
*"(confirm Idss from datasheet)"*. That confirmation never happened, and R2 was
never checked against the capsule's **reference load**. The AOM-5024 datasheet
(p.1) states sensitivity −24 ±3 dB re 1 V/Pa and current consumption 500 µA
**both at 3 VS with RL = 2.2 kΩ**, and output impedance 2.2 kΩ. R2 is the DC
bias resistor **and** the AC load, so raising it from 2.2 k to 3.9 k raises the
terminal sensitivity by **+2.14 dB (63.1 → 80.7 mV/Pa)**. That is the entire
source of CAL-1's 2.14 dB ceiling shift.

Consequences that follow, all measured with `ac2.py`:

- Differential gain **from MIC_OUT** = **2.970 V/V** at 1 kHz → **G2 (~3 V/V
  differential) is MET.** Confirmed.
- Differential gain **from the capsule's open-circuit EMF** = **1.873 V/V**,
  because R2 loads the 2.2 kΩ source. Not a defect — but if anyone ever
  re-derives the chain from the capsule rather than from MIC_OUT they will find
  a "missing" 4.0 dB. Worth one sentence in DETAIL_DESIGN.
- The 500 µA / 3.9 kΩ operating point puts the drain at ≈3.0 V on a 4.95 V
  rail — comfortable, ~2 V of headroom each way. No issue.

### CAP-1 — C1 is a 6.3 V part on a 4.95 V node, and its DC-bias derating costs ~10 dB of the rail rejection that PSR-1 already finds marginal

`C1 = CL32A107MQVNNNE / C49066`, 100 µF **6.3 V** X5R 1210, sitting on VMIC_F
at ≈4.95 V. Two problems:

1. **Voltage derating 1.27×.** Thin for a node fed directly from a 25 ft
   outdoor cable with no series protection (and see POE-1: this is the part
   that a compliant PSE's 10 V detection probe over-volts, and the part that
   fails short first under passive injection).
2. **DC-bias derating.** A 1210 X5R 100 µF/6.3 V at 4.95 V typically retains
   20–30 % of nameplate. Re-running the analysis at C1 = 25 µF (and the 25 V
   10 µF parts at 8 µF):

| f | Vdiff/Vrail nominal | **derated** | acoustic equivalent per 1 mV, derated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 Hz | −12.54 dB | **−2.73 dB** | **43.7 dB SPL** (vs 33.9) |
| 100 Hz | −16.78 | **−5.32** | **41.1 dB SPL** |
| 1 kHz | −36.66 | **−23.81** | 22.6 dB SPL |

DETAIL_DESIGN.md's "fc = 1/(2π·100·100 µ) = 16 Hz" is a nameplate calculation;
the in-circuit corner is **~64 Hz**. Nothing in the repo records that the
derating was considered. A 100 µF/16 V or 25 V 1210 in the same land would
remove most of it.

### PWR-1 — ARCHITECTURE.md's pod current budget is wrong by 2.3× typical, and its stated maximum is exceeded by the op-amp alone

ARCHITECTURE.md's power table: *"5V_AUDIO … On-pod load ~2 mA typ, <5 mA max"*.
ADR-0001 dec. 5 leans on the same number ("~5 mA load makes a 0.3 V Schottky
drop … pointless").

Measured from datasheets: **U1 IQ = 2 mA typ / 2.5 mA max per channel, 2.8 mA
max over −40…125 °C** (SBOS855E p.7). OPA1678 is a **dual** and both halves are
used, so U1 alone is **4.0 mA typ / 5.0 mA max / 5.6 mA over temperature**. Add
the VMID divider (5 V / 44 kΩ = 0.114 mA) and the mic branch (0.5 mA):

**pod draw = 4.6 mA typ / 6.2 mA worst case**, not 2 mA typ / <5 mA max.

Physically harmless — the round-trip cable IR at 6.2 mA is **4.0 mV** over the
0.64 Ω that ARCHITECTURE computes, and V+ at the pod stays ≥ 4.89 V against U1's
4.5 V single-supply floor. But it is a wrong number in the document that the
protection ADR cites as its justification, and it is the number that feeds the
central's 6-pod budget.

### POUR-1 — the BEEP netclass is declared at 0.4 mm expressly to stay under R-POUR's threshold, and the shipped copper is exactly 0.5 mm

`03_src/rules/nets.yaml`, BEEP class, verbatim:

```
    min_width: 0.4mm     # 150mA over a short SW-corner loop needs no pour (0.4mm
                         # ~0.9A capacity, 1oz); kept < 0.5 so R-POUR (>=0.5mm =
                         # "high-current, must pour") does not fire
```

Measured on the sealed board (`geom2.py`), per-net track widths:

```
 5V_BEEP                  widths={0.5: 19}   vias=4
 BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN     widths={0.5: 24}   vias=0
```

**All 43 beep segments are 0.5 mm.** `policy_audit.py` R-POUR (lines 547–572)
reads `track_width` from the netclass in `.kicad_pro` — which is 0.4 — not the
copper, so the class is never classified high-current and the rule is never
evaluated against the artifact. The comment in the source says out loud that the
declaration was chosen to keep the rule from firing.

Electrically this is nothing: wider copper is strictly better for a 150 mA
burst, and a dedicated beep pour on a 2-layer board would be worse. It is filed
as a **gate-coverage gap**: R-POUR's premise ("the netclass is the design's own
declaration of what carries current") is defeated whenever the declaration and
the routing disagree, and this board is the existence proof. A one-line
cross-check — *actual max track width per netclass vs the declared floor* —
would close it fleet-wide.

### SEP-1 — the "beep pair never shares copper with / never crosses the analog cell" invariant is false against the artifact (electrically nil, but stated as a fact)

`nets.yaml`: *"this pair NEVER shares copper with the analog ground on the
pod"*. `floorplan.yaml`: *"the switched return runs straight to J1 pins 3,6
without crossing the analog cell"*. Measured minimum separations
(`clearance3.py`, analytic capsule/rect geometry; the sub-0.15 mm figures carry
~0.1 mm of square-end approximation error and DRC at 0.127 mm passed, so read
them as "at the DRC floor"):

| pair | separation | where |
|---|---|---|
| BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN ↔ **AUDIO_N** (same layer, F.Cu) | **≈0.13 mm** | F.Cu beep track (22.10,32.00)→(22.50,31.60) against pad **J1.2** — the beep return routes *through the RJ45 audio-tail field* |
| BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN ↔ 5V_AUDIO (same layer) | ≈0.15 mm | beep track (22.60,28.20)→(21.30,26.90) against pad J1.4 |
| AUDIO_P ↔ BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN (same layer) | 0.250 mm | F.Cu beep (39.60,45.60)→(43.60,45.60) against an AUDIO_P via at (41.40,44.80) — **the audio pair's stubs to TP3/TP4 run through the beep block** |
| **5V_AUDIO ↔ 5V_BEEP** (cross-layer) | ~0 mm in XY | a **5V_AUDIO B.Cu track at x = 33.5, y 45.4…47.8 runs directly under the LS1 body** and crosses the F.Cu 5V_BEEP diagonal |
| AUDIO_P ↔ 5V_BEEP (cross-layer) | 0.025 mm in XY | broadside crossing at (41.4, 47) |

**Coupling, computed rather than asserted:** the tightest same-layer approach is
a 0.57 mm run of 0.5 mm track at ~0.13 mm — edge capacitance including fringe
≈ **0.02 pF**. At the brief's deliberately slow beep edges (≈1 V/µs) that is
**≈0.1 nA** into a 100 Ω-ish driven node: **~10 nV**. The cross-layer crossings
through 1.6 mm of FR4 are ~0.003 pF. Inductive coupling between the beep loop
and the input loop, estimated at M ≈ 0.3 nH and di/dt = 1.5×10⁵ A/s, is ~45 µV.

**And here is the answer to the "does the buzzer couple into the preamp"
question, which is the useful result:** the beeper only injects while it is
deliberately producing **355 mV at MIC_OUT** of intended acoustic signal
(106.8 dB SPL × 80.7 mV/Pa). Every electrical crosstalk path measured above is
**≥ 70 dB below the acoustic signal it accompanies.** *The buzzer is not a
coupling problem on this board. It is a headroom problem (CAL-1).*

So SEP-1 is a **claim-vs-artifact defect, not an electrical one**: the
invariants in `nets.yaml` and `floorplan.yaml` are written as facts, they are
false, and nothing checks them. Either soften them to what was actually
achieved, or make them machine-checked (a min-separation assertion between named
netclasses is a natural E-INV).

### MK1-1 — the vendored electret footprint is not derived from the capsule's terminal geometry, and its own description contradicts part.yaml

`03_src/lib/crow_mic_pod.pretty/AOM-5024L-HD-R.kicad_mod` is two 0.9 mm THT
holes at 2.54 mm pitch with a 4.85 mm silk circle and a 5.20 mm courtyard.
Its `descr` says: *"Hand-wired: capsule **wire leads** solder to these two THT
pads"*. `02_parts/AOM-5024L-HD-R/part.yaml` says the opposite: *"two D-shaped
**solder-tab terminals on the can's bottom face** … **NOT flying wire leads**"*,
and that the 2.54 mm pitch was chosen to *"land these two tabs"* — with
"footprint not yet authored" at the time of writing. The datasheet's Dimensions
figure is a raster drawing (no extractable text), so **no number in the repo
establishes the actual tab spacing**, and 2.54 mm is an assumption carried
forward as if it were measured.

Consequences, whichever way it turns out:

- If the tabs are wider apart than 2.54 mm the capsule cannot sit on the pads
  and must be flying-wired. **Nothing on this board retains a 9.7 mm × 5 mm can
  mechanically** — no bracket, no keepout, no clip. Outdoors on a pole, a
  capsule held only by two solder joints is a fatigue item.
- The capsule's **can is the "−" terminal, i.e. GND**. Measured courtyard gap:

```
  MK1 <-> R2   0.232 mm      (the tightest non-legalizer gap on the board)
```

  R2 carries VMIC_F (≈4.95 V) and MIC_OUT. A grounded 9.7 mm metal can resting
  0.23 mm from R2's courtyard, hand-placed by a person under the datasheet's
  binding "≤2 s per terminal, ≤90 W, 360 ±10 °C" constraint, is an assembly
  hazard: contact kills the microphone silently. A 1 mm keepout around MK1
  costs nothing on an 80 × 45 mm board that is mostly empty.
- The mic has no acoustic isolation from the PCB (see STRUCT-1).

### ESD-1 — only 2 of the 8 exposed contacts are clamped, and the deliberate beep-pair isolation leaves a surge with no reference

D1 protects contacts **1 and 2 only**. Contacts 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 reach — with
zero series impedance — the LS1 coil, both diode cathodes, **U1 pin 8**, and the
GND pour. The connector is exposed, handled, outdoors, and at the end of a 25 ft
cable that is the array's surge aperture (ADR-0001's own words).

- **5V_AUDIO (4,5):** an ESD strike lands on U1's V+ with only C9 (10 µF, ~15 mm
  of 0.4 mm track away at (31.0,28.0)) and C10 (100 nF) to absorb it. Bulk MLCCs
  do absorb ESD reasonably; this is the least bad of the three.
- **The beep pair (3,6) is the interesting one.** ADR-0001 dec. 3 deliberately
  keeps 5V_BEEP/BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN galvanically separate from GND on the pod
  (G8). D3 (SMAJ6.0A) clamps *differentially* between them. **Nothing references
  either to GND.** A common-mode surge on the cable therefore appears in full
  across the beep-to-GND clearance, whose only bound is the DRC floor
  (0.127 mm rule / 0.3 mm zone clearance). PCB surface breakdown at 0.3 mm is
  roughly 1–3 kV; the IEC 61000-4-2 air-discharge level for a handled connector
  is 8 kV. The strike will therefore flash over somewhere on the board rather
  than through a designed path — under a component, across the LS1 land, or into
  the pour edge.

The conventional answer is an isolation bridge (a 1–2 kV Y-cap or back-to-back
diodes) from the isolated return to GND, which preserves the DC isolation G8
requires while giving a surge a defined path. Nothing is broken today; this is
what the next revision should carry.

### MLCC-1 — Class-II ceramics in the two most microphony-sensitive nodes of a microphone preamp

`C2` (1 µF X5R 0603, C15849) is the **input coupling** cap, and `C4` (10 µF X5R
0805, C15850) is the **gain-leg** cap. Both are Class-II dielectrics, both are
piezoelectric, and both sit at the two points in the chain where a spurious
voltage gets the full +9.5 dB of chain gain. The board is destined for an
outdoor pole mount (wind-induced vibration) with a magnetic transducer soldered
45.6 mm away. C4's DC bias is ≈0 V, which helps; C2's is ≈2.5 V, which does not.

For reference, the target to beat is the capsule's own noise floor: 14 dB(A) SPL
= **8.09 µV at MIC_OUT** (computed below). Typical X5R microphony in audio
circuits is 0.1–1 mV/g. C0G/NP0 (or film for C2) in the same lands removes the
mechanism entirely; C2 at 1 µF C0G is large but a 0805/1206 C0G at 0.47 µF with
R3 raised to 220 k gives the same 1.6 Hz corner.

### STRUCT-1 — LS1 and MK1 are hard-mounted to the same 1.6 mm FR4, so the calibration stimulus reaches the capsule by three paths with different delays

This is a design question, not a defect, and it cannot be resolved in-repo — but
it is the question the array's purpose hangs on. The calibration burst reaches
MK1 by at least:

| path | speed | delay over 45.62 mm | at 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| **airborne** (the one being measured) | 343 m/s | **133 µs** | 6.4 samples |
| plate **bending** wave, 1.6 mm FR4 at 4 kHz (c_B = √ω·(D/ρh)^¼ ≈ 201 m/s) | ~201 m/s | **227 µs** | 10.9 samples |
| in-plane **extensional** wave, √(E/ρ(1−ν²)) ≈ 3500 m/s | ~3500 m/s | **13 µs** | 0.6 samples |

Two spurious arrivals within ±6 samples of the intended one, one early and one
late, with unbounded relative amplitude — MK1's can is soldered directly to the
same board LS1 is reflowed to, with no isolation of any kind. If the structural
components are within ~10 dB of the airborne one they smear the correlation peak
the array uses to time-align six channels, and they do it **differently on each
pod** (mounting torque, enclosure standoffs, potting). A 41 µs bias is ~14 mm of
apparent range error per pod.

Bounding this needs one measurement on one assembled board (drive LS1 with the
capsule's sound port blocked, and compare). It should be on the bring-up list.
Design answers, if it matters: mount the capsule in a grommet, or move the
calibration transducer off the pod PCB, or slot the board between LS1 and MK1.

## 3. What was checked and found CLEAN — with the measurement

A clean result is only useful if it names its coverage.

**U1 rotation (CPL 270) — independently derived, and I concur.** Derived here
without re-running `rotation_authority`, from three facts:

1. **Row-vs-column orientation excludes 0 and 180 outright.** Our footprint
   (`Package_SO:SOIC-8_3.9x4.9mm_P1.27mm`, board rot 0) has its pads in two
   **columns** at x = ±2.475 with rows along Y — verified directly from the
   board: U1.3 at (49.525, 28.635) = U1 pos (52,28) + local (−2.475, +0.635).
   JLC's model name `SOIC-8_L5.0-W4.0-P1.27-**LS6.0**-BL` encodes a **lead span
   in the perpendicular axis**, i.e. two **rows** along X separated ~5.41 mm
   (reported pad rows at y = ±2.705). Rows→columns forces {90, 270}. Separation
   from 0/180: **4.18 mm rms** — this channel is numbering-free.
2. **Which of 90 / 270 is selected by JLC's numbering.** Solving for the JLC pad
   positions that the reported 270° fit implies gives JLC pad 1 at local
   (−1.905, **+2.705**) and pad 8 at (−1.905, −2.705) — i.e. pin 1 at the
   **lower-left**, numbering counter-clockwise: exactly the standard EasyEDA/LCSC
   convention for a horizontally-drawn SOIC. Under KiCad's rot-270 map
   (x,y)→(−y,x) that lands on our pad 1 at local (−2.475, −1.905). Under 90 it
   lands 6.43 mm away, at the opposite corner. Reported separation: **0.235 vs
   5.910 mm, 25×.**
3. **A consistency check the wrong answer cannot produce.** The 0.2351 mm
   residual is **entirely in one axis** and equals **exactly half** the
   independently documented land-length delta (`twin_adjudications.yaml`:
   pad1↔8 ours 4.95 mm vs JLC 5.42 mm, Δ = 0.47 mm; 0.47/2 = 0.235). A wrong
   rotation would leave residuals in both axes.

**Evidence used: the model's dimensional naming (numbering-free), the
EasyEDA numbering convention (NOT numbering-free), and the residual-axis
consistency check.** Because step 2 rests on a convention that a mirrored or
mis-numbered vendor model would violate, **the JLC order-preview eyeball on U1's
pin-1 dot must still be performed.** The stake, stated concretely: at 90° instead
of 270°, pin 8 (V+) lands on the pad wired to GND and pin 4 (V−) on the pad wired
to 5V_AUDIO — reversed supply, U1 destroyed on first power-up.

> **Terminology trap, flagged because it already caused a misreading.**
> `verification/rotation_measurements.txt` marks U1, LS1, D2 and D3 as
> `polarity=single-channel`. In A-POL that means **one evidence channel**, not
> one amplifier. **OPA1678 is a DUAL op-amp and BOTH halves are used** (U1A
> non-inverting +1.5 on pins 1/2/3, U1B inverting −1 on pins 5/6/7 — verified
> against the netlist). There is no unused op-amp section left floating, which
> was the classic defect this audit was asked to look for. A future reader who
> takes "single-channel" literally will mis-reason about this part.

**LS1 rotation (CPL 0) — confirmed, and the previous audit's dismissal was right
for the wrong reason.** The order-readiness review says "D2/D3/LS1 are
two-channel and need no eyeball." LS1's land pattern is **fully 90°-rotationally
symmetric** (four identical 2.5 × 2.5 pads at (±3.5, ±3.5)), so pad geometry
cannot discriminate rotation at all — `PAD-CLOUD rms 0.1414 at all four angles`.
The only discriminator is JLC's `fp_text "+"` at (−2.50, −1.00), which sits in
the same quadrant as its pad 1 and matches ours at (−5.60, −3.50) beside our pad
1, which part.yaml ties to the datasheet's "POLARITY PAD(+)" top-left callout
(rev 1.04 p.2). That chain holds. Worth recording that the *consequence* of a
rotation error here is benign: at 180° both coil terminals land on the NC
mechanical dummy pads (silent buzzer, no damage); at 90/270 the drive is
polarity-reversed (a coil still sounds). No damage path.

**Signal-path noise and dynamic range — the preamp is noise-transparent.**
Computed from datasheet numbers (`ac2.py` §F):

```
 stage A input-referred: en 4.50 + Rsrc(1.41k) 4.81 + fb(6.67k) 10.48 + R3 0.56
                         => 12.39 nV/rtHz
 stage B own noise at OUTB: 20.26 nV/rtHz
 differential output noise 42.33 nV/rtHz -> input-referred 14.11 nV/rtHz
 over 20 kHz: 2.00 uV rms input-referred
 AOM-5024 self-noise (SNR 80 dB @ 94 dB SPL, A-wtd) = 14 dB(A) SPL = 8.09 uV
 total 8.33 uV -> the preamp adds 0.26 dB to the microphone's own floor
```

**The circuit noise is 12.2 dB below the capsule's own self-noise and costs
0.26 dB of system SNR.** The largest single contributor is the stage-A feedback
network (R6∥R7 = 6.67 kΩ, 10.5 nV/√Hz) — scaling R6/R7 down 4× would recover
~0.2 dB, which is not worth a respin. Dynamic range, floor to the CAL-1 ceiling:
**87–93 dB**, comfortably inside 24-bit/48 kHz. **This part of the design is
good and should not be touched.**

**Cable drive / stability into a capacitive load — correct by construction.**
OPA1678's specified capacitive-load drive is **100 pF** (SBOS855E p.7), and
25 ft of Cat5e presents ~400 pF. The design is safe because R10/R11 = 100 Ω sit
**outside** both feedback loops — verified from the netlist, not assumed:

```
 OUTA (4)  U1.1  R6.1  R8.1  R10.1      <- feedback R6 closes AT the op-amp output
 OLA  (2)  R10.2 C5.1                   <- the cable hangs off the far side of R10
 OUTB (3)  U1.7  R9.1  R11.1
 OLB  (2)  R11.2 C6.1
```

The op-amp sees 100 Ω + C_cable, decoupled from the loop; the pole with 400 pF
is at 4 MHz. Output loading at OUTA (R6 ∥ R8 ∥ (R10+C5+10 k) ≈ 3.4 kΩ) draws
440 µA at 1.5 Vpk against ISC = ±50 mA. **No stability or drive issue.**

**Rail at the far end of the longest allowed run.** 25 ft of 24 AWG solid Cat5e,
supply on the paralleled blue pair and return on the paralleled brown pair =
0.64 Ω round trip. At the corrected 6.2 mA worst-case draw (PWR-1) the drop is
**4.0 mV**. Against a 5.00 V −2 % source (4.90 V) the pod sees **4.896 V**, i.e.
**396 mV above U1's 4.5 V single-supply floor**. Headroom is ample; the rail
problem on this board is ripple (PSR-1), not IR.

**Hot-plug.** 8P8C contacts all wipe together — there is no first-mate ground —
so any contact may make first. Worst case is 5V_AUDIO before GND: the pod's GND
floats to +5 V and the only return is through C5/C6 into the central's input,
current-limited by R10/R11 = 100 Ω to ≤50 mA for a few microseconds. D1 does not
conduct (bidirectional, VBR 6.5 V > 5 V). Survivable. Inrush into the rail bulk
(C8+C9 = 20 µF through 0.64 Ω) is a **transient the CENTRAL must absorb per
port**, ×6 ports — worth naming in the interface contract alongside DC-1.

**Pin maps (canon S-VER) — all re-verified against the datasheet FIGURE, page
cited, and cross-checked against the netlist:**

| ref | part | figure | agrees with netlist |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | OPA1678IDR | SBOS855E **Fig 5-3 + "Pin Functions: OPA1678" table, p.4** (1=OUTA 2=−INA 3=+INA 4=V− 5=+INB 6=−INB 7=OUTB 8=V+) | yes — U1.8→5V_AUDIO, U1.4→GND, U1.3→INP, U1.5→VMID |
| D1 | TPD2E2U06DRLR | SLLSEG9C **§5 "Pin Configuration and Functions", DRL 5-pin SOT top view + Pin Functions table, p.3** (1,2=NC 3=IO1 4=GND 5=IO2) | yes — D1.3→AUDIO_P, D1.5→AUDIO_N, D1.4→GND, D1.1/D1.2 floated (the datasheet's own permitted choice) |
| LS1 | CMT-8504-100-SMT-TR | rev 1.04 **mechanical drawing + Recommended PCB Layout, p.2** (+ top-left, − bottom-left, 2 dummy right) | yes — LS1.1→5V_BEEP, LS1.2→BEEP_SWITCHED_RETURN, LS1.3/4 no net |
| D2/D3 | SS14 / SMAJ6.0A | KiCad D_SMA pad 1 = cathode/band | yes — both pad 1 → 5V_BEEP (freewheel blocks in normal drive) |

| MK1 | AOM-5024L-HD-R | Dimensions figure p.3 (can bottom marked + / −) | yes — MK1.1→MIC_OUT, MK1.2→GND |

**Footprint land patterns vs datasheet.** LS1's vendored `.kicad_mod` reproduces
the datasheet's Recommended PCB Layout exactly (2.5 × 2.5 pads at ±3.5, outer
span 9.5, inner gap 4.5 — matches `part.yaml land_pattern` field for field, and
correctly uses the **PCB-layout** numbers rather than the smaller component-body
pad geometry, which is the trap the part.yaml gotcha warns about). J1's stock
KiCad footprint is hole-for-hole against Rev K. U1's and D3's land-length deltas
are the already-adjudicated KiCad-IPC-vs-vendor split. **MK1's is the only
footprint not derived from a datasheet number — see MK1-1.**

**Schematic hygiene.** No single-connection nets except `D1.1`/`D1.2`
(`unconnected-(D1-NC-Pad1/2)`), which are datasheet NC pins and float by the
datasheet's own permission. No unintended shorts. No floating inputs: every
op-amp input has a defined DC path (INP via R3 to VMID; INA via R6/R7; +INB
directly on VMID; INB via R8/R9). No missing pull-ups (no logic on this board).
No unused op-amp section. `J1.9–12`, `J1.SH ×2`, `LS1.3/4`, `H1–H4` and the two
NPTH posts carry no net — all four groups are declared intentional in ADR-0001
dec. 4 and the LS1 part.yaml.

**Balanced-pair route symmetry.** AUDIO_P carries **95.72 mm** of track and
AUDIO_N **46.12 mm** (3 vs 5 vias) — a 2.07× mismatch on a nominally matched
pair, on an 80 mm board. Quantified rather than assumed: the extra ~50 mm of
0.25 mm track adds ~0.1 Ω (against 100 Ω of R10/R11) and ~25 pF of coplanar/
plane capacitance, putting the resulting leg mismatch pole at **~64 MHz**.
**Irrelevant in the audio band**; the already-deferred "matched-pair route" item
is cosmetic and should be labelled as such rather than carried as a risk.

**Board and fab.** Edge.Cuts is four segments, 80.000 × 45.000 mm centreline
(80.100 × 45.100 outer). 234 tracks / 72 vias; widths {0.2498: 3, 0.25: 140,
0.4: 48, 0.5: 43}; F.Cu 205 / B.Cu 29; GND on both layers with 43 stitching
vias. The three 0.2498 mm segments are the documented KRT nanometre-floor
artifact and clear the 0.127 mm tier floor by 1.97×.

## 4. Coverage — what this audit did NOT establish

Named honestly, so the next reader knows the boundary.

- **The 106.8 dB SPL figure in CAL-1 is a free-field inverse-square
  extrapolation from a 10 cm datasheet minimum.** Near-field and enclosure
  effects give it roughly ±3–6 dB of uncertainty. Even at −6 dB (100.8 dB) it
  meets the worst-case ceiling of 101.3 dB. It has not been measured on hardware.
- **The AOM-5024 terminal-sensitivity shift (+2.14 dB) assumes the capsule is a
  2.2 kΩ Thévenin source**, which is what the datasheet's "Output Impedance
  2.2 kΩ" states but is not the same as a characterised r_ds. The supply-voltage
  contribution (3 V spec point vs 4.95 V actual) is unquantified and moves the
  same direction.
- **PSR-1's acoustic-equivalent column is only as good as the rail spec that
  does not exist.** The transfer functions are exact for the shipped topology;
  the ripple amplitude is the unknown, and it lives on the sibling CENTRAL
  board.
- **DC-1 cannot be closed from this board.** It needs the central's input
  network.
- **MK1's actual tab spacing was not obtained** — the datasheet Dimensions
  figure is a raster with no extractable text and no vector geometry.
- **STRUCT-1's wave speeds are analytic estimates** for nominal FR4
  (E ≈ 22 GPa, ρ ≈ 1850 kg/m³, h = 1.6 mm) and are sensitive to the enclosure
  mounting. They establish that three arrivals exist within ±6 samples, not
  their amplitudes.
- **The JLC 3D model for C192421 was not re-fetched.** The rotation derivation in
  §3 uses the model's dimensional naming and the geometry reported in
  `verification/rotation_measurements.txt`, plus an independent consistency
  check; it does not re-read the mesh.
- **Not re-run, because today's order-readiness audit already ran them:** DRC,
  policy_audit, F-LEGIBLE, F-PAYLOAD, A-EVID, A-POP/A-POS, P-FACT, M-BOM,
  M-SHIP, M-BEACON, fleet_regrade, the live stock scan, and twin_overlay.
