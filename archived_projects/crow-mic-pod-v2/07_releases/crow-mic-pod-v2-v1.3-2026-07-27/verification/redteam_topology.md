## Findings — crow-mic-pod-v2 (topology / protection / ratings lens)

| # | Severity | Finding | Evidence | Fix |

| 1 | **P0** | J1 pins 4,5 (**5V_AUDIO+**) / 7,8 (**GND_AUDIO−**) are *exactly* the IEEE 802.3af/at "Alternative B" (spare-pair) PoE power-injection pins — and this exact polarity (fixed regardless of MDI/MDI-X) matches what this board expects. 5V_AUDIO ties **directly, with zero series impedance**, to U1 pin 8 (V+), R1, R4, C7–C10 (confirmed in exported netlist `/tmp/pod_rt.net`, net code 1). Any switch doing passive/legacy/forced PoE on that port (common on outdoor security-camera PoE switches — exactly the class of infra this array would share a rack with) drives 44–57V DC straight onto U1 V+. OPA1678IDR abs-max `vs_max = 40V` (`02_parts/OPA1678IDR/part.yaml:26`) — exceeded by 10–42%. Destroys U1, almost certainly the 5V-rail-rated decoupling caps (C7–C10, rating undocumented anywhere in this project) and possibly the mic bias node (MK1 `vdd_range: 1~10VDC`, `02_parts/AOM-5024L-HD-R/part.yaml:35`). No TVS, fuse, series-R, or polarity protection exists on 5V_AUDIO/GND_AUDIO at all — ADR-0001 explicitly says "Rails 5V_AUDIO/5V_BEEP rely on the central's protection... here" (`01_docs/decisions/0001-protection-and-grounding.md:24`), i.e. protection was **delegated to the assumption that only the sibling CENTRAL board is ever on the other end.** ADR-0003, which specifically analyzes the "plug into a real switch" hazard, only discusses PHY-voltage-on-AUDIO_P/N and "5V_AUDIO backward into a switch port" (`0003-...md:15`) — it never considers a switch **injecting** power into the pod (the actual, dominant real-world hazard). | Add a PoE-defeat: minimum a series TVS/clamp + PPTC fuse on 5V_AUDIO rated well under 40V (e.g., a 6–6.8V clamp + resettable fuse, or a small reverse-blocking/overvoltage crowbar), OR — cheaper — deliberately break the PoE-alias by moving 5V_AUDIO off pins 4/5/7/8 in the next respin. | 3 | **P1** | Reverse-polarity risk on 5V_AUDIO/GND_AUDIO is real and **not actually covered** by the stated mitigation. ADR-0001 decision 5 (`0001-...md:46-52`) accepts "no reverse-polarity FET" and relies on "the keyed RJ45 + mandatory labeling + controlled install." But this is a **custom, hand-crimped, non-standard pinout** (ADR-0003) — the keying only prevents plugging in a *different device*; it does nothing to prevent a technician hand-wiring a custom Cat5e run with pins 4/5 swapped with 7/8 (adjacent conductors in a punch-down/crimp jig). ## Verdict: **DO NOT ORDER** — hold for a P0 fix.

The RJ45-everywhere directive (ADR-0003) converts what was meant to be a passive, protected pod into a device with a bare, standards-compliant RJ45 jack whose custom power pins alias exactly onto PoE's fixed "Alternative B" assignment, with **zero protection** between the jack and the op-amp supply pin. This isn't a theoretical corner case — PoE switches are common in exactly the kind of outdoor/security/sensor-network infrastructure a "crow acoustic array" would be deployed alongside, and the labeling mitigation (ADR-0003) never analyzed this failure mode (it only discussed low-energy PHY signaling and backward power flow, not power injection). Order only after adding at least a minimal overvoltage clamp + fuse (or equivalent) on 5V_AUDIO, or after explicitly getting the user's informed sign-off that this specific risk (not just the general "no reverse-polarity protection" one already in ADR-0001) is accepted for this deployment.
