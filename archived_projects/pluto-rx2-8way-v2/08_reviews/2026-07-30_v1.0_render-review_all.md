## 1. What this board appears to be, from the pictures alone

A single-sided-assembly, 4-layer, 46.1 × 73.1 mm portrait rectangle, green mask,
ENIG-looking gold pads, with **ten identical vertical SMA jacks** standing ~9 mm
proud of the top face and **nothing at all on the bottom** (`twin_bottom.png` and
`render_bottom_bare.png` show only pad rings, a dense stitching-via lattice, and
four mounting holes — no silk, no parts).

Eight of the ten SMAs are arranged in a ring around a small QFN at the board
centre; a ninth and tenth sit side by side at the top edge. The silk names them
`ANT1..ANT8` plus `RX1` and `RX2`, and a note block in the lower-left says, in
plain language, what the board is:

```
pluto-rx2-8way-v2  8-WAY AoA
70 MHz - 6 GHz
RX ONLY - NO TRANSMIT
PASSIVE ANT - 0 VDC MAX
ANT8 = RX1 TAP -20.26 dB
RX1/RX2 = SDR OUTPUTS
U_MCU HAND-SOLDER ONLY
USB-C = PWR + BOOTLOADER
```

So: an **eight-element antenna-array front end for angle-of-arrival work with a
two-receiver SDR**. The centre QFN (`U_SW`, PE42482, an SP8T RF switch) fans out
to the eight antenna jacks on eight near-identical straight microstrip arms
(visible as eight black clearance channels radiating from the chip in
`render_top_bare.png` — they look deliberately equal-length, and the audit
confirms 0.53 mm spread over 8 paths). The switch common goes to `RX2`. `RX1` is
**not** switched: it is hard-wired to `ANT8`, and the switch's eighth port is fed
from that same node through two 220 Ω resistors — a −20 dB tap. That gives a
sensible architecture: one SDR channel permanently on a reference element, the
other scanning the array, with a calibration position where both receivers see
the same signal. The −20.26 dB on the silk is arithmetically right (50 Ω source
into 50 Ω ∥ 490 Ω, then 50/490 → −20.25 dB), which is a good sign about the rest
of the design math.

Control comes from a bare, unpopulated 23-pad castellated land pattern in the
bottom-right quadrant — an RP2040-Zero module, hand-soldered, USB-C at the board
edge, driving four switch-select lines through 47 Ω series resistors with 10 kΩ
pull-downs, plus one status LED. A ferrite bead and a 4.7 µF bulk cap separate
the module's 3V3 output from the switch's supply.

**What the pictures do not show, and should:** the module itself. It is the
largest object on the board and the only part a human has to fit, and it has no
body in any of the six 3D views, no reference designator on the silk, and no
"USB" or orientation marking. The bottom-right quadrant of every twin render is
simply empty board.

## 3. READABILITY VERDICT — `pdf/schematic.pdf`

**FAIL.** A competent engineer can follow the **signal chain** from this drawing —
ten SMA symbols, one SP8T, an eight-arm fan-out, RFC → RX2, a two-resistor tap
onto RX1/ANT8 — because the RF section is spread out and its labels do not
collide. Everything else fails.

**Power path — unreadable.** At `U_MCU` pins 2 and 3, *three* net labels
(`N3V3_MOD`, `ANT1`, `GND`) are printed on top of one another as a single
illegible blob; you cannot tell which of ground and 3V3 is on which pin.
`C_SW1` (100 nF) and `C_BULK` (4.7 µF) are drawn stacked in one vertical column
joined by what reads as a wire — it is actually the outline of a 90°-rotated
`N3V3_MOD` label. They read as one node; they are on **opposite sides of
`FB_3V3`**. `C_SW2` (1 µF) is parked at the far top-right of the sheet, nowhere
near either `C_SW1` or `U_SW`'s VDD pin, with the `N3V3` label printed across its
reference designator.

**Control path — unreadable.** `SEL_V1..SEL_V4` and `LED_STAT` are stacked at
grid pitch on `U_MCU`'s GP0..GP4 and overlap each other and their own wires.
I read `R_S1`'s left terminal as being on `LED_STAT` and concluded that the RF
switch's `LS` pin was being toggled by the LED drive — and had to fall back to
`source/*.net` to discover that `R_S1` is actually `SEL_V1 → SW_V1` and `LS` is
tied to GND. **That is the test, and the drawing failed it: I misread the
control topology, in a specific and alarming direction, from a released
document.**

**Contributing defects:**

* No sheet frame, **no title block, no revision, no date, no sheet number**. `pdftotext` finds no such tokens anywhere on the page.
* **No power or ground symbols at all** — every rail is a bare text label, so there is no visual cue distinguishing supply from signal.
* `RX2_OUT`, `RX1_TAP`, `RX1_TAP_MID`, `220Ω`, `47Ω`, `680Ω` are struck through by wires or by other labels.
* Only ~65 % of the 900 × 450 pt page is used; the right third is blank while the symbols are crammed at grid pitch.
* ERC on the shipped `.kicad_sch` reports **131 `endpoint_off_grid` warnings** — the mechanical cause of the pile-up.
* And the document is **stale** (finding #1), so even where it is legible it is describing a different board.

`policy_audit.md` waives S-OCCL on the grounds that the converter-generated
`.kicad_sch` "is not this board's human document". Whatever was intended by
that, **`pdf/schematic.pdf` is the human document that this release actually
ships**, it carries all four waived occlusions plus several more, and it does not
match the netlist beside it. The waiver's premise does not hold for the file it
was meant to protect.
