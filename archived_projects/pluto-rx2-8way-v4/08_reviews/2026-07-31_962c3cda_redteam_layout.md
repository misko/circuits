## Executive decision

The basic RF topology is disciplined: all phase-critical RF traces are top-layer, have zero routing vias, see continuous In1 ground outside the intentional SMA antipads, and pass the project's declared 1.0 mm routed-copper spread gate. Power and calculated steady-state thermal margins are also generous.

The package is nevertheless not orderable. Ten vias overlap RF-switch SMD lands, including seven in the exposed pad, while the archive contains no selective filled/capped via-in-pad fabrication contract. All 3,446 vias use the same 0.15 mm drill, so drill diameter cannot identify the subset. The same vias imply a nominal 10.67:1 aspect ratio through the declared 1.6 mm board, exceeding the fabricator's published 10:1 guidance without documented DFM acceptance. Finally, the board's primary 6 GHz transmission-line cross-section is declared as a bare CPWG while the delivered board is soldermask-covered; the documentation calls the value preliminary and supplies no reproducible solver output or coupon. These are pre-order defects, not build-characterization items.

## Repair comparison for the through-via defect

The lower-risk starting point is a **1.2 mm four-layer JLC7628 construction**, subject to connector/mechanical confirmation and a fresh controlled-impedance solution. With a 0.15 mm nominal via it gives `1.2 / 0.15 = 8.0:1`. JLCPCB's current impedance stack page lists 1.2 mm four-layer options and the same 7628 outer-prepreg family; the order-specific stack must still be selected and frozen because changing core thickness can affect broader mechanics even when the top-to-In1 dielectric remains 0.2104 mm. [JLCPCB impedance stackups](https://jlcpcb.com/impedance).

Keeping 1.6 mm and enlarging holes is geometrically invasive. The mathematical minimum for 10:1 is a 0.160 mm production drill; a practical 0.20 mm drill gives 8:1, but the existing 0.25 mm pads cannot retain an acceptable annular ring. Pads would need to grow to at least approximately 0.30-0.35 mm. That conflicts with the 0.5 mm-pitch RF-switch lands: the present 0.25 mm pad is already the largest size compatible with the authored 0.20 mm clearance/landability arithmetic. Increasing thousands of pads would also require rechecking coplanar impedance, fence coupling, QFN escape, hole-to-copper clearance, and soldermask. JLCPCB's BGA guidance likewise shows 0.35 mm pads for 0.15 mm filled/capped vias, reinforcing that 0.25/0.15 is not a robust POFV construction. [JLCPCB BGA / POFV guidance](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/bga-design-guidelines---pcb-layout-recommendations-for-bga-packages).

Therefore the preferred redesign sequence is: (1) obtain the exact orderable 1.2 mm four-layer stack and accepted POFV definition; (2) solve and freeze the masked CPWG on that stack; (3) regenerate board and fab output; (4) re-run DRC, endpoint/launch congruence, length, reference-plane, via-fence, hole-spacing, and mechanical checks; and (5) obtain assembly DFM. This preference is not approval to order the present archive.

## Positive evidence and review limits

- The RF reference plane is materially better than a simple rules-only review would reveal: In1 is uninterrupted under the routed RF sections, and control routing does not cross the RF paths.
- There is no carrier switch-node, high-current gate-drive loop, or meaningful `U_SW` heat source. The primary thermal uncertainty is the plug-in module regulator and physical underside clearance.
- The board passes syntactic DRC/parity and its scalar routed-length gate. Those successes do not test POFV process definition, plated-hole capability, soldermask-aware impedance, launch congruence, connector service acceptance, or physical module fit.
- No full-wave EM simulation, vendor DFM response, impedance coupon measurement, VNA data, X-ray/cross-section, or populated thermal/fit measurement was available. The verdict must remain `DEFECTIVE` / `DO-NOT-ORDER` until the P0 findings are repaired in regenerated artifacts and independently rechecked.
