## 1. The relay land pattern — the highest-stakes item, verified from the figure

This is the item the brief flagged: a previous revision shipped a footprint drawn against
the **wrong datasheet sub-figure**. I re-derived the correct land from the datasheet
without reference to the footprint, then compared.

**Datasheet read.** `02_parts/DIP05-1A72-13L/datasheet-reed-relay-series-dip.pdf`
(sha256 `7616ace1…e75141`, matching `part.yaml`), Standex DIP-series **Version 03,
01 Aug 2025, p.3 "Pin-Out (Top View) (2.54 mm [0.10"] pitch grid)"**, rendered at
600 dpi and cropped to **sub-figure 13** alone. What the figure shows, read off the
grid square by square:

- **Four leads only.**
- Upper row, left lead labelled **14**, right lead labelled **8** — joined by the
  **switch (contact)** symbol.
- Lower row, left lead labelled **2**, right lead labelled **6** — joined by the
  **coil** symbol.
- Grid measurement: rows are **3 pitches = 7.62 mm** apart. Leads 14↔8 are
  **6 pitches = 15.24 mm** apart (the extreme ends of the DIP-14 grid). Leads 2↔6 are
  **4 pitches = 10.16 mm** apart, i.e. each inset exactly one pitch from its row's end.

So the datasheet's own sub-figure 13 says: **CONTACT = DIP leads 14 and 8, both on one
row; COIL = DIP leads 2 and 6, both on the other row; coil-to-contact separation is the
7.62 mm ROW spacing.** For contrast I also read sub-figures 12, 19, 21 and 51 on the same
page — sub-figure 12 has **eight** leads with coil and contact interleaved along the
package, which is a completely different land. Getting these two confused is exactly the
failure mode named in the brief.

**Footprint compared.** `03_src/lib/cooksense.pretty/Relay_StandexDIP_1A_pinout13.kicad_mod`,
four THT pads:

| land pad | local (x,y) mm | maps to DIP lead | datasheet function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (−3.81, −5.08) | **2** | COIL |
| 2 | (−3.81, +5.08) | **6** | COIL |
| 3 | (+3.81, +7.62) | **8** | CONTACT |
| 4 | (+3.81, −7.62) | **14** | CONTACT |

I verified this mapping by transforming the datasheet figure's lead coordinates into the
footprint frame rather than by trusting the footprint's `descr` string. Taking the figure's
own pixel grid (pitch ≈ 100 px = 2.54 mm) and applying a single **+90° rotation** about the
package centre:

| DIP lead | figure (px) | transformed (mm) | footprint pad | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | (297, 718) | (−3.78, −5.11) | 1 @ (−3.81, −5.08) | 0.03 |
| 6 | (697, 718) | (−3.78, +5.05) | 2 @ (−3.81, +5.08) | 0.03 |
| 8 | (797, 420) | (+3.78, +7.59) | 3 @ (+3.81, +7.62) | 0.03 |
| 14 | (200, 420) | (+3.78, −7.57) | 4 @ (+3.81, −7.62) | 0.05 |

**A pure rotation with no reflection.** Every residual is within the pixel-measurement
error of the render. **NOT MIRRORED.** The pin-1 silk dot at (−4.60, −9.20) sits at the
unpopulated DIP-lead-1 grid corner (−3.81, −7.62), which is the correct end of the body to
mark. Silk body 6.5 × 19.3 mm (±3.25, ±9.65) matches `package:` in `part.yaml`.

**Mirror hazard assessment (recorded because it is the named risk).** On this particular
part a mirror would in any case be electrically harmless: the coil is a bare reed coil with
no internal diode, so COIL_A/COIL_B are interchangeable, and the contact is Form 1A
SPST-NO, so CONTACT_A/CONTACT_B are interchangeable; both lead pairs are symmetric about
the package centre-line. The land is therefore self-symmetric under reflection. **This is
luck, not design margin** — it is true only for the no-diode `-13L`. If any future revision
adopts a `-13`-code **internal-diode** variant, the coil becomes polarised and the mirror
check becomes load-bearing. `part.yaml` already anticipates this by holding land pad 1
(= DIP 2) at the positive rail by convention; the board honours that on all twelve.

**Coil/contact node split, all 12 instances, read from the board file directly** (I
re-extracted pad positions and nets with `pcbnew` rather than trusting the dossiers, and
they agreed exactly):

| ref | rot | pad1 (COIL+) | pad2 (COIL−) | pad3 (CONTACT) | pad4 (CONTACT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K_U1 | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U1_N | KP_U1 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_U2 | 0 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U2_N | KP_U2 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_U3 | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U3_N | KP_U3 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_U4 | 0 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U4_N | KP_U4 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_U5 | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U5_N | KP_U5 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_U6 | 0 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_U6_N | KP_U6 | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_D1 | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_D1_N | KP_D1 | D_SEL_BUS |
| K_D2 | 0 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_D2_N | KP_D2 | D_SEL_BUS |
| K_D3 | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_D3_N | KP_D3 | D_SEL_BUS |
| K_D4 | 0 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_D4_N | KP_D4 | D_SEL_BUS |
| K_PRESS | 180 | 5V_KEY_RELAY | COIL_PRESS_N | RKEY_MID | U_SEL_BUS |
| K_STOP | 0 | **5V_STOP** | COIL_STOP_N | RSTOP_MID | **KP_U6** |

All twelve: coil nets on the west column, contact nets on the east column, **never mixed**.
Pairwise symmetry holds on pads 1 and 2 across all twelve (K_STOP's separate `5V_STOP` rail
is the documented always-available STOP rail, ADR-0011 §4 — same KIND of net, a coil supply).

**K_STOP is the one structural divergence and it resolves benign.** On eleven relays pad 4
carries a select BUS; on K_STOP pad 4 carries a keypad line (`KP_U6`) and pad 3 carries
`RSTOP_MID`. Because the contact is a non-polar SPST-NO, the two contact pads are
interchangeable and this is not a defect. I traced the intent: `RSTOP_MID → R_STOP → KP_D1`,
so closing K_STOP shorts `KP_U6` to `KP_D1` through `R_STOP` — it presses the STOP key
directly at its matrix intersection, bypassing the U/D select relays so that STOP works
regardless of what the Pi has selected. That is coherent and is what the safety model
requires.

**The crosspoint topology, re-derived from the netlist alone.** `K_U1..K_U6` connect
`KP_Ux` → `U_SEL_BUS` (row select); `K_D1..K_D4` connect `KP_Dx` → `D_SEL_BUS` (column
select); `K_PRESS` bridges `U_SEL_BUS` ↔ `R_KEY` ↔ `D_SEL_BUS`, closing the selected
intersection. A clean 6×4 crosspoint keypad emulator. No relay contact is on a rail or a
ground.

**Isolation domain, measured from the netlist.** I enumerated every net touching a relay
COIL pad and every net touching a relay CONTACT pad:

- contact domain (14 nets): `KP_U1..U6`, `KP_D1..D4`, `U_SEL_BUS`, `D_SEL_BUS`,
  `RKEY_MID`, `RSTOP_MID`
- coil domain (14 nets): `5V_KEY_RELAY`, `5V_STOP`, `COIL_*_N` ×12
- **intersection: EMPTY.**
- The contact domain's only non-relay members are `J_KEY_MATRIX`, `R_KEY`, `R_STOP`, and
  three test points (`TP_USEL`, `TP_DSEL`, `TP_RKEY`). No ground, no rail, no logic gate
  touches it anywhere on the board.

So ADR-0002's claim that the isolation boundary runs between the columns is **TRUE both
geometrically and in the netlist**, independently confirmed.

**Placement pattern.** Twelve relays at x = 26.0 … 193.6 mm, pitch **15.24 mm measured**,
rotation alternating 180/0/180/0. That alternation makes contact-column-facing and
coil-column-facing gaps alternate, which is what the `layout.notes` isolation-comb rule
requires, and it puts adjacent coils anti-parallel, which is the datasheet p.3
magnetic-coupling mitigation. Consistent.

**RELAY GROUP VERDICT: PASS ×12.**

## 2. Coil drivers `U_ULNA` / `U_ULNB` — TBD62083AFWG

**Datasheet read.** `02_parts/TBD62083AFWG/TBD62083A_20160511.pdf`. Three separate figures
read, so the map is confirmed twice over:

1. **p.2 "Pin explanations" table** (text layer): I1–I8 = 1–8, GND = 9, COMMON = 10,
   O8 = 11 … O1 = 18.
2. **p.1 "Pin connection (top view)"** figure, rendered at 150 dpi: pin 1 (I1) bottom-left
   with the body notch on the left; 1–9 along the bottom row left-to-right; 10–18 along the
   top row right-to-left. Agrees with the table.
3. **p.2 "Equivalent circuit (each driver)"** figure: INPUT → series R → clamp → **a
   resistor to GROUND** → series R → DMOS gate, with the **clamp diode to COMMON** on the
   output.

**Winding.** Transforming the p.1 figure by a single **+90° rotation**: pins 1–9 land on the
west column with pin 1 at the top, pins 10–18 on the east column with pin 10 at the bottom.
The footprint (`Package_SO:SOIC-18W_7.5x11.6mm_P1.27mm`) is exactly that. **Rotation only,
no mirror.**

**Land pattern vs the package drawing.** p.9 `P-SOP18-0812-1.27-001` at 200 dpi: body
11.35–11.68 × 7.37–7.62 mm, lead span 10.01–10.64 mm, pitch 1.27, lead width 0.36–0.51.
The KiCad SOIC-18W_7.5×11.6 land has pads at x = ±4.65 with 2.05 mm length, i.e. copper from
3.625 to 5.675 mm from centre, which brackets the 5.005–5.32 mm lead tips with toe and heel
extension. **Correct land for this package** — not the narrow-body SOIC-18.

**Channel alignment — the check that matters most here.** For each channel n, IN_n must be
the select line whose coil OUT_n drives. Verified all eleven used channels:

| ch | IN pad / net | OUT pad / net | relay coil |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1 / SEL_U1 | 18 / COIL_U1_N | K_U1 ✓ |
| A2 | 2 / SEL_U2 | 17 / COIL_U2_N | K_U2 ✓ |
| A3 | 3 / SEL_U3 | 16 / COIL_U3_N | K_U3 ✓ |
| A4 | 4 / SEL_U4 | 15 / COIL_U4_N | K_U4 ✓ |
| A5 | 5 / SEL_U5 | 14 / COIL_U5_N | K_U5 ✓ |
| A6 | 6 / SEL_U6 | 13 / COIL_U6_N | K_U6 ✓ |
| A7 | 7 / SEL_D1 | 12 / COIL_D1_N | K_D1 ✓ |
| A8 | 8 / SEL_D2 | 11 / COIL_D2_N | K_D2 ✓ |
| B1 | 1 / SEL_D3 | 18 / COIL_D3_N | K_D3 ✓ |
| B2 | 2 / SEL_D4 | 17 / COIL_D4_N | K_D4 ✓ |
| B3 | 3 / PRESS_TIMED | 16 / COIL_PRESS_N | K_PRESS ✓ |

**No channel crossover.** A single transposition here would fire the wrong key and be
invisible to DRC. `GND` (pad 9) on ground and `COMMON` (pad 10) on `5V_KEY_RELAY` on both
parts — COMMON is the flyback clamp-diode cathode and must be the coil supply rail, which it
is, on both. Pairwise symmetry between the two instances: identical KIND on every pad.

**Floating inputs on U_ULNB resolve as safe, and only the datasheet says why.** U_ULNB has
IN5–IN8 (pads 5–8) on `unconnected-*` with OUT5–OUT8 also unused, while IN4 *is* tied to
GND. On a DMOS array a floating gate input would normally be a defect. The **p.2 equivalent
circuit shows an internal resistor from the input node to GND**, so an open input is
defined-OFF — this is why it is not a finding. Recorded because the reasoning is
datasheet-specific and does not transfer to a substitute part. The IN4-tied /
IN5–IN8-floating inconsistency is cosmetic.

**DRIVER VERDICT: PASS ×2.**

## 3. Decoders, shift register, watchdog, expander, Schmitt buffer

### `U_DECU` / `U_DECD` — SN74HC238DR

**Datasheet read.** `02_parts/SN74HC238DR/SN74HC238_D2804.pdf` (an image-only databook
scan — the text layer is empty, so both figures were rendered at 200 dpi and read as
images). **p.1 "SN74HC238 … D or N PACKAGE (TOP VIEW)"**: A=1, B=2, C=3, /G2A=4, /G2B=5,
G1=6, Y7=7, GND=8, Y6=9, Y5=10, Y4=11, Y3=12, Y2=13, Y1=14, Y0=15, VCC=16. Cross-read
against **p.2 "logic symbols"**, which independently prints the same pin numbers in
parentheses. Pin 1 top-left, 1–8 down the left, 9–16 up the right — footprint matches
exactly, **no mirror**.

Both parts: `/G2A`=`/G2B`=GND (permanently enabled), `G1` = the gated enable, VCC = 3V3,
GND = GND. `U_DECU` Y0–Y5 → `SEL_U1..SEL_U6`, Y6/Y7 unused. `U_DECD` **C tied to GND**, so
only Y0–Y3 are reachable → `SEL_D1..SEL_D4`; Y4–Y7 unused *and unreachable*, which is the
right way to leave them. Pairwise symmetry between the two instances holds on every pad.

**Polarity is the safety-critical property here and it is correct.** The '238 has
**active-HIGH** one-hot outputs, and the TBD62083A turns its channel ON with a HIGH input.
A '138/'139 (active-LOW, both present in `02_parts/` as unused entries) would have energised
**eleven of twelve coils at once**. The board has the '238. **PASS ×2.**

## 4. Single-gate safety logic and the one-shot

Reviewed against **SCES487I Figure 4-1 "DBV or DCK Package, 6-Pin SOT-23 or SOT-SC70
(Top View)"** (`SN74LVC1G11DBVR`, committed), **SCES212AB §6 "DCK PACKAGE (TOP VIEW)"** plus
its Pin Functions table (`SN74LVC1G00DCKR`, **not committed** — fetched, see F-EV1/F1), and
**SCHS166F p.1 Pinout + p.2 Functional Diagram + p.2 Truth Table** (`CD74HC221M96`,
committed).

**Winding, all three packages: rotation only, no mirror, on all 15 instances.**
- 1G11 DBV: 1=A, 2=GND, 3=B, 4=Y, 5=VCC, 6=C — CCW, pads at x=±1.14 / 0.95 mm pitch. ✓
- 1G00 DCK: 1=A, 2=B, 3=GND, 4=Y, 5=VCC — CCW, pads at x=±0.84 / 0.65 mm pitch. The
  reviewer explicitly confirmed the **DCK** figure and noted that the **DPW** variant on the
  same page has A and B swapped (B=1, A=2). The board is DCK and uses the DCK map. ✓
- CD74HC221 SOIC-16: pin 1 top-left, CCW; the p.2 Functional Diagram independently confirms
  the timing-node numbers (1CX=14, 1CXRX=15, 2CX=6, 2CXRX=7). ✓

**Pad-number vs net-kind symmetry — no divergence on any instance.**

`SN74LVC1G11DBVR` ×10 (`U_AND1..3`, `U_CAND1..2`, `U_LATCHG`, `U_FAULTAND`, `U_OSCLR`,
`U_DECUEN`, `U_DECDEN`): pad 1 = input ×10, pad 2 = GND ×10, pad 3 = input ×10,
**pad 4 = output ×10**, pad 5 = 3V3 ×10, pad 6 = input ×10. All four tied C inputs go to
**3V3, never GND** — a GND tie would have dead-shorted the permit logic to 0. No 1G11 input
sits on an `unconnected-*` net.

`SN74LVC1G00DCKR` ×4 (`U_LATCHA`, `U_LATCHB`, `U_OENAND`, `U_STOPINV`): pad 1 = input ×4,
pad 2 = input ×4, pad 3 = GND ×4, **pad 4 = output ×4**, pad 5 = 3V3 ×4. `U_STOPINV`'s
pads 1 and 2 share `STOP_REQ` — that is the intended NAND-as-inverter, not a short.

**Logic polarity re-derived from the netlist, independent of the docs:**
`FAULT_SET_N` = AND(`WD_OK`, `ESTOP_OK`, `TEMP_OK`) → low on any fault → sets the NAND SR
latch; `U_LATCHA`/`U_LATCHB` are properly cross-coupled (Y_A→B_B, Y_B→B_A);
`FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR` gates `U_AND3` and `U_CAND2`. Fail-safe pull directions are right
throughout: `R_FAULTPU` 100k→3V3 (default = faulted), `R_FLCPD`/`R_FSETNPD` 100k→GND
(default = not-permitted), `R_OE` 10k→3V3 (default = 595 outputs disabled).
`U_OENAND` = NAND(`MCU_RELAY_ENABLE`, `WD_OK`); `U_STOPINV` = inverter → `STOP_REQ_N`.
No VCC/GND swap, no output-onto-output, no doubly-driven input anywhere in the group.

**`U_ONESHOT` — both channels used, both correctly formed.** Channel 1 is the PRESS
one-shot: 1/A=GND, 2/1B=`PRESS_REQ` (rising trigger), 3=`OS_CLR_N`, 13/1Q=`PRESS_TIMED`,
4/1Q̄=`PRESS_TIMED_N`, with `C_OS` 1 µF across 14–15 and `R_OS` 510k from 15 to 3V3

## 6. `J_TC` — the connector nobody was assigned

The connector reviewer found a **13th** connector on this board with **no dossier** in
`06_build/pin_audit_v20/`, hence outside every reviewer's assignment. `pin_audit.py` skips
any footprint with `len(numbered) <= 3`, and `J_TC` has two electrical contacts, so it was
excluded by construction. Thermocouple polarity is exactly what this review exists to catch,
so I reviewed it myself rather than leave it unexamined.

**Datasheet read.** `02_parts/PCC-SMP-K/PCC-OST-SMP_spec.pdf` p.2 "PCC-SMP Drawing" at 200
and 600 dpi (the PDF has no text layer). The miniature-connector isometric gives
**ø1.77 mm, 4 PLACES**: two contact holes at **7.9 mm** span and two mounting-bracket holes
at **15.7 mm** span, **6.8 mm** behind the contact row. The footprint
`cooksense:Omega_PCC-SMP-K_TypeK_PCpin` has contacts at ±3.96 (7.92) and NPTH at ±7.85
(15.70), offset 6.80. **Geometry confirmed.**

**Net path re-derived:** `J_TC.1` → `TC_POS_IN` → `R_TCP` → `TC_POS` → `U_TC.4` (**T+**);
`J_TC.2` → `TC_NEG_IN` → `R_TCN` → `TC_NEG` → `U_TC.2` (**BIAS**) + `U_TC.3` (**T−**).
Given `part.yaml`'s pin 1 = chromel (+) / pin 2 = alumel (−), that is the correct Type-K
polarity into the MAX31856, and the BIAS-to-T− tie is the part's standard bias arrangement.
**Verdict: QUESTION**, for the reason below.

**Why it is not a PASS.** The Omega drawing carries a **⊖ polarity mark on the connector
body** but **no numbered pin table** mapping a body end to a PCB hole, and the land is
**geometrically symmetric** — pad 1 and pad 2 are both ø1.77 drills in 2.80 pads at ±3.96,
distinguished only by pad 1's rectangular outline. So "pad 1 = chromel (+)" rests on the
footprint author's reading of the ⊖ position, and no artifact in this tree lets a reviewer
confirm it independently. The part is hand-soldered (`lcsc: ""`, not in the JLC catalog), so
orientation is a human judgement at assembly.

There **is** an orientation cue and it is the right one: the footprint carries an
`F.SilkS` `"+"` at (−6.2, 0), beside pad 1. Two caveats worth recording: it sits **inside the
silk body outline** (±9.5 × ±5.5) and the 20.8 × 15.5 mm housing therefore covers it once
fitted — usable at insertion, unverifiable afterwards, and the canon's own "silk under a body
is silk nobody reads" applies; and a reversed thermocouple is **not** an open or a short, so
the MAX31856's `TC_FAULT_N` flags need not fire — the channel simply reports temperature
falling as the cavity heats. **A human should confirm ⊖-to-pad-1 against a physical part at
first article, and the confirmation should be written down.**

### Connector findings

**C1 — `J_DOOR` pin 4 is the one asymmetry in the GH-5 family, and it is a fail-permissive
injection path into the door interlock. QUESTION, and the most consequential finding in this
review.**

`J_DOOR`: 1=3V3, 2=`DOOR_RAW`, **4=`DOOR_RAW`**, 3/5=GND.
`J_ESTOP`: 1=3V3, 2=`ESTOP_RAW`, **4=GND**, 3/5=GND.

An SHT45 pod harness cross-plugged into `J_DOOR` puts the pod's *pulled-up* SCL wire (module
10 kΩ to its VCC, and VCC is fed from `J_DOOR.1` = real 3V3) onto `DOOR_RAW` against
`R_DOORPD` = 10 kΩ: **3.3 × 10/20 = 1.650 V**.

I closed the one number the connector reviewer could not, using the SN74HC14 datasheet
(SCLS085L §5.5, fetched — it is not committed, see F-EV1). TI specifies thresholds only at
V_CC = 2.0 / 4.5 / 6.0 V, so there is **no guaranteed spec at 3.3 V**; but the conclusion does
not need interpolation. Taking the **4.5 V** row, which is the conservative one for
reading-high: **V_T+ MIN = 1.55 V**. The injected 1.650 V **exceeds V_T+ MIN**, so a
conforming device is *permitted* to have already switched HIGH and to read that node as a
valid logic 1. (Interpolating to 3.3 V gives V_T+ ∈ [1.14, 2.35] and V_T− ∈ [0.61, 1.75] —
1.650 V sits inside both bands, i.e. indeterminate.) Either way: **`DOOR_RAW` can read CLOSED
with no door switch attached**, which is the door interlock's fail-permissive direction.

The same mis-plug on `J_ESTOP` is **safe**, and only because pin 4 there is GND — the pod's
pull-up lands on ground and `ESTOP_RAW` sees no injected source. This is the exact hazard
class ADR-0018 closed on `COIL_EN_IN` with a 680 Ω pull-down at the pin (10 kΩ injection →
0.210 V); **that hardening was never carried to `DOOR_RAW` or `ESTOP_RAW`**, which still hold
10 kΩ. Cheapest candidate fix: make `J_DOOR` pin 4 = GND, matching `J_ESTOP`, which removes
the path entirely; or take `R_DOORPD`/`R_ESTOPPD` to 680 Ω.

**C2 — `J_DOOR` ↔ `J_ESTOP` harness transposition de-latches the E-stop. QUESTION.** Same
C189896 housing, same GHR-05V-S plug, adjacent with a 0.090 mm courtyard gap (ADR-0013's own
measured anchors: `J_ESTOP` 70.88, `J_DOOR` 81.76), and both harnesses are electrically
identical 2-wire dry contacts on pins 1–2 — a swapped pair mates perfectly and looks right.
Traced in the netlist: `ESTOP_OK` feeds `U_AND1.6` (the coil-rail AND chain) **and**
`U_FAULTAND.3` (the latch SET); `DOOR_OK` feeds only `U_OSCLR.1` (the one-shot clear).
Swapped, an E-stop press drops `DOOR_OK` → the PRESS one-shot is held cleared, and E-stop
pole B still physically breaks the isolated loop at `J_ISOLOOP` (a screw terminal, not
swappable), so the appliance contactor still opens — **but `ESTOP_OK` stays high, so no fault
is latched and the coil rail stays up**: release the button and everything resumes without a
deliberate re-arm. Mirror image: opening the door now sets a *latched* fault (nuisance, but
self-revealing at commissioning). Graded QUESTION because the primary stop paths survive —
it is a loss of the latching property, not a defeat.

**C3 — the remaining cross-plug cells in the GH-5 set. QUESTION, collisions listed.** Four
identical C189896 refs = 12 ordered pairs. `J_MODE` is correctly excluded from the family, and
that exclusion was re-derived rather than assumed: GHR-05V-S housing cross-section **4.15 mm**
(eGH p.3) against the S4B-ZR-SM4A-TF outer height **3.70 mm** (eZH p.2, cavity for a 3.40 mm
ZHR-4), pitches 1.25 vs 1.50.
- door/E-stop harness → `J_RH_AMBIENT` or `J_RH_EXHAUST`: the dry contact bridges **pin 1
  (`3V3_SW_RHA`/`3V3_SW_RHE`) to pin 2 (GND)** — a hard short of the switched sensor rail
  through an AO3401A drain every time the door closes. Current-limited by the AMS1117
  (brown-out, loud, recoverable), not destructive. Its GND wire on pins 3/5 also grounds
  `SDA_A`/`SDA_B`.
- pod → `J_ESTOP`: `ESTOP_RAW` becomes the pod's ground return, pulled low by `R_ESTOPPD` →
  E-stop reads TRIPPED (**fail-safe**).
- `J_RH_AMBIENT` ↔ `J_RH_EXHAUST`, and `J_THERM_A` ↔ `J_THERM_B`: **silent transposition.**
  Benign for the over-temperature hard stop — both thermal channels are structurally
  identical with the same threshold and both `TH_CAM_{A,B}` feed both comparator halves, so
  whichever probe is hot still trips it — but every logged reading and camera image is
  attributed to the wrong physical location.
- Partial insertion: a GH shroud does not exclude a *shorter* plug of the same series — a
  GHR-05V-S will enter the 8- and 10-circuit shrouds unlatched at an arbitrary offset. Worst
  realistic landing is a 3V3 wire onto a `TH_*` NTC node (indistinguishable from the
  already-handled NTC-open case) or a 10 kΩ bridge between two `KP_*` keypad lines. Won't
  latch, so an observation rather than a graded cell.

- **`J_KEY_MATRIX`'s netless MP tabs are RIGHT, judged not assumed.** All ten circuits are

  the housing; a GND shell tab would bridge SELV into that domain and destroy the isolation
  this board is built around. Leaving them netless is the correct call.
- Every connector footprint carries F.SilkS geometry (8–14 elements each — stock KiCad
  JST/Molex/PinSocket bodies including their pin-1 ticks), and the board silk names every
  housing plus `5V SELV IN`, `ISO 30V`, `NOT SELV`, `1C2L3L4E`.
- The thermal heads share one GND pin (pin 2) between the camera rail return and three
  ratiometric NTC dividers: common-impedance error ≈ 1 mV ≈ 0.08 °C at the trip. Negligible.
- `02_parts/LTV-817S-TA1/part.yaml` still calls the isolated output connector **"J10"**, a
  name this board no longer uses (it is `J_ISOLOOP`). Cosmetic doc drift.

### Evidence findings

**F-EV1 — 26 of 54 dossiers (48%) point at no committed datasheet PDF. Blocking for a
review of this kind.** This review's entire premise is "read the datasheet yourself from
the tree". For these parts that was impossible from committed evidence alone:

| dossier | MPN | what `part.yaml` offers |
|---|---|---|
| `U_LATCHA`, `U_LATCHB`, `U_OENAND`, `U_STOPINV` | SN74LVC1G00DCKR | URL only, **no sha256**, and the `doc_id` (`SCES214`) contradicts the real document (`SCES212AB`) — the same file's own `layout.source` names SCES212AB correctly |
| `U_SCHM` | SN74HC14DR | URL only |
| `U_SR1` | SN74HC595DR | URL only |
| `U_LDO` | AMS1117-3.3 | URL only |
| `U_TC` | MAX31856MUD+T | URL only |
| `U_OPTO` | LTV-817S-TA1 | URL only |
| `J_PWR` | 43650-0224 | URL only |
| 12 × `K_*`, `J_ISOLOOP`, `J_LOADCELL`, `J_PI`, `U_EXP` | — | **the dossier shows no datasheet at all** (F-EV2) |

Four of these are safety-chain gates whose pinout was, until this review, asserted on "the
1G-family convention" rather than on a hashed local artifact. I closed the pinout question by
fetching each document from the URL `part.yaml` itself cites and reading the correct
package figure — all maps proved correct — but the *provenance* is not what the tree claims.
**Fix: commit the PDFs with sha256, and correct `SN74LVC1G00DCKR.datasheet.doc_id` to
SCES212AB citing the DCK figure.**

**F-EV2 — `pin_audit.py` silently emits content-free dossiers for 16 of 54 parts, including
all twelve relays. This is a GATE defect, not a paperwork one.** `skills/kicad-pcb/scripts/pin_audit.py`
resolves each ref's MPN from the JLC BOM's `MPN` column and then opens
`02_parts/<MPN>/part.yaml`. Two failure modes, both silent:

1. **Empty `MPN` column.** The twelve `K_*` relays and `J_ISOLOOP` are deliberately
   `not_on_assembly_bom` (JLC cannot source them; they are hand-soldered), so the JLC BOM
   carries the MPN in the `Comment` column and leaves `MPN` blank. `pin_audit.py` therefore
   reports `MPN unknown`, `datasheet: (none)`, and `(not in yaml)` for every pin.
2. **MPN string ≠ directory name.** `U_EXP` (`MCP23017-E/SS` vs dir `MCP23017-E-SS` — the
   slash makes it a nested path), `J_PI` (`2.54-2*20PPC104` vs dir `2.54-2x20PPC104`),
   `J_LOADCELL` (`B5B-XH-A(LF)(SN)` vs dir `B5B-XH-A`).

The consequence is the serious part: **the single highest-stakes land pattern on the board —
the relay whose predecessor was drawn against the wrong datasheet sub-figure — is precisely
the one the dossier generator blanks out.** A reviewer working only from the dossiers, as the
protocol says to, would have had no pin map and no datasheet path for it and could not have
performed the check at all. I only caught the relays because I went looking for
`02_parts/DIP05-1A72-13L/` by hand.

**Suggested fix:** fall back to the `Comment` column when `MPN` is empty, sanitize
`/` `*` `(` `)` when forming the directory name, and — most important — **fail loudly**:
have `pin_audit.py` print a summary line naming every ref it could not resolve, and
non-zero-exit or emit a `RESOLUTION FAILED` banner in the dossier body so the blank cannot
be mistaken for "nothing to check".

### QUESTIONs

**Q1 — `U_EXP` interrupt routing: the only alert line to the Pi is on the wrong port's
interrupt pin unless firmware sets `IOCON.MIRROR`.** Datasheet DS20001952C p.1: pin 19 =
INTB (port B's interrupt), pin 20 = INTA (port A's).

- All eight **status inputs** are on **port B** (pads 1–8: `EFUSE_FLT_N`,
  `MODE_AUTO_HW_EXP`, `ESTOP_OK_EXP`, `DOOR_OK_EXP`, `TEMP_OK_EXP`, `FAULT_EXP`,
  `TC_FAULT_N`, `WD_OK_EXP`).
- Port A (pads 21–28) is four `RAIL_EN_*` outputs, `CONTACTOR_REQ`, `REARM_N`, and two
  static `BOARD_ID` straps — nothing whose change is worth an interrupt.
- `INT_ALERT` = `J_PI.13` + `U_EXP.20` — i.e. the Pi watches **INTA**.
- `EXP_INTB` (pin 19) is a **single-node net**: the only named single-node net on the entire
  board (I checked all 198 nets). `01_docs/pin_map.md` line 21 documents phys 13 as
  "expander INTA" and mentions INTB nowhere.

So an interrupt-on-change on any safety status input raises **INTB**, which goes nowhere,
while the Pi is wired to INTA. The MCP23017 *can* fix this in firmware — the p.1 feature list
says "INTA and INTB Can Be Configured to Operate Independently or Together", i.e. the
`IOCON.MIRROR` bit ORs both ports onto both pins — and `part.yaml`'s own gotcha mentions
`IOCON.ODR` but not `MIRROR`. **I cannot resolve this from the inputs I was given: it is
either a correct design with an undocumented and binding firmware requirement, or a dead
alert path.** A human must answer: is `IOCON.MIRROR = 1` a recorded, tested firmware
requirement? If yes, say so in `pin_map.md` and the expander's `part.yaml` — it is load-bearing
for a safety readback and currently written down nowhere. If no, the board is wrong.
(Polling would also mask it, which is exactly why it needs an explicit answer rather than an
assumption.)

**Q2 — the relay `part.yaml` calls this a "mains-switching stage"; on this board the
contacts do not switch mains.** `02_parts/DIP05-1A72-13L/part.yaml` `layout:` is headed
"P-LAYOUT: reed relay (**mains-switching stage**, DO-NOT-SUBSTITUTE)". But
`ARCHITECTURE.md` and the netlist agree the contacts intercept the **OEM membrane keypad
ribbon** — SELV keypad lines. My netlist enumeration above shows the contact domain touches
only `J_KEY_MATRIX`, two resistors and three test points; there is no mains net on this
board at all. This cuts both ways and that is why it is a QUESTION rather than a nit: if a
reader believes those contacts carry mains, the 6.12 mm pad-edge clearance and the 1.5 kV
coil/contact rating become load-bearing in a way the netlist does not require; and if a
future revision ever *does* put mains there, the model-72 contact rating (200 V, 0.5 A
switch) and the board's clearances need re-derivation, not inheritance. **Please correct the
label to say what the contacts actually switch.**

**Q3 — `U_SCHM` pin 12 is named `6A_Y` in the machine-readable pin map; the datasheet names
it `6Y`.** `02_parts/SN74HC14DR/part.yaml` `pins: 12: 6A_Y   # placeholder - see gotcha`,
with a gotcha explaining "pin 12 = 6Y (output), pin 13 = 6A (input) - name key kept
unique". The **net is correct** (`DOOR_OK`, the output), so there is no pad-to-net
contradiction and I did not escalate it to a FAIL. But this is a fabricated pin name in a
machine-graded artifact on the one package that debounces E-STOP, DOOR and MODE, and it is
the kind of thing that lets a later revision treat pin 12 as an input. **Two questions for a
human: what constraint required the name to be "unique", and does any gate consume this
field?** If nothing consumes it, rename it to `6Y` and delete the workaround.

**Q4 — `ARCHITECTURE.md` still names the ULN2803A as the coil driver.** The block diagram
reads "2x SN74HC238 DECODERS (one-hot, active-HIGH!) -> **ULN2803A** -> coils". The board
fits **TBD62083AFWG**, and the part change was the whole point of the coil-margin work
recorded in `DIP05-1A72-13L/part.yaml` (a Darlington's saturation voltage made pull-in go
negative inside the declared envelope). The relay `part.yaml` also still says "the ULN2803
COM clamp on RELAY_5V is the protective circuit". Documentation only — the copper is right,
and I verified the COM clamp exists on the correct rail — but on a safety board a stale
driver name in the top-level architecture invites exactly the substitution the margin
analysis forbids.

**Q5 — `U_ONESHOT` pad 2 (`1B`, the press trigger) can float, breaking the board's own
convention.** `PRESS_REQ` has exactly two nodes: `U_ONESHOT.2` and `U_SR1.7` (595 **QH**, a
three-state output disabled by `OE_N`). No pull resistor. `SR_OE_N` is HIGH — outputs
high-Z — whenever `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` or `WD_OK` is low, which includes the board's own
power-up state (`R_MCUENPD` pulls `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE` low). So a CMOS trigger input floats
for a real part of the operating envelope. The **same 595** has QD→`DECU_G1_RAW` and
QG→`DECD_G1_RAW` each fitted with a 100k pull-down (`R_DECUPD`, `R_DECDPD`); QH has none,
which reads as an omission rather than a decision. Graded QUESTION, not FAIL, because the
actuation path is independently interlocked: both float conditions also pull
`KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED` low, which drops the `5V_KEY_RELAY` coil rail through `Q_COIL`, so a
spurious pulse cannot press anything; and `R_KRSTPD` holds `SRCLR̄` low while the Pi is
absent, so QH = 0 when `OE_N` first drops. Residual real defects: an out-of-spec floating
input (excess I_CC on the HC221) and reliance on an off-board strap for the interlock.
**Recommendation: fit a 100k pull-down on `PRESS_REQ` to match `R_DECUPD`/`R_DECDPD`.**

**Q6 — `U_LATCHG` pad 1 (`A`) has no defined state when the Pi is unpowered.** `KEY_LATCH`
has exactly two nodes, `J_PI.32` and `U_LATCHG.1`, and no pull resistor anywhere in the
netlist. Every other Pi-driven logic input has one — `MCU_RELAY_ENABLE`/`R_MCUENPD`,
`HOST_AUTH`/`R_HOSTAUTHPD`, `STOP_REQ`/`R_STOPPD`, `KEY_RESET_N`/`R_KRSTPD`, all 100k to
GND — so `KEY_LATCH` is the lone exception. Header pin 32 is Pi GPIO12, which has an
internal pull-down, but only while the Pi is powered; board-powered-with-Pi-unpowered leaves
an LVC input floating. Consequence is milder than Q5 (`KEY_LATCH_G` only clocks RCLK, and a
spurious latch is masked by `SR_OE_N`), but the asymmetry looks unintended.

**Q7 — `U_ONESHOT` `part.yaml` describes section 2 as unused; the board uses it.** The
gotcha reads "Unused section 2: 2Ā=VCC, 2B=GND, 2R̄=GND (held reset), 2Q/2Q̄ open, 2CX open".
As built, section 2 is an active ~7 ms rearm one-shot with 2B on **3V3 not GND** and 2R̄ on
**`WD_OK` not GND**. The pin *functions* still match the datasheet so this is not a pinout
error, and the circuit is correct as built (2Ā falling with 2B=H, 2R̄=H → one pulse, p.2
truth-table row 4; and the reset-as-trigger row cannot fire spuriously because 2R̄ = `WD_OK`
also drives `U_EXP` `/RESET`, so while `WD_OK` is low GPA5 is high-Z and `R_REARMPU` holds
2Ā high). **Update the tie-state paragraph and the `pins:` "(unused)" comments.**

**Q8 — `U_TC` / MAX31856MUD+T: the datasheet is not committed and the pin-configuration
figure could not be read. This is the one gate this review was supposed to close and could
not.** `02_parts/MAX31856MUD+T/` contains only `part.yaml`; the dossier's `datasheet:` field
is a URL. `part.yaml` states in its own words that the pin map came from **EasyEDA CAD
C2653162** and is *"PENDING datasheet-figure confirmation in pin review"* — this review is
that gate. Every fetch route from this host failed: analog.com and maximintegrated.com return
blocked/HTML bodies, LCSC's C2653162 entry serves a `default_*.pdf` placeholder, and the
Mouser/DigiKey/Arrow/Farnell/RS/Adafruit mirrors returned JS, HTML, 410 or XML.

What can be said: two independent secondary sources (a vendor-independent pin table and a
search-index extract of the ADI PDF) both give 1 AGND, 2 BIAS, 3 T−, 4 T+, 5 AVDD, 6 DNC,
7 DRDY, 8 DVDD, 9 CS, 10 SCK, 11 SDO, 12 SDI, 13 FAULT, 14 DGND — identical to `part.yaml`
and to the board. That is **corroboration, not the primary figure**, so per protocol it is a
QUESTION and not a PASS. Unverified along with it: **pin 2 (BIAS) tied directly to pin 3
(T−)** on `TC_NEG`, which is believed to be the datasheet's typical-circuit connection but
was not confirmed against the figure. **Recommendation: commit the ADI PDF beside the part
and re-run this single part from a host with network egress before sealing.** A thermocouple
front end on a cooking-appliance over-temperature path should not ship on a CAD-library pin
map.

**Q9 — `U_COMP` vs `U_COMP2` diverge structurally on pins 2/3 and 5/6; deliberate and
correct.** `U_COMP` has IN− = `TCAM_THRESH`, IN+ = `TH_CAM_x`; `U_COMP2` has IN− =
`TH_CAM_x`, IN+ = `TCAM_OPEN`. Same *kinds* of net with the roles swapped, so the two ICs
form a **window** comparator: `U_COMP` pulls `TEMP_OK` low below 0.4231 V (over-temperature),
`U_COMP2` pulls it low above 2.0370 V (NTC open / unplugged). All four outputs are open-drain
onto one `TEMP_OK` wired-AND with a single 10k pull-up to `3V3_ANALOG`, which is correct for
the LMV393's open-drain output (Table 4-1, TYPE = O). Fail-safe direction verified in both
halves. Raised because the protocol requires flagging structural divergence between identical
parts; resolved as intentional.

**Q10 — `01_docs/DETAIL_DESIGN.md` §1 contradicts the shipped analog front end.** It names
the comparator an **LM393** (the board fits LMV393, whose common-mode ceiling is the
load-bearing constraint) and it says *"NTC OPEN → node 3.3 V → comparator sees 'cold' (NOT
safe at the comparator) — detected in software … accepted for v1.2"*. Shipped v1.7 detects
open **in hardware** via `U_COMP2`. Neither `TCAM_OPEN`, `R_OPENT`/`R_OPENB`, `R_CLMPA`/`B`
nor `U_COMP2` appears anywhere in `DETAIL_DESIGN.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `pin_map.md` or
`01_docs/decisions/` — the only derivation lives in a comment block in
`03_tscircuit/src/cooksense.tsx` (~lines 620–700). Per the S5 spot-check habit those numbers
were re-derived from the committed part values and **all reproduce exactly**: open-circuit
node 3.3·22/32 = **2.2687 V**; threshold 3.3·100/162 = **2.0370 V**; over-temperature trip
with 68k/10k and the 22k bleed = **72.80 °C**; cold-end nuisance-trip floor = **−10.4 °C**.
The ceiling claim also checks against the datasheet: SLCS136V §7.2.2.1 p.11 gives
V_ICR = 0 to VCC−0.7 over temperature → 2.6 V at 3V3 (2.5 V on the 5-V table's VCC−0.8), so
the worst-high open reading 2.2829 V sits inside range with ~217 mV margin. **The design is
sound; the document that is supposed to hold the math is stale.** On a safety board the
derivation for a hardware over-temperature trip should not live only in a source comment.

**N1 — `02_parts/LTV-817S-TA1/part.yaml` `layout.keep_short` names net `OPTO_LED`; the board

**N2 — `AMS1117-3.3`'s `verified:` note is inherited, not re-read.** It reads "carried from

**N3 — the dossier `side` column is unreliable and could mislead a reviewer.** In

instead. Worth fixing in `pin_audit.py`, because a reviewer trusting that column could be led
astray on exactly the check this protocol exists for.

## Why the verdict is FAIL, stated plainly

**The thing this review was most afraid of did not happen.** The relay land is right. I
derived it from the datasheet's own sub-figure 13 at 600 dpi, transformed the figure's lead
coordinates into the footprint frame, and got a **pure rotation with no reflection**, every
residual inside the pixel error. Coil on one row, contact on the other, 7.62 mm apart, coil
and contact domains provably disjoint in the netlist, all twelve instances identical, the
pin-1 silk dot on the correct corner. The driver channel mapping is 11-for-11 correct. The
decoders are the active-HIGH '238 and not the '138 that would have fired eleven coils at
once. **No mirrored footprint anywhere on the board, and no pad-to-net contradiction against
any datasheet that could be read.**

The bar for PASS is zero FAILs, and the bar is not met:

1. **One FAIL (F1)** — the pinout of four safety-chain gates rests on a `part.yaml` whose
   `doc_id` contradicts the real document, with no committed, hashed PDF. The map is correct;
   the evidence is not what the tree claims.
2. **Three pin maps could not be verified against a primary figure at all** — `U_TC`
   (MAX31856, whose own `part.yaml` says "PENDING datasheet-figure confirmation in pin
   review" — this *was* that gate), `J_PWR` (the power inlet's pin-1-versus-key, never
   confirmed by any artifact in this tree), and `J_ISOLOOP` (a NOT-SELV interlock block whose
   land was derived from the project's own 2P footprint, checker and checked sharing a method
   — canon M1).
3. **48% of the dossiers point at no committed datasheet**, and **`pin_audit.py` silently
   emits content-free dossiers for 16 of 54 parts including all twelve relays** (F-EV2). The
   protocol's own input generator fails on exactly the parts that most need review, and fails
   quietly. That is a gate defect, and a gate that cannot fail is worthless.
4. **One fail-permissive path into the door interlock (C1)** — 1.650 V injected on
   `DOOR_RAW`, above the SN74HC14's V_T+ MIN, so a conforming part may read the door CLOSED
   with nothing attached. ADR-0018 already fixed this exact class on `COIL_EN_IN`; the fix was
   not carried across.

None of items 2–4 is a copper error I can prove. All four are reasons this review **cannot
certify** the board, which is a different statement from "the board is wrong" — and the
honest one. A partial result honestly reported is worth more than a passing claim.

VERDICT: FAIL
