## 1. The four v1.7 changes, graded

### 1.1 Relay part change -12L → -13L — **CLAIM HOLDS, verified from the figure**

I did not take the part.yaml's word for the pin-out. `datasheet-reed-relay-series-dip.pdf`
page 3 was rendered at 400 dpi (`pdftoppm -r 400 -f 3 -l 3`) and sub-figure 13 read
directly. It shows **four leads and nothing else**:

* a switch (Form A) drawn between the pin **14** and pin **8** terminals — both on the
  pins-8..14 side of the DIP;
* a coil drawn between the pin **2** and pin **6** terminals — both on the pins-1..7 side.

So coil and contact are on OPPOSITE SIDES of the DIP body, and the separation between
them is the DIP row spacing. That is exactly what ADR-0002, the v1.1 isolation comb and
every downstream pour-relief rule assume. The claim under test is TRUE, and it is true
for the reason the part.yaml gives.

The footprint implements it correctly. `03_src/lib/cooksense.pretty/Relay_StandexDIP_1A_pinout13.kicad_mod`,
four pads, against the DIP-14 corner grid (2.54 pitch, 15.24 span, 7.62 row spacing):

| pad | role | footprint (x, y) mm | required DIP lead position | match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (rect, pin-1 marker) | COIL_A | (−3.810, −5.080) | DIP 2 = (−3.81, −5.08) | ✓ |
| 2 | COIL_B | (−3.810, +5.080) | DIP 6 = (−3.81, +5.08) | ✓ |
| 3 | CONTACT_B | (+3.810, +7.620) | DIP 8 = (+3.81, +7.62) | ✓ |
| 4 | CONTACT_A | (+3.810, −7.620) | DIP 14 = (+3.81, −7.62) | ✓ |

Column separation 7.620 mm. Nearest coil-pad-to-contact-pad centre distance is
hypot(7.62, 2.54) = **8.032 mm** (pad1↔pad4 and pad2↔pad3), i.e. the 7.62 mm the design
assumes is a conservative lower bound, not the actual worst pair. Measured pad-edge
separation on that worst pair: **6.349 mm**.

The netlist agrees: every K_* device carries pins 1/2 = COIL (5V_KEY_RELAY or 5V_STOP,
and COIL_*_N to a ULN output or Q_STOPDRV) and pins 3/4 = CONTACT (KP_* / *_SEL_BUS /
RKEY_MID / RSTOP_MID). No coil net appears on a contact pad on any of the twelve.

### 1.2 Five 10k series resistors on U_EXP GPB1-GPB5 — **CORRECT, and the arithmetic holds**

Netlist confirms each `*_EXP` net contains exactly two nodes — the resistor and the
expander pin — and that every gate consumer stayed on the RAW net:

```
MODE_AUTO_HW  -> R_MODEHWPD.1, R_MODEHWSER.1, U_AND1.3(A), U_SCHM.8
MODE_AUTO_HW_EXP -> R_MODEHWSER.2, U_EXP.2
TEMP_OK  -> R_HYS1/2, R_TEMPOK, R_TEMPOKSER.1, TP_TEMPOK, U_AND2.1, U_CAND1.6,
            U_COMP.1, U_COMP.7, U_COMP2.1, U_COMP2.7, U_FAULTAND.6
TEMP_OK_EXP -> R_TEMPOKSER.2, U_EXP.5
```
(same shape for ESTOP_OK / DOOR_OK / FAULT / WD_OK).

Forcing direction: an MCP23017 output driving 3.3 V through 10 kΩ delivers 0.33 mA into
an SN74HC14 push-pull output whose sink impedance the tsx bounds at ≤82.5 Ω → the RAW
node moves ≤27 mV. Reading direction: MCP23017 input leakage ±1 µA through 10 kΩ = ±10 mV
of error. Both fine. A bonus the block does not claim: it also defangs the `GPPUB=0xFF`
hazard the ADR-0019 comment worries about, because the internal 100k pull-up now works
through 110 kΩ into a push-pull output.

Same check for R_WDOKSER: WD_OK's consumers (U_AND1.3, U_CAND1.1, U_FAULTAND.1,
U_OENAND.2, U_ONESHOT.11, U_EXP.18, R_WDOKPD, TP_WDOK, U_WD.1) all sit on the raw net;
only U_EXP.8 is behind the resistor. As authored.

### 1.3 The 10k/22k divider on U_EXP GPA0 — **P1-1, the ratio is graded against the wrong network**

**The finding.** The divider was sized as a two-resistor divider off a stiff 5 V source.
It is not. `06_build/netlists/cooksense.net`:

```
EFUSE_FLT_N   -> R_FLTDIVT.1, R_PG.1, TP_PGOOD.1, U_EFUSE.6
EFUSE_FLT_DIV -> R_FLTDIVB.1, R_FLTDIVT.2, U_EXP.1
```

`U_EFUSE.6` is FLT_N, an OPEN-DRAIN output (SLVSE57C p.4 Pin Functions; the part.yaml
gotcha says so too). In the no-fault state it is OFF. The only source for the node is
**R_PG = 100 kΩ** to 5V_PROTECTED — confirmed on the shipped BOM
(`fab/bom_jlc.csv`, R_PG is inside the `0402WGF1003TCE / C25741` 100 kΩ group).

So the divider is 100 k + 10 k + 22 k, and

```
V(GPA0) = V_5VP × 22 / (100 + 10 + 22) = V_5VP × 22/132 = V_5VP / 6
```

not `V_5VP × 22/32`. Measured against the sanctioned rail envelope
(power_tree.yaml `linear_rails: 5V_PROTECTED` vout 4.754 … 5.25 V):

| rail | GPA0, as built | GPA0, as the tsx comment claims |
|---|---|---|
| 4.754 V (vout_min) | **0.7923 V** | 3.2684 V |
| 5.000 V (nominal) | **0.8333 V** | 3.4375 V |
| 5.250 V (vout_max) | **0.8750 V** | 3.6094 V |

Worst-high with all three legs at ±1 % in the direction that raises the tap
(99 k / 9.9 k / 22.22 k, rail 5.25): **0.8897 V**.

Against the committed MCP23017 datasheet, `DS20001952C.pdf` DC Characteristics
(extracted directly, `pdftotext -layout`):

* D031 — CS, GPIO, SCL/SCK: **V_IL max = 0.2 V_DD** = 0.660 V at V_DD 3.3 V
* D041 — CS, GPIO, SCL/SCK: **V_IH min = 0.8 V_DD** = 2.640 V

The power-good state lands at 0.79–0.89 V: **133–229 mV above V_IL(max) and 1.75–1.85 V
below V_IH(min)** — inside the undefined band, and never a guaranteed HIGH. The fault
state (FLT_N pulling the node down) is 0 V. On a typical Schmitt input (V_T+ ≈ 0.5 V_DD)
both states read LOW. The readback GPA0 exists to provide is **degenerate**: the host's
documented convention "HIGH = power good" will report a permanent power fault.

**Second-order, and it falsifies the second E-INV `why` in the same block.**
electrical_invariants.yaml:840 keeps TP_PGOOD on the raw node so "the instrument must
see the real node". As built the raw node is no longer the real node: with 32 kΩ hung on
a 100 kΩ pull-up it now rests at 5.00 × 32/132 = **1.2121 V** instead of 5.0 V. Any
bring-up table that expects ~5 V at TP_PGOOD for a healthy flag is wrong.

**Proportionality.** The defect being fixed was 5.0 V on a 3.6 V-abs-max pin through a
100 kΩ source = **14 µA** of clamp injection, which the tsx itself quotes. That was
worth fixing, but the fix cost the signal.

**Quantified remedy** (offered so the fix is not another guess). With R_PG fixed at
100 kΩ, the tap must satisfy both V ≥ 2.640 V (guaranteed HIGH) and V ≤ 3.600 V
(V_DD+0.3 abs max). Solving V = 5.0·R_b/(100 k + R_t + R_b):

* there is **no solution with R_t > 0 of any usable size** — even R_t = 0 needs
  R_b ≥ 111.9 kΩ to clear V_IH, and R_b ≤ 257 kΩ to stay under abs max;
* so: **delete R_FLTDIVT, set R_FLTDIVB ≈ 150 kΩ** → tap = 5.0 × 150/250 = **3.000 V**,
  which is 360 mV under abs max and 360 mV over V_IH(min), and 0 V on fault;
* or, cleaner: **move R_PG's top end from 5V_PROTECTED to 3V3** and delete the divider
  entirely. FLT_N is open-drain; nothing requires it to be pulled to 5 V. TP_PGOOD then
  reads a genuine 3.3/0 flag and the E-INV `why` at line 840 becomes true again.

Grade **P1**, not P0: EFUSE_FLT_N has no hardware consumer (the tsx and E-INV both say
so and the netlist confirms it — nothing but R_PG, the test point and the divider), and
the failure direction at the host is restrictive ("power is bad"), not permissive.

## 2. Hypotheses I formed and then refuted with my own measurement

**R-1. "The isolation boundary is asserted, not measured."** Refuted. I measured it on
the placed board with pcbnew, comparing every isolated-domain item (U_SEL_BUS,
D_SEL_BUS, RKEY_MID, RSTOP_MID, KP_U1-6, KP_D1-4 — 448 tracks/pads/zone shapes) against
every non-isolated copper item (5995) on shared layers, by binary-search on
`SHAPE::Collide`:

* **minimum same-layer isolated↔SELV copper clearance = 6.220 mm**
  (D_SEL_BUS pad ↔ COIL_D1_N track, F.Cu);
* next: 6.220 (KP_D4↔COIL_D4_N), 6.230, 6.270, 6.280, 6.300, 6.310, 6.349 …;
* **minimum isolated-pad to filled-copper-zone distance = 6.630 mm** (3V3 pour), with
  **zero** pairs below 6.0 mm.

The ≥6 mm requirement holds as built, on the actual copper, not in the rule file.

**R-2. "The /SR NAND latch's forbidden state is a permissive window."** Real mechanism,
no hazard. With FAULT_SET_N low (a live fault) and REARM_PULSE_N low (the 7 ms re-arm
pulse), both latch outputs go HIGH, so FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR = 1 = permissive at U_AND3.6
and U_CAND2.3 for up to 7 ms. **Refuted** because FAULT_SET_N = WD_OK·ESTOP_OK·TEMP_OK,
and those same three nets feed U_AND1 (pin3 WD_OK, pin6 ESTOP_OK), U_AND2 (pin1 TEMP_OK)
and U_CAND1 (all three → CTR_SAFE) DIRECTLY. Whichever term drove the latch to SET is
also holding the coil rail and the contactor off by itself. The forbidden state cannot
arm anything.

**R-3. "J_LOADCELL puts 5 V logic on Pi GPIO20/21."** LC_DAT/LC_CLK reach J_PI.38/.40
through only 33 Ω and a PESD5V0S1BA, which does not clamp at 3.3 V — so a 5 V-powered
HX711 would inject ~42 mA into the Pi's clamp. **Refuted**: the mating board's
`archived_projects/cook-loadcell/01_docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:15` reads
"U1 HX711: DVDD=3V3", and BRIEF.md:34 "DVDD = 3V3 from the hub (RP2350-safe logic
levels)". The 5 V on J_LOADCELL.1 feeds only the S8550 excitation regulator (E+ ≈ 4.3 V).
Logic is 3.3 V. No injection.

**R-4. "The coil pull-in shortfall (P1-2 below) kills the STOP relay too."**
**Refuted, and this is what keeps P1-2 out of P0.** K_STOP's coil is on 5V_STOP
(vout_min 4.754 V, a 0R link off 5V_PROTECTED) and is driven by **Q_STOPDRV, a 2N7002
MOSFET**, not by the ULN2803 Darlington. At ~8 mA its drop is ≈0.1 V, so the K_STOP coil
sees ≈4.65 V against a 70 °C pull-in requirement of 4.20 V — **+450 mV**. The one relay
that must answer a fault is the one relay that keeps its margin. This is a direct
consequence of the ADR-0011 §4 decision to give K_STOP its own rail and its own driver.

**R-5. "The OVLO setpoint arithmetic is a story."** Recomputed from scratch against
SLVSE57C's V_OVLO(R) = 1.13 / 1.20 / 1.27 V:

* nominal 1.20 × (100 + 26.1)/26.1 = **5.7977 V** (declared 5.798 ✓);
* earliest, all terms adverse (±0.5 % + ±100 ppm/°C over ±45 K = ±0.95 %):
  1.13 × 125.398/26.348 = **5.378 V** (declared 5.3682 with the I_EN term; both clear the
  5.250 V supply ceiling);
* latest: 1.27 × 126.802/25.852 = **6.229 V** (declared 6.2394 with I_EN).

Both bounds reproduce. Against SMBJ5.0A V_BR(min) 6.40 V at 25 °C and the tree's own
−20 °C figure 6.2819 V, and against the DIP05 coil max 7.5 V (DS p.2 Coil Data, "Coil
Voltage Max." column — I read it), the setpoint clears everything. The v1.2–v1.6
100 k/15 k value (9.200 V nominal) really was above both. Fixed.

## 3. P1-2 — reed-relay coil pull-in margin is unbounded in the tree and fails its own envelope

**This is not a v1.7 regression. It is a v1.0-era property that the v1.7 rebuild did not
touch and that nothing in the tree computes.**

The requirement, read directly off the committed relay datasheet, page 2 "Coil Data
(at 20 °C)", row 1A / switch model 72 / Pin Out 10,11,12,13:

* Coil Voltage nominal **5** VDC, max **7.5** VDC
* Coil Resistance typical **500 Ω ± 10 %**
* **Pull-In Voltage max 3.5 VDC**
* Drop-Out Voltage min 0.75 VDC

and immediately under the table, the footnote that nothing in this tree carries:

> "The Pull-In, Drop-Out Voltage and Coil Resistance will change at rate of **0.4 % per °K**"

so the required coil voltage is `V_PI(T) = 3.5 × (1 + 0.004·(T − 20))`:

| T | required V_PI |
|---|---|
| 20 °C | 3.500 V |
| 50 °C — BRIEF.md:117's own enclosure warn level | **3.920 V** |
| 70 °C — the relay's own max operating temperature (DS p.3) | **4.200 V** |

The supply. `03_src/cooksense/rules/power_tree.yaml`, `linear_rails: 5V_KEY_RELAY`:
`vout_min: 4.740` (5V_PROTECTED 4.754 minus Q_COIL's 11 mV), `vout_max: 5.25`.

The driver drop is the term that decides it, and **it does not exist anywhere in the
tree**. `02_parts/ULN2803ADWR/part.yaml` records `limits: {vout: "50V", iout: "500mA/ch",
vin: "30V"}` — no saturation voltage — and no datasheet PDF is committed for it (the
cook-hub copy of the same dossier has none either). SLRS049's lowest specified V_CE(sat)
point is I_C = 100 mA; this board runs each coil at ~8–10 mA, where the datasheet
publishes nothing. A Darlington's floor is V_BE(out) + V_CE(sat)(driver) ≈ 0.65–0.70 V at
25 °C, falling ~2 mV/K to ≈0.56–0.61 V at 70 °C.

Available coil voltage = 4.740 − V_CE(sat):

| corner | available | required | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 °C, vout_min, V_CE(sat) 0.60 | 4.140 V | 3.920 V | **+220 mV** |
| 50 °C, vout_min, V_CE(sat) 0.90 (SLRS049's 100 mA max, the only CITED bound) | 3.840 V | 3.920 V | **−80 mV** |
| 70 °C, vout_min, V_CE(sat) 0.60 | 4.140 V | 4.200 V | **−60 mV** |
| 70 °C, vout_min, V_CE(sat) 0.90 | 3.840 V | 4.200 V | **−360 mV** |
| 70 °C, 5.00 V nominal rail, V_CE(sat) 0.60 | 4.400 V | 4.200 V | +200 mV |

**At the top of the board's own declared thermal envelope combined with the bottom of
its own declared supply envelope, the twelve ULN-driven coils are at or below their
maximum pull-in voltage.** The board has no guaranteed-actuation corner across the
envelope ADR-0021 just made a commission fact.

Two further consequences of the same shortfall, both from DS p.1:

* "Contact Resistance (max.) 10 mOhm … **Measured with 40 % Pull-In Overdrive**"
* "Operating Time (max.) 0.5 ms Including Bouncing … **Measured with 40 % Pull-In Overdrive**"

40 % overdrive is 1.4 × V_PI = **4.90 V at 20 °C** and **5.88 V at 70 °C**. Neither is
reachable on this board at any corner, so the 10 mΩ contact resistance and the 0.5 ms
operate time are both outside their specified conditions. The DETAIL_DESIGN §3 STOP
sequence's "wait ≥1 ms relay release" is separately worth re-checking, because DS p.1
qualifies release time as "**Measured without Coil Suppression**" and every U/D/PRESS
coil is clamped by the ULN2803's internal COM diode, which lengthens release.

**Direction of failure, and why this is P1 and not P0.** A coil that does not pull in is
a key that is not pressed — for K_U1-6, K_D1-4 and K_PRESS that is the benign direction
(no cook, no key). K_STOP is unaffected (see R-4: MOSFET driver, +450 mV). There is no
permissive failure anywhere in this mechanism.

**What closes it** (none of these is a respin, if the measurement lands the right way):

1. Bench-measure ULN2803A V_CE(sat) at I_C = 10 mA and 5V_KEY_RELAY at the coil under
   load, at 25 / 50 / 70 °C, on the first assembled board — the G4/G5 bench-bring-up
   gates already block appliance connection, so the exposure is contained.
2. Add the cited V_CE(sat) to `02_parts/ULN2803ADWR/part.yaml limits:`, and an E-INV
   assertion carrying the DS p.2 0.4 %/K derating against `5V_KEY_RELAY.vout_min` —
   this is the only number on the board that decides whether the actuators actuate,
   and it is currently asserted nowhere.
3. If the measurement is adverse, the cheap fixes are (a) narrow the sanctioned supply
   floor above 4.85 V, or (b) declare a board operating-temperature ceiling below 70 °C
   and put it in the ORDER_README. Replacing the Darlington array with MOSFET drivers is
   the expensive one and should not be reached for before (1).

## 4. P2 findings

**P2-1 — eFuse current limit is below the rail budget it protects.** R_ILM = 1.2 kΩ
(tsx, code-pinned C138040). SLVSE57C §8.3.3.2 Equation 4 gives R_ILM = 2000 / I_LIMIT^0.95
— exponent verified against the datasheet's own anchor (§Features: "R_ILM ≥ 487 Ω
(4.42 A maximum)": 2000/4.42^0.95 = 487.4 Ω ✓). At R_ILM = 1200 Ω:

```
I_LIMIT = (2000/1200)^(1/0.95) = 1.712 A
```

power_tree.yaml declares `iout_max_A: 2.0` for 5V_PROTECTED and the brief commissions a
"5V SELV ≥2A" supply. The eFuse regulates at 1.712 A, so the declared budget can never be
delivered. No functional impact — power_tree's own worst-case simultaneous draw is
0.50 A, so the limit sits 3.4× above the real load — but the declared budget is
unreachable and R_ILM's setpoint is derived nowhere in the tree. Either derive it or
drop `iout_max_A` to ≤1.7 A.

**P2-2 — C_DVDT 1 nF does not control inrush.** SLVSE57C §8.3.3.1 Equation 3:
C_dVdt(pF) = 42000 / SR_ON(V/ms), so 1 nF requests **42 V/ms**. But the Switching
Characteristics table gives SRON at VIN 5 V with **CdVdt = Open** as **21.9 V/ms** (and
11.6 V/ms at 3300 pF, which Equation 3 reproduces: 42000/3300 = 12.7). A requested rate
faster than the open-pin default cannot be produced — the internal capacitance dominates
and the part slews at ≈21.9 V/ms regardless. C_DVDT is inert.

What actually bounds inrush is the current limit. C_OUT on 5V_PROTECTED ≈ CE1 220 µF +
C_IN1 10 + C_LDOIN 10 + C_KR 10 + C_STOPR 10 + ≈0.3 of ceramics ≈ **260 µF**. Equation 2
(I_INRUSH = C_OUT × SR_ON) demands 260 × 21.9 = **5.69 A**, well above I_LIMIT 1.712 A, so
the eFuse enters regulation during every start-up: output ramps at 1.712 A / 260 µF =
6.6 V/ms, reaching 5 V in **≈0.76 ms** with ≈4.3 W peak in the FET (≈3.3 mJ) — inside
the datasheet's thermal-shutdown envelope (Figure 59 gives >10 ms at 6.229 W), and the
polyfuse's 2 A hold is untouched. **It works, but it works by the current limit, not by
the component fitted to do the job.** Note also that dVdt cannot be made to do the job
here: 0.5 A of inrush needs SR_ON = 1.92 V/ms → C_dVdt ≈ 22 nF, against the part.yaml's
own `cdvdt: <=3300pF` ceiling. Record the mechanism honestly rather than leaving 1 nF
looking like a designed value.

**P2-3 — the −20 °C TVS bound is 42 mV wide.** Latest guaranteed OVLO trip 6.2394 V
against SMBJ5.0A V_BR(min) at −20 °C 6.2819 V = **+42.5 mV, 0.68 %**. I reproduced both
numbers (§R-5) and they hold, so this is not a blocker — and the consequence of losing
it is bounded and benign anyway: the tree's own two-point Littelfuse interpolation puts
TVS conduction at that corner at ≈9.3 mA / 58 mW, which is leakage, not a failure. But
0.68 % across two independent parts' worst-case corners is a bound, not a margin, and
the file should not read as though it were one.

**P2-4 — DOOR_OK is not in the coil rail or the contactor gate.** Netlist:
DOOR_OK reaches only U_OSCLR.1 (→ OS_CLR_N, the CD74HC221 clear), R_DOOROKPD, U_SCHM.12
and R_DOOROKSER. It is in neither U_AND1/2/3 (KEY_RELAY_ALLOWED) nor
U_CAND1/2 (CONTACTOR_DRV = CONTACTOR_REQ · WD_OK · ESTOP_OK · TEMP_OK · FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR)
nor U_FAULTAND (the latch SET). **Opening the door clears a press in flight but does not
drop the external cooking contactor and does not latch a fault.** This matches
ARCHITECTURE.md's stated contactor gate and is therefore a declared design decision, and
the OEM appliance's own door interlock is untouched and primary (BRIEF §3 line 33). It is
also already recorded in the tsx that the door is unsupervised (a short across J_DOOR.1-2
reads "closed" undetectably). Recorded here so the door's actual authority is not
overstated in the release notes; not graded higher.

**P2-5 — pad-map documentation drift (no electrical effect).**
`02_parts/DIP05-1A72-13L/part.yaml` `pins:` lists `2: COIL_A, 6: COIL_B, 8: CONTACT_B,
14: CONTACT_A` — the physical DIP lead numbers — while the shipped footprint's pads are
numbered **1, 2, 3, 4** and the netlist binds K_* pins 1..4. The footprint `descr`
explains the renumbering, so nothing is wrong on the board; but a `pins:` block that
names pads the footprint does not have is what a pad-map checker compares against.
Separately, `03_tscircuit/src/cooksense.tsx:20` still reads
"Remap at board: tsx1->1(COIL_A/+), tsx2->7(COIL_B/-)" — pads **1 and 7**, which are the
code-12 lead numbers and exist on neither the footprint (1/2/3/4) nor sub-figure 13
(2/6/8/14), in the same comment that correctly states "pads = 2,6,8,14 (code 13)". Stale
comment, correct netlist.

## 5. Other things checked that came out clean

* **Default / failure direction of every field connector.** J_MODE unplugged: MODE_RAW
  ↓ (R_MODEPD 10k) → MODE_AUTO_HW low → AND-chain false; COIL_EN_IN ↓ (R_COILENPD 680R)
  → coil rail dead. J_ESTOP unplugged: ESTOP_RAW ↓ → ESTOP_OK low → chain false AND
  FAULT_SET_N low → latch SET. J_DOOR unplugged: DOOR_RAW ↓ → DOOR_OK low → OS_CLR_N low
  → press cleared. J_THERM_A/B unplugged: TH_CAM floats to 3.3 × 22/32 = 2.269 V through
  R_CLMPA/B, above the 2.037 V open-detect threshold → U_COMP2 low → TEMP_OK low →
  restrictive. All restrictive.
* **Power-up state.** WD_OK low during the TPS3823 reset delay → FAULT_SET_N low → latch
  SET → FAULT_LATCH_CLEAR low → U_AND3 false → coil rail cannot come up without an
  explicit re-arm. U_ONESHOT section 2 held reset by 2R_N = WD_OK forbids a spurious
  power-up re-arm pulse. Confirmed on the netlist, not just the comment.
* **Reverse-polarity path.** Q_REV is a P-FET with G=GND, S=N5V_RPP (load side),
  D=N5V_FUSED (input side): forward, V_GS ≈ −4.8 V turns it on; reversed, the body diode
  (anode at drain) is reverse-biased and V_GS = 0 keeps it off. D_REVCLAMP (SS34, K on
  N5V_FUSED) conducts on reverse and its fault current passes THROUGH F1, so the polyfuse
  trips — correct, and correctly downstream of F1.
* **Q_COIL gate.** V_GS = −5 V against AO3401A abs max ±12 V; R_HSG 100 k → 50 µA hold.
* **ULN2803A unused inputs.** U_ULNB IN4 is tied to GND and IN5-8 are open. The ULN's
  internal base-emitter network defines an open input as OFF, so this is safe; the
  corresponding OUT4-OUT8 are unconnected.
* **Fab package.** The twelve DIP05 relays appear on `bom_jlc.csv` with a blank LCSC and
  are **absent from `cpl_jlc.csv`** — the declared not_assembled / self-supplied pattern,
  same as J_TC and J_ISOLOOP. The v1.0/v1.1 A-POP defect (relays as CPL placement rows
  with a blank LCSC) is not present. Orderability of the package is another lens's call;
  from this lens nothing is contradictory.
* **Board gate.** 0 DRC violations / 0 unconnected / 0 schematic-parity issues, measured.

## 6. What would flip this verdict

* If the P1-2 bench measurement (§3 item 1) shows V_CE(sat) at 10 mA above ≈0.55 V AND
  the board is to be qualified to 70 °C, the U/D/PRESS relays have no guaranteed pull-in
  and that becomes a **P0 respin** of the coil-driver stage.
* If host software or the ORDER_README bring-up gates auto-start on EFUSE_FLT_N reading
  HIGH, P1-1 makes the board **never start**, and it becomes a P0 for function rather
  than for safety.

Neither is true of the hardware as staged, so: **ORDER-OK-WITH-NOTES**.
