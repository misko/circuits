# Independent USB 2.0 RF schematic review

## Evidence

The exact schematic named in the header was exported independently to a
netlist and traced against the USB2517I, FSUSB42MUX, USBLC6-2SC6,
STM32G0B1CBT6, TE 292304-1, and GCT USB1130-15-A source evidence.

- Topology: J2 D-/D+ reach U5 pins 3/1 and leave U5 pins 4/6 for U6 pins
  58/59. Each external U6 downstream pair reaches the common D-/D+ pins of
  one FSUSB42, leaves its selected HSD1 pair, passes through one USBLC6 array,
  and reaches the like-polarity J3-J6 contacts. U6 port 5 pins 11/12 reach U7
  USB DM/DP pins 33/34 directly. There are no polarity swaps, branch stubs, or
  discrete terminations in the schematic; both PHYs provide their USB
  terminations internally.
- ESD: U5 and U17-U20 use the USBLC6 rail-to-rail pass-through pinout exactly:
  IO1 is the P path, IO2 is the N path, pin 2 is ground, and pin 5 is the local
  USB VBUS. The supplied ST PDF specifies USB 2.0 high-speed applicability,
  3.5 pF maximum line capacitance, and IEC 61000-4-2 level 4 device protection
  (15 kV air / 8 kV contact). The connector-facing arrays are on the intended
  connector side of the data switches. Physical loop length and system clamp
  voltage remain PCB-phase evidence, not a schematic claim.
- VBUS and power-off: upstream J2 VBUS reaches only U5's VBUS pin and the
  100 kOhm / 100 kOhm divider to U6 VBUS_DET; it does not power the 3.3 V rail.
  Each downstream ESD VBUS pin is on its corresponding post-eFuse port rail.
  With logic power absent, the FSUSB42 datasheet specifies power-off I/O
  leakage of at most +/-2 uA for 0-4.3 V switch-pin voltage.
- Disconnect: all four FSUSB42 SEL pins are tied low, so the HSD1 throw is the
  only selected data path and HSD2 pins are true no-connects. OE is active
  high for disconnect and each OE has a 10 kOhm pullup to 3.3 V, giving a
  hardware-disabled reset state. The exact onsemi PDF specifies OE-high
  disconnect, break-before-make behavior, 25 ns maximum turn-off at 3.0-3.6 V,
  and USB 2.0 480 Mbit/s operation.
- Risk tier: `ordinary-high-speed` is justified for the four external 480
  Mbit/s paths. Treating this as only a 240 MHz digital clock would understate
  sensitivity to launch discontinuities, return paths, and switch-node noise.

## Blocking finding

The schematic is plausible for USB 2.0, but the RF contract does not contain
the performance evidence its declared 1 MHz-2.4 GHz band requires. Its three
claims cover differential impedance, return continuity, and uncoupled launch
length only. There is no numeric insertion-loss, jitter, or disconnect-
isolation acceptance over temperature and the declared band, and no numeric
schematic-level ESD/protection budget.

This omission is material because the exact FSUSB42 evidence is much narrower
than the declared band: -3 dB bandwidth is 720 MHz typical with zero added load
and 550 MHz typical with 5 pF; off isolation is only characterized as -30 dB
at 240 MHz; on-capacitance is 3.7 pF typical; and total jitter is 200 ps typical
at 480 Mbit/s. None is a guaranteed corner specification through 2.4 GHz. The
contract therefore has no reproducible acceptance test for whether four series
switches preserve the USB eye or sufficiently isolate a disabled port.

The management-port declaration also overstates the termination: the exact
STM32G0B1 datasheet provides a USB full-speed 12 Mbit/s PHY only. The direct
port-5 circuit is valid and will negotiate full speed with the USB2517I, but it
is not a high-speed PHY endpoint and should not inherit an unexplained 2.4 GHz
performance band.

Disposition: revise `rf.yaml` to describe the management link as full-speed;
derive a defensible electrical band for each port class; add numeric insertion-
loss/jitter and OE-high isolation acceptance with temperature/capacitive-load
corners; and cite evidence that reaches those bounds. If the selected switch
cannot provide guaranteed evidence, replace it or make first-article USB eye
and isolation measurements explicit prototype-only acceptance rather than a
pre-production performance claim. Re-review the exact regenerated schematic.
